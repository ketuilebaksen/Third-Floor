Someone who’s never carried the expectations of a franchise that built its entire identity on winning. It lives somewhere deep in your chest. And it only comes out on days when everything clicks. When the team you bleed green and gold for walks onto that field and reminds the entire football world exactly who they are.

Let’s go back to the beginning. For two hundred seventy-four days, this fan base carried the weight of last season. The wildcard loss to Chicago. The eighteen-point lead that slipped away. The cold handshake afterward. And then a whole offseason of hearing it. Every criticism. Every hot take. Every analyst sitting behind a desk telling the world that Green Bay’s offense had lost its identity. That the Bears were the new power in the NFC North. That the balance in this rivalry had shifted.

We heard all of it. We absorbed every taunt, swallowed every doubt, and kept showing up. Because that’s what Packers fans do. We don’t walk away when things get complicated. We lean in. We wait. And at Lambeau Field, the waiting finally paid off in the most emphatic way possible.

Thirty-one to seven. Not a close win that needed a late comeback. Not a squeaker that kept you nervous until the final whistle. A full, dominant, sixty-minute beatdown that sent a message to every team in the NFC. The Green Bay Packers are back, and they mean business.

Now, to really appreciate what happened, you have to understand what Chicago walked into this game representing. Because this wasn’t just a decent defense. The Bears had been the number one defense in the entire National Football League since week two. Nobody had been able to move the ball on them consistently. Offenses were walking in with game plans and walking out with nothing. People were talking about this Chicago defense in elite terms. Historically dominant terms. The kind of defense that makes offensive coordinators lose sleep on Saturday nights.

And Matt LaFleur walked into that film room, looked at all that tape, thought about that handshake after last year’s wildcard loss, remembered every bit of disrespect that came out of Chicago during the offseason, and he built a plan. A plan designed to tear that defense apart piece by piece.

The result? Four hundred twenty-eight total yards. Seven point three yards per play. Against the number one defense in football.

Let that sink in for a second. Seven point three yards every single time the Packers snapped the ball. That’s not moving the chains. That’s ripping big chunks out of a defense over and over again until there’s nothing left.

And the man leading that charge was Jordan Love.

Packers Nation, I’ve seen good Jordan Love performances. I’ve seen him flash the kind of talent that made this franchise confident enough to move on from a future Hall of Famer. But what he put on display against the Bears was something different. It felt more mature. More intentional. More precise than anything we’ve seen from him at this stage of a season.

Twenty-five of thirty-one. Over eighty percent completions. Three hundred sixty-seven passing yards. Two touchdowns. Zero interceptions. A passer rating of one thirty-seven point five. And ten point eight yards per attempt.

I’m going to be honest with you. I didn’t expect numbers like that. Even with all my belief in what this offense could become, those numbers against that defense went beyond anything I imagined going in.

But here’s the part that really matters. It wasn’t just the numbers. It was how he got there. That’s why I’m saying Jordan Love is different now.

LaFleur and the coaching staff made a conscious decision from the very first snap. They were going to attack vertically. They weren’t going to dink and dunk their way down the field and hope the Bears made a mistake. They were going to test the depth of the field. Challenge cornerbacks in single coverage. And trust their receivers to win contested battles downfield.

And Jordan bought in completely. He wasn’t hesitating at the line. He wasn’t checking down the second pressure showed up. He was reading the field, spotting his opportunities, and throwing with conviction on deep routes that had zero margin for error. That’s the difference. The old version of Jordan might have been a little tentative. A little cautious. This version doesn’t blink. This version hunts.

National analysts noticed it too. They said it seemed like Jordan was hunting the deep ball against Chicago. They started asking out loud if he’s now a top three deep ball thrower in the entire NFL. Think about how far we’ve come for that question to even be asked.

And he spread it around. Christian Watson. Matthew Golden. Tucker Kraft. J. Michael Sturdivant. Jordan was finding playmakers in every part of the field. Short, intermediate, deep. Left side, right side, middle. The Bears had no idea where the ball was going next.

But let’s talk about the two guys who made this game unforgettable. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden.

Watson finished with six catches, one hundred forty-three yards, and two touchdowns, including a spectacular fifty-three yard catch. More than twenty-three yards every time he caught the football. Six-foot-four, about two hundred fifteen pounds, and a forty time under four point three. When he’s healthy and rolling, there just aren’t many defensive backs in this league who can handle that combination of size and speed. And against the Bears, he was rolling.

And Matthew Golden. Six catches for a career-high one hundred twenty-three yards, about twenty yards per catch. After a quiet rookie season and questions about where he fit on the depth chart, Golden is becoming the clear number two receiver in Green Bay. And some of his catches were tough. Contested. Fighting through physical corners. Adjusting his body in the air as defenders closed in. Coming down with the football anyway.

Here’s what people need to understand about these two. They aren’t just speed guys running straight down the field to stretch the defense. They’re route runners. They’re contested-catch winners. They went up against physical cornerbacks and press coverage and came down with the ball. The full route tree, executed with precision, against the best defense in the league. That receiving performance is going to be remembered by this fan base for a long, long time.

But as impressive as the offense was, and it was genuinely impressive, what the defense did on the other side of the ball might have been even more remarkable.

You need to know what Chicago was trying to do. The week before they came to Lambeau, the Bears completely steamrolled the Jets on the ground. They came into this game leading the entire NFL in rushing yards and rushing touchdowns. Their plan was obvious to anyone who watched film. Pound the football. Control the clock. Keep Jordan Love on the sideline. And grind out a win with that dominant defense, the same way they’d been winning games all year.

And the Packers defense looked at that plan and said, absolutely not.

D’Andre Swift and the Chicago running backs combined for fourteen carries and forty-nine yards. Forty-nine yards. The best rushing team in football came to Lambeau and got completely shut down. The Packers didn’t just stop the run. They suffocated it.

Devonte Wyatt. Xavier McKinney. Evan Williams. Keisean Nixon. Zaire Franklin. The guys up front and the guys in the secondary were locked in from the opening kickoff. They won individual battles at the line of scrimmage. They filled gaps. They forced cutbacks into traffic. They gave the running backs nowhere to go. Chicago came in thinking they were going to impose their will on the ground, and they left Lambeau with almost nothing to show for it.

And once the run game was dead, the chaos really started.

Five turnovers. Three interceptions and two fumbles. Xavier McKinney picked off two passes by himself. Evan Williams was everywhere with twelve tackles. Tyson Bagent was a mess the moment he realized the run game wasn’t going to save him. And when Case Keenum came in to try to steady things, it didn’t matter. The pass rush and the secondary were operating on a completely different level. There was no escape route. No safe checkdown. The Packers forced the issue on every possession and made Chicago pay for every single mistake.

And meanwhile, the Packers offense gave the ball away zero times. Five to zero in the turnover battle. You do that in the NFL, and you’re going to win almost every single time.

Now, I know some people want to point at Chicago’s backup quarterback situation and use it to discount this performance. Let me shut that argument down right now. Chicago had been winning games all season with this same offensive system and this same approach. The offense had kept humming no matter who was under center. And this Packers defense took it apart anyway. That’s not an asterisk. That’s dominance.

And then there was the moment that slammed the door. Early in the fourth quarter, MarShawn Lloyd bounced a run outside, found open space, and took it into the end zone to make it twenty-nine to seven. Three-score game. Lights out. And the best part was what it represented. Last season, this team too often had leads and let opponents creep back in. Not this time. The Packers kept applying pressure, kept extending the lead, and finished the job. That’s growth.

Even the handshake told the story. Matt LaFleur and Ben Johnson, two coaches who really don’t like each other. Last year, it was a cold handshake after a painful playoff loss. This time, LaFleur walked over with a twenty-four point win in his pocket. You could see which side of that handshake felt better.

Now, was it a perfect day? Not quite. The one blemish was the kicking game. Rookie Trey Smack had a rough afternoon, missing two field goals and an extra point. That’s something that has to get cleaned up, no question about it. But when you’re winning by twenty-four, when your offense is putting up four hundred twenty-eight yards, when your defense is forcing five turnovers, a rough day from the kicker is a footnote. Not a headline. You fix it during the week and you move on.

What doesn’t get lost in the footnotes is what this win actually meant. It moved Green Bay to three and two. And the record might have looked modest, but the identity this team showed was anything but modest. This was a team that played with purpose. With aggression. With tactical sophistication. And with the kind of confidence that you simply cannot fake.

LaFleur had a plan. His players executed it. The offensive line gave Jordan time, and he used every second of it. The receivers won their matchups when it mattered most. The defense controlled the line of scrimmage, created turnovers, and dictated the pace of the game from start to finish. That’s what a complete team looks like.

And that’s why I keep saying Jordan Love is different now. It’s not just one big stat line. It’s the way he plays. The decisiveness. The trust in his receivers. The willingness to let it rip against the best defense in the league. Analysts have since pointed out that he’s been getting the ball out faster than any quarterback in football, and that he’s been leading the league in big-time throws over a stretch of games. That’s not a quarterback who’s hoping things work out. That’s a quarterback who’s in command.

And think about where this offense can still go. Josh Jacobs is back. Tucker Kraft is finding his rhythm again after his injury. Savion Williams is emerging as a reliable third option. And on defense, Micah Parsons still hasn’t even made his real impact yet. When he comes back for the stretch run, this defense, which already shut down the best rushing team in football, gets another superstar.

So yes, this team has had its ups and downs. There have been tough losses. There will be more tough games in a brutal NFC North. But the Bears game showed us the ceiling. It showed us what the Green Bay Packers look like when everything clicks. And that ceiling is high. Really high.

For two hundred seventy-four days, we waited. And on that Sunday at Lambeau, the Packers answered every single doubt. They didn’t just beat the Bears. They beat the number one defense in the league. They shut down the number one rushing attack. And they did it with a quarterback who looks more dangerous than ever.
