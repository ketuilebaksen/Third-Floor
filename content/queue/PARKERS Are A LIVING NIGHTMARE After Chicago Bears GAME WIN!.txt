Nightmare number one. If you’re a defense, the Green Bay Packers are your worst dream come to life.

It starts with Jordan Love. Three hundred sixty-seven passing yards. Two touchdowns. Zero turnovers. Read that line again. Three sixty-seven, two, and zero. Against a Bears defense that came in with a reputation for blitzing, for getting after the quarterback, for being aggressive up the middle. A defense that a lot of smart people thought would rattle Jordan and throw off the timing of this offense. Instead, Jordan Love stood in there and carved them up like a Thanksgiving turkey.

And it wasn’t dink and dunk. It wasn’t checkdowns and screens. This was a quarterback going for the throat. Every time he dropped back, you could feel it. He wasn’t just taking deep shots. He was hunting them. The coaching staff came into this game with a clear plan: attack the Bears with splash plays, because they believed their wide receivers were better than the guys covering them. And for four quarters, that belief turned into reality.

Now meet the two men who turned Monday morning film sessions around the league into horror movies.

Christian Watson. Six catches. One hundred forty-three yards. Two touchdowns. More than twenty-three yards every single time he touched the football. When Christian Watson is healthy and rolling, he is one of the most dangerous deep threats in this entire sport. He’s got that speed that makes safeties panic. That speed where a cornerback looks fine at the snap, and two seconds later he’s three steps behind and praying. And against the Bears, Watson was that guy all day long.

And then Matthew Golden. Six catches. One hundred twenty-three yards. Twenty-plus yards per catch. Packers Nation, this young man is growing up right in front of us. Some of his catches came with defenders draped all over him. There were moments where he had to adjust his body in the air, fight through contact, almost play defense himself to win the football, and he came down with it. That’s not just talent. That’s a mentality. That’s a kid who wants the ball when it matters.

So now put yourself back in that defensive coordinator’s chair. You’ve got Watson on one side and Golden on the other. Both of them just went over a hundred twenty yards. Both of them averaged twenty-plus a catch. You roll your coverage toward Watson? Golden eats you alive. You double Golden? Watson’s gone, and the only thing your safety sees is the back of his jersey. There’s no good answer. There’s only the least bad one. And that’s what makes this offense a nightmare.

And here’s the part that should really scare people. This offense isn’t even fully healthy yet. Tucker Kraft is still working his way back from a major injury. LaFleur told us himself that it takes time, that guys coming back from big injuries need reps before they look like themselves again. Savion Williams just came off IR and is ramping up. So what happens when Tucker Kraft is back to being Tucker Kraft, a physical, tough, short-yardage monster? What happens when Savion is fully acclimated and LaFleur starts moving him all over the formation? You’ve got Watson and Golden stretching the field, Kraft working the middle, Savion as a wild card, and Jordan Love throwing darts. Good luck. Seriously. Good luck.

There’s a question people around the league are now asking out loud: is Jordan Love a top three deep ball thrower in the NFL? And honestly, after this game, it’s getting hard to argue against it. When he drifts in the pocket and lets it fly, you know someone in green and gold is going to be under it. And the best part is the trust. Jordan trusts his receivers to make the play. His receivers trust Jordan to put it where only they can get it. That two-way trust is the secret ingredient of every great passing offense in history. And right now, Green Bay has it.

Nightmare number two. If you’re a quarterback lining up against this Packers defense, you’d better protect the football. Because they will take it from you.

Five takeaways. Let me say it louder for the people in Chicago. Five takeaways. And the Packers offense? Zero turnovers. Five to zero. In the NFL, a turnover margin like that doesn’t just win games. It breaks teams. It breaks spirits. It sends a message.

The Bears had to use two quarterbacks in this game. Caleb Williams was inactive, even though he was spotted warming up at Lambeau. So Chicago rolled out Case Keenum and Tyson Bagent. And the Packers defense made both of them pay. Three interceptions through the air between those two quarterbacks. Throws that should never have been thrown. Decisions that made everybody watching wince. And the Green Bay defense was sitting right there, waiting, like they knew it was coming.

Because they did know it was coming. We talked about it before the game. Tyson Bagent nearly threw four interceptions against the Jets the week before, with three of them wiped out by penalties. This defense studied that. They prepared for it. And when the mistakes came, they didn’t drop them. They didn’t miss them. They took the football and they took the game.

And don’t forget who they did it without. Edgerrin Cooper, one of the most important players on this defense, is out with a torn Achilles. That’s a massive loss. Speed, instincts, physicality, gone. A lot of teams would have used that as an excuse. Not this one. LaFleur said it before the game: it’s the ultimate team sport, and it takes all eleven doing their job. And that’s exactly what we saw. Guys stepping up, filling roles, flying to the football. Nobody tried to be Edgerrin Cooper. Everybody just did their job, and the result was five takeaways.

And what about the Chicago run game? Coming into this one, the Bears led the entire league in rushing yards per game. That was the thing I was most nervous about. D’Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai, the two-headed monster, the engine of the whole offense. So what happened? Swift averaged over four yards a carry, but he only got nine attempts. Nine! Why? Because the Packers built a lead so fast that Chicago had to abandon the plan. When you’re down big, you can’t grind out the clock. You have to throw. And when the Bears had to throw, the Packers took the ball away.

That’s what great teams do. They don’t just stop your strength. They make your strength irrelevant. They force you to play their game. And once you’re playing their game, you’re done.

Nightmare number three. If you’re in the NFC North, the Green Bay Packers just reminded you who runs this division.

Let’s talk about the rivalry for a second, because this matters. The Packers had lost two straight to the Bears. Two straight. The Bears had been talking. Their fans had been talking. Ben Johnson came over from Detroit, a guy who spent years trying to beat Green Bay, and he was supposed to be the man who flipped this rivalry for good. The betting line said Chicago. The experts said Chicago. Everyone was ready to crown the Bears.

And then the handshake happened after the game. LaFleur and Johnson, two coaches who really don’t like each other. And this time, it was LaFleur with the thirty-one to seven win in his pocket. You could only imagine how that handshake felt on both sides. One of them was smiling a lot more than the other.

Streak over. Rivalry restored. Order back in its proper place.

And look at the bigger picture now. The Bears drop to three and two, and they’ve already lost two division games, one to the Vikings and one to the Packers. They’re heading to Atlanta to face a hot Falcons team and Bijan Robinson, and they don’t even know who their quarterback is going to be. Do they go back to Keenum? Stick with Bagent? Hope Caleb is ready? That’s chaos. Even the analysts said Ben Johnson looked hesitant as a play caller, like he didn’t trust his own offense enough to open things up. That’s a team with real problems.

The Vikings started hot, but their offense has big questions. The Lions are sitting at two and two, searching for answers. And the Packers? Three and two, healthy weapons coming back, a quarterback playing his best football, and a defense that just took the ball away five times. You tell me. Who in this division looked better than Green Bay yesterday? Nobody. Not even close.

And here’s the thing about division wins. They count double. You beat a division rival, you gain on them and you knock them down at the same time. That’s why this game felt so huge. The Packers didn’t just climb back into the race. They climbed over the Bears to get there.

And now, nightmare number four. The scariest one. A nightmare in January.

And I want to give some love to MarShawn Lloyd, too, because his moment might have been the most symbolic of the whole game. Early in the fourth quarter, with the Packers up big, he bounced a run outside, found daylight, and took it to the house to make it twenty-nine to seven. Three-score game. Door slammed shut. And think about the storyline there. All week, everyone was talking about how the Packers couldn’t run the football. Dead last in the league. And who delivers the knockout punch? A running back, on a run, breaking it to the outside and outrunning everybody to the end zone. You couldn’t write it better.

And here’s what I loved most about that moment. It was about finishing. Last season, this team too often had leads and let opponents creep back in. Jordan talked about it. Holding the lead in the second half. Finishing. That was the lesson of the offseason. And this time, they didn’t let Chicago hang around. They didn’t let it get uncomfortable. They stepped on the gas and they finished the job. That’s growth. That’s a team learning from its pain.
