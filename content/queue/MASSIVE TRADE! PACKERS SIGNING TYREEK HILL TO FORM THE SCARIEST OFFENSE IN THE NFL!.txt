Let’s start with the running back trade ideas, because there’s been a lot of buzz about this. A writer at A to Z Sports, Wendell Ferrer, used an NFL trade simulator to explore five potential running back trades for Green Bay. Now, let’s make this crystal clear before we go any further. These are hypothetical proposals. They are not confirmed negotiations. There’s no sign the Packers have offered any of these packages or even contacted the teams involved. But they’re interesting, and they tell us a lot about where this team is.

Here’s the important context. When that article came out on October sixteenth, Josh Jacobs was still on the commissioner’s exempt list. But according to a later report from PackersWire on October twentieth, Jacobs has returned. And we heard Matt LaFleur say this week that Jacobs got a real practice in and that he believes Jacobs is on track to play. So the urgency to go get another running back right now? It’s not nearly as high as it was a couple of weeks ago.

But Green Bay still has to think about the future. MarShawn Lloyd, Caleb Johnson, Chris Brooks, and Pierre Strong have all had moments, but none of them has established himself as a clear long-term answer behind or after Jacobs. And here’s the other piece of the puzzle. The Packers don’t have their 2027 first-round pick, because it went to Dallas in the Micah Parsons trade. That means every draft pick Green Bay still has matters a lot. You can’t just throw picks around.

So let’s go through the options.

The first one is the big name. Jonathan Taylor from the Indianapolis Colts. In the simulator, the Packers would send their 2027 second-round pick to Indy and get back Taylor plus a 2028 sixth-round pick. And look, Taylor is twenty-seven years old and one of the most talented running backs in the league. Through five games this season, he had three hundred ninety-eight rushing yards, six rushing touchdowns, and thirteen catches for one hundred six yards. That’s production. But there are some red flags. His three point nine yards per carry is below his normal standard, and his seventy-nine point six rushing yards per game is his lowest average since 2023.

And the bigger issue is the money. Taylor signed a two-year, forty-four million dollar extension with the Colts in August. Green Bay would owe about three and a half million for the rest of this season, then sixteen million in 2027, and nearly eighteen million in 2028. Nothing beyond this season is fully guaranteed right now, though his 2027 salary becomes fully guaranteed in February. And with the heavy workload he’s carried since his days at Wisconsin, durability is a real question.

So would I do it? Honestly, no. Not with Jacobs back. Taylor is a great player, but giving up a second-round pick, when you already don’t have your first, and taking on that contract? Green Bay has bigger priorities. And I’ll get to the biggest one in a minute.

Option number two is J.K. Dobbins from the Denver Broncos. And this one is almost too good to be true. In the simulator, the Packers would send a 2027 third-round pick to Denver and get back Dobbins, a 2028 third-round pick, and a 2028 fifth-round pick. So Green Bay gets a veteran back and two future picks for one third-rounder. That’s a fantastic deal for the Packers. I just can’t see Denver ever agreeing to it.

Dobbins ran for seven hundred seventy-two yards and four touchdowns last season at five yards per carry, before another season-ending injury cut his year short after ten games. Through five games this season, he has two hundred twenty-nine rushing yards, one touchdown, and three point six yards per carry. The injury history is the big worry. Financially, Green Bay would take on about four million for the rest of this season and eight million in 2027, with no guaranteed money attached to that future number. I like the flexibility. But don’t hold your breath on this exact deal.

Option number three is Javonte Williams from the Dallas Cowboys. The simulator has Green Bay sending a 2027 fourth-round pick to Dallas for Williams and a 2028 fifth-rounder. Williams has been productive since leaving Denver. Last season he ran for over a thousand yards and eleven touchdowns at four point eight yards per carry. Through five games this season, he had two hundred seventy-six rushing yards and six touchdowns, although his average dipped to three point seven.

His contract is a lot easier to swallow than Taylor’s. He signed a three-year, twenty-four million dollar extension with Dallas. Green Bay would owe roughly the veteran minimum for the rest of this season, then seven point two five million in 2027 and eight million in 2028. That’s a reasonable price for a productive back. The question is why Dallas would ever do it. Unless the Cowboys are struggling badly enough to start selling, there’s not much reason for them to move him. But if Williams were really available at that price? I’d want the Packers to at least pick up the phone.

And there’s a fourth option in that list, Chase Brown from the Cincinnati Bengals, another interesting young back whose name has come up. Same story. Hypothetical, interesting, but not something to count on.

So here’s where I land on all these running back ideas. They’re fun to talk about. Some of them would be great value. But with Josh Jacobs back, and with no first-round pick in 2027, I don’t think a running back is the move that changes this season. I think the move that changes this season is a lot faster. And a lot scarier.

Let’s talk about Tyreek Hill.

Packers Nation, I want you to close your eyes for a second and picture this. It’s January. It’s freezing at Lambeau. Snow is coming down sideways. And Jordan Love takes the snap, looks left, pump fakes, and launches a deep ball down the right sideline. And streaking underneath it, leaving a cornerback and a safety in the dust, is number ten. Tyreek Hill. Touchdown, Green Bay. Eighty thousand people lose their minds.

That’s the dream. And I’m telling you, it’s not as crazy as it sounds.

Let’s go through where Tyreek Hill is right now. He’s a free agent. The Dolphins released him back in February, after he suffered a brutal knee injury in week four of the 2025 season, a dislocated knee and a torn ACL. He’s spent months rehabbing. He’s posted workout videos of himself cutting and sprinting on the beach, and the knee looks good. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said earlier this month that he expected Hill could sign with a team soon. And here’s the key detail. Rosenhaus said Tyreek wants to play only for a contender.

A contender. Well, who just went into Lambeau and destroyed the Bears thirty-one to seven? Who has a quarterback playing at an elite level? Who has a defense that forced five turnovers in one game? The Green Bay Packers look like a contender to me.

Now, let me be clear, because I always keep it honest with you. As of the latest reports I’ve seen, Tyreek Hill has not signed with the Packers or with anybody else. He’s been linked to teams like the Chiefs, the Giants, and the Ravens. Green Bay’s name has been pushed out there by writers who believe the Packers should go get him. But nothing is done. So what we’re talking about is a possibility. But it’s a possibility that could completely change the season.

And here’s why I believe Tyreek Hill could be the guy who makes the Green Bay Packers champions.

Reason number one. Jordan Love’s deep ball.

Against the Bears, Jordan Love went twenty-five of thirty-one for three hundred sixty-seven yards and two touchdowns. A passer rating of one thirty-seven point five. Ten point eight yards per attempt. National analysts started asking if he’s a top three deep ball thrower in the NFL. And when you have a quarterback who throws the deep ball like that, what do you want most? The fastest receiver alive. Tyreek Hill is the perfect match for Jordan Love’s arm. Love throws it fifty yards downfield, and Hill runs under it like it’s nothing. That’s a combination that can win you games all by itself.

Reason number two. The receiver room becomes unguardable.

Right now, Christian Watson is putting up monster numbers. He’s on pace for around sixteen hundred yards. He’s six-foot-four with sub four point three speed. Matthew Golden has become a legit number two receiver, with a career-high one hundred twenty-three yards against the Bears. Now add Tyreek Hill. Watson outside. Golden on the other side. Hill in the slot or flying across the formation in motion. Who does the defense double? You can’t double all three. You can’t roll coverage to everyone. Somebody is getting one-on-one every single snap. And with Jordan Love throwing, one-on-one means points.

Reason number three. Josh Jacobs.

Here’s what people aren’t talking about enough. With Jacobs back, this offense finally gets a real run game. And when you combine a physical downhill runner like Jacobs with a deep threat like Tyreek Hill, defenses are stuck in an impossible spot. If they bring a safety down to stop Jacobs, Hill burns them over the top. If they keep two safeties deep to stop Hill, Jacobs runs right through a light box all day. Pick your poison. That’s how championship offenses are built. Balance. Speed. Power. All at once.

Reason number four. Matt LaFleur’s offense.

LaFleur loves motion. He loves misdirection. He loves putting defenders in conflict. Give him Tyreek Hill and the playbook explodes. Jet sweeps with Hill flying across the formation. Quick screens where Hill catches it behind the line and is gone before anybody can touch him. Play-action shots with Hill on a deep post while Watson clears out the sideline. Hill as a decoy, pulling two defenders with him, while Golden runs free underneath. The creativity would be unlimited. I genuinely think LaFleur would be like a kid on Christmas morning.

Reason number five. Experience in the biggest moments.

Tyreek Hill has been there. He’s won a Super Bowl. He’s played in AFC Championship games. He knows what January football feels like. He knows what it takes. This Packers team is young in a lot of spots. Golden is young. A lot of the defense is young. Having a veteran star who’s been on the biggest stage and delivered could be invaluable in that locker room when the pressure gets real in the playoffs.

And reason number six. The cost.

This is the part that really makes it interesting. Remember, the Packers don’t have a 2027 first-rounder. They have to protect their draft picks. A trade for Jonathan Taylor would cost a second-rounder. Saquon Barkley would cost picks and a big contract. But Tyreek Hill is a free agent. Signing him wouldn’t cost a single draft pick. Not one. It’s just about the contract. And coming off a major injury at thirty-two, he’s not going to command the kind of money he made in his prime. If the price is right and the medicals check out, this could be the best value move on the entire market.

Now, I’m not going to pretend there are no risks. The knee is the big one. Coming back from a dislocated knee and a torn ACL is serious, and nobody knows for sure if he’ll have the same explosiveness. He’s thirty-two, and speed receivers don’t always age gracefully. He’s had off-field issues throughout his career, and Green Bay has always been careful about who it brings into that locker room. And historically, the Packers are not a franchise that makes splashy moves like this.

But then again, they made the Micah Parsons trade, which nobody saw coming. That told me this front office is willing to be aggressive when it believes there’s a real window. And Packers Nation, if there was ever a window, it’s right now. Jordan Love is playing like a superstar. Watson is healthy and explosive. Golden is rising. Jacobs is back. The defense is taking the ball away. This is the moment.

So let me paint the picture of what this season could look like. Tyreek Hill signs. He takes a few weeks to ramp up, gets his legs under him, gets on the same page with Jordan Love. By December, he’s fully up to speed. The Packers start rolling. The offense is putting up thirty points a week. Defenses have no idea what to do. Green Bay locks up the NFC North. Home playoff games at Lambeau in January. The cold. The snow. The noise. And a Packers team with the fastest receiver in the game, a red-hot quarterback, a bruising running back, and a ball-hawking defense.

You tell me who beats that team. Seriously. Who in the NFC wants to come to Lambeau in January and try to cover Tyreek Hill, Christian Watson, and Matthew Golden at the same time while Josh Jacobs runs them over?

That’s how you win a championship. That’s how you bring the Lombardi Trophy back to Titletown.

And look, I know I’m getting fired up. I know it’s not done. But this is Third Floor, and we believe in this team. And when I look at this roster, I see a team that’s one big move away from being the favorite in the NFC. And I can’t think of a bigger, more exciting move than Tyreek Hill.

So here’s my message to Brian Gutekunst and the front office. Make the call. If the knee checks out, if the price makes sense, go get him. Don’t spend a second-round pick on a running back when Jacobs is back. Don’t overpay in a trade. Go sign the most dangerous deep threat of this generation and put him next to Jordan Love. Let’s see what happens.

The trade deadline is November tenth, and Hill is still out there looking for a contender. The next couple of weeks could decide the whole season.
