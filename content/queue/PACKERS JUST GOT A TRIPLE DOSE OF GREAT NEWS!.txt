Packers Nation, it’s been a long time since this fan base got this much good news in one week. And today, I’ve got three reasons to be fired up about this team.

Number one. Micah Parsons is back on the practice field.

Number two. The trade market is setting up perfectly for Green Bay, with four players who could actually help, some of them for a surprisingly cheap price.

And number three. The path to the playoffs is wide open. Win the next three, and the Packers’ playoff chances reportedly jump to ninety percent.

Welcome back to Third Floor. The NFL trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth. That’s just days away. And with Green Bay sitting at five and three, I fully expect this front office to be buyers. So today we’re going through all three pieces of good news, and we’re asking one simple question about every trade target: would he actually help this team turn a good start into something bigger? Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the biggest one. Good news number one. Micah Parsons.

After nearly eleven months recovering from a torn ACL and additional meniscus damage, Parsons practiced this week for the first time since his injury last December. The Packers opened his twenty-one-day practice window, which runs through November twenty-fifth. And that happens to be the same day Green Bay plays the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve. That could be a possible target for his return, but nothing has been confirmed.

Parsons was honest about where he’s at. He said his conditioning is probably the worst it’s been in his NFL career. He doesn’t expect to play his usual workload right away. But he said his knee feels great, he wasn’t nervous about getting back out there, and whenever he’s on the field, he’s going to give everything he has.

And think about what this means. Green Bay is five and three without him. Last season, after he got hurt, the Packers lost their final five games. This year, the defense has kept winning without him. Now that defense is about to get one of the best pass rushers in football back for the stretch run. Even a limited Micah Parsons changes how offenses game-plan. And he ended his press conference with three words. Let the show begin.

That alone would make it a great week. But there’s more.

Good news number two. The trade market.

There are four players who’ve been rumored to be available, spread across the defensive line, the offensive line, and the backfield. And what’s exciting is that most of them are on teams that are clearly sellers. Sellers usually accept less as the deadline gets closer. So let’s go through all four.

The first name is back like it never left. Josh Sweat.

The trade rumors around Sweat were hot in March, in June, in August. And now they’re back again in November. CBS Sports published a recent piece about him, and the big reason they see Green Bay as a landing spot is Jonathan Gannon.

Here’s the connection. Gannon coached Sweat when he was the defensive coordinator in Philadelphia. And when Gannon became the head coach in Arizona, Sweat followed him there as a free agent. That relationship could give the Packers a real edge if they want to reunite them. And it would add to what’s already a growing strength, with Micah Parsons on the way back.

The article also pointed out that Chicago just made a blockbuster move for Jeffery Simmons, and said Green Bay could look to join that arms race in the NFC North.

Now, I’ll be honest. Edge rusher isn’t nearly the need we once thought it might be. Lukas Van Ness has answered the call. The whole group has been a pleasant surprise. And the number one priority for Brian Gutekunst should be fixing the run game. But if the price is right on Sweat, and there are plenty of rumors that Arizona would trade him, then I’m listening. I’m never going to say no to adding extra talent on a defensive line.

And here’s why. Remember when the Eagles beat the Chiefs in the Super Bowl a couple of years ago, with Sweat on that roster? Patrick Mahomes spent the whole night seeing ghosts. Six sacks. Eleven quarterback hits. Seventeen pressures. A forty point five percent pressure rate. And with a Vic Fangio defense, zero blitzes. Zero. They got all of that with just four guys up front, because they had absolute dudes on that defensive line.

And look at the last ten Super Bowl champions and where they ranked in total sacks. Almost all of them were in the top ten or right around it. Several were top five. There was one rare outlier at thirtieth. But the pattern is clear. Good defensive lines that get after the quarterback usually belong to teams that win in the playoffs.

So imagine this. Micah Parsons coming off one edge. Lukas Van Ness, having a career year, on the other. Josh Sweat rotating in fresh. Devonte Wyatt and Karl Brooks in the middle. Jonathan Gannon calling the shots. That’s a pass rush that could make any quarterback in the NFC see ghosts.

The one obstacle? Jerry Jones. Things aren’t going well in Dallas, and the Cowboys just suffered an ugly loss to the Cardinals. In situations like that, you rarely see a coaching change. What you usually see is a splashy trade to distract the fan base. We’ve seen it before. And Sweat is reportedly a player Jones likes. So Green Bay might have to beat Dallas to get him.

But before we go further, let me explain why the run game keeps coming up, even when we’re talking about exciting defensive players.

Run blocking is the foundation of play-action. And play-action is the foundation of an efficient passing game. When the line can’t move defenders off the ball, defenses can sit back on the pass. The quarterback faces more pressure. And every third down gets harder. So fixing the interior of the line isn’t just about handing the ball off better. It lifts the entire offense. That’s why the offensive line has to be at the top of the shopping list.

And that brings us to the second name. Jackson Powers-Johnson of the Las Vegas Raiders.

The Raiders got a good win over the Jets on Sunday. But in that division, with that quarterback and that defense, I think they know they’re not going anywhere this year. They’re still in rebuild mode. And ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler thinks Powers-Johnson could be an asset for a team looking for help inside.

Fowler wrote that Powers-Johnson was benched in week six and would probably welcome a change at this point. Several teams are searching the guard market, and Powers-Johnson wouldn’t cost nearly as much as the big names. Fowler said a day-three pick swap might get it done, letting the Raiders improve their spot in the back of the draft.

If that’s the price, why not? The Packers are still struggling to run the ball. There’s no real clarity on the interior. And they’d be giving up a late day-three pick, which Green Bay has plenty of.

I know Powers-Johnson hasn’t lived up to his draft position. But I also think Las Vegas has been a tough fit. It would’ve been hard for anybody to have success behind that line, especially last season, which was his second year in the league, and he dealt with some injuries too. Sometimes a player just needs the right landing spot.

And here’s a fun detail. In a report from right before his draft, Green Bay was one of the teams doing a lot of homework on him. So we know that at one point, about two and a half years ago, Gutekunst had real interest. Why couldn’t he have that interest again?

And this is a great example of how the deadline market works. A benched guard on a rebuilding team is a very different asset from a starter on a team that just fell out of the race. The first one is cheap. The second one can be expensive. Green Bay can choose either path. And that leads us to the third name.

Landon Dickerson.

We’ve talked about him before, and I really hope he ends up in green and gold. Philadelphia’s season is basically over. They lost to the Commanders on Sunday night. They’re dead last in the division. And their quarterback is done for the year, leaving Tanner McKee or Joe Milton under center. This is a team that’s going to sell off pieces, get a great draft position, and with Howie Roseman’s track record in the draft, they’ll probably do well with it.

They’re not trading Saquon Barkley. But ESPN confirmed on Monday that the Eagles have had trade talks involving other players, as Roseman looks for future draft pick compensation.

And ESPN’s Matt Bowen spoke glowingly about Dickerson. He said that when Dickerson is healthy, he can move defenders off the ball as a run blocker in both zone and gap schemes. And he can set an anchor in pass protection and handle power rushers down the middle with his six-foot-six, three hundred thirty-two pound frame.

That’s exactly the kind of player you want on the interior. Someone you can trust both in pass blocking and in run blocking. Because let’s be honest about this current line. Only one Packers offensive lineman has a run-blocking grade above sixty this year. And a couple are in the thirties. It’s been that bad.

So I think an offensive lineman is going to be added at some point over the next few days. And should the Packers trade for one? I say yes.

But there’s one reason to love the Dickerson idea and one reason to be careful, and they’re the same thing. Health. Bowen’s praise came with a condition. When he’s healthy. That qualifier matters, because a lineman who’s great when healthy but keeps missing time doesn’t solve the problem the way a durable one does. If the Packers go after him, the medical review has to be part of the price.

And the fourth name. Tony Pollard.

We’ve talked about Pollard for a while, and he would give Green Bay a different kind of option at running back. He’s been phenomenal with Tennessee. He had a great game against Cincinnati on Sunday, and he’s on a three-week stretch of excellent football. That probably helps his trade value from the Titans’ point of view. Tom Pelissero reported that multiple teams are interested. And earlier reports had Tennessee’s asking price at around a third or fourth-round pick.

I don’t expect the Packers to swing huge at running back. But a back like Pollard makes sense. I don’t see this running back room going anywhere with what it has right now. And I know the offensive line isn’t great either. Some people say Walter Payton, Barry Sanders, and Emmitt Smith wouldn’t look good behind this line. But I think it’s partly both. There are two sides to every problem, and this run game is the same. Partly the backs. Partly the big guys up front.

Now, here’s how I think about all four of these names together.

The ideal deadline isn’t four trades. It’s one or two precise moves that fix the biggest weakness without costing the future. A guard or center who raises the floor of the run game, plus maybe one running back. That’s a measured, smart response to a real problem. Spending big on a defensive luxury, no matter how exciting the name, wouldn’t fix what’s actually holding this offense back.

But the Sweat idea also says something about how contenders think. The best teams don’t wait for a weakness to become a crisis. They look at what they’re already good at and ask if adding more makes them even harder to beat. That’s the lesson from that Eagles Super Bowl run. Dominate up front, and everything else gets easier. Green Bay has the pieces to follow that model on defense. The question is whether they want to push even further or protect their flexibility.

Still, the front office has to be honest about what’s broken. If only one lineman grades above sixty and a couple are in the thirties, no amount of defensive talent hides that. A pass rush can get you stops. But it can’t create the kind of balance that wins in January. So even if Sweat is the most exciting name, I keep coming back to the interior line as the place where a trade would change the most.

And that brings us to good news number three. The playoff picture.

According to Zack Cruz, if the Packers win their next three games, at New England, against Minnesota, and against the Rams, their playoff chances rise to ninety percent. Ninety percent.

Think about that. Three games. The Patriots, the defending AFC champions. The Vikings, the division rival who beat Green Bay in week one. And the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve, which just might be the night Micah Parsons returns.

That’s a tough stretch. No doubt about it. But look at what this team has going for it right now. Jordan Love is carrying the offense, with two touchdown passes in seven of eight games. Christian Watson is playing the best football of his career, broken rib and all. The defense has allowed under fifteen points a game over the last four weeks. Ty’Ron Hopper is emerging. Evan Williams is playing like the best safety in football. And Micah Parsons is on his way back.

Add one or two smart moves before Tuesday, a guard like Landon Dickerson or Jackson Powers-Johnson, maybe a back like Tony Pollard, and this team could hit that three-game stretch stronger than it’s been all season.

So that’s your triple dose of great news, Packers Nation. A superstar coming back. A trade market full of realistic options. And a clear path to the playoffs. This could be the week that turns a good start into something special.
