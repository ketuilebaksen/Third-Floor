Packers Nation, just when it felt like everything was finally coming together, we got hit with some sad news. Forty-eight hours after the Green Bay Packers dropped forty-five points on the defending AFC champions, after the run game finally came alive, after the whole locker room was celebrating a total team win, Matt LaFleur stepped up to the podium and confirmed it.

Josh Jacobs needs surgery.

And the timing couldn’t be worse. Right on the edge of the NFL trade deadline. Right before a huge division game against the Minnesota Vikings. With the Packers sitting at six and three and building real momentum.

Welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’re breaking down everything we know about the Jacobs injury. How it happened. Why the surgery is necessary. Whether he could land on injured reserve. And what it means for this backfield going forward. Plus, we’ll look at three cheaper running backs who were floated as deadline options before this injury, and whether one of them might have been the answer. Let’s get into it.

Before we start, smash that like button, subscribe to Third Floor, and turn on notifications. And drop a comment right now: how worried are you about the run game without Josh Jacobs? Scale of one to ten.

Let’s start with what happened. Because this was one of those injuries that seemed small at first, and then turned into something much bigger.

It happened in the first half of the Patriots game. According to Yahoo Sports writer Jason Owens, Jacobs appears to have hurt his finger while stiff-arming Patriots safety Craig Woodson. His fingers seemed to get caught in Woodson’s facemask. And if you’ve ever seen a finger get caught in a facemask at full speed, you know that’s not good.

Jacobs went to the locker room. And later, he came back to the sideline with his finger wrapped up. And here’s the part that tells you who Josh Jacobs is. He wanted to keep playing. He wanted to get back out there. But he never re-entered the game. He was listed as questionable to return, and the Packers decided to finish the game without him, splitting the carries among the other backs.

And that detail matters. Because Green Bay found a way to win without him. After Jacobs went down, the Packers still finished with thirty-two carries for one hundred twenty-nine yards and two rushing touchdowns as a team. LaFleur even joked in the locker room about the run game, before revealing those numbers to a team that went crazy hearing them. So the backups proved they could get the job done for an afternoon.

But here’s the thing. One afternoon of committee carries is not the same as building an entire game plan without your starting running back. Week after week. Against defenses that know he’s not there.

After the game, LaFleur confirmed that Jacobs had fractured his finger. And at first, LaFleur was hopeful. He initially thought Jacobs might be able to play through it. Running backs play through hand injuries all the time. You tape it up, you wear a club or a brace, and you go.

But on Monday, the news changed. LaFleur announced that Jacobs needs surgery.

And here’s the detail that explains why. According to LaFleur, Jacobs had no grip strength in that hand. None.

Think about what that means for a running back. Your job is to carry the football through traffic. Through contact. With linebackers and safeties trying to rip it out of your arms on every single carry. Without grip strength, ball security becomes a massive problem. One strip, one fumble, at the wrong moment, and a game is gone. You can’t play running back if you can’t hold onto the football.

So the surgery is the right call. Even if it hurts in the short term.

Now, the big question. How long will Green Bay be without him?

And the honest answer is, we don’t know yet. LaFleur did not rule out injured reserve. He didn’t confirm it either. But it’s on the table. And if the Packers do place Jacobs on IR, NFL rules say he’d have to miss at least four games.

Four games. That’s a big chunk of the second half of the season. And the very next game is the Minnesota Vikings on November fifteenth. A division rival. A team that beat Green Bay in week one. Without a clear timeline, the Packers have to prepare like Jacobs won’t be there.

So let’s be clear about what’s confirmed and what isn’t. The fracture is confirmed. The surgery is confirmed. The lack of grip strength is confirmed. Injured reserve and a return date are not confirmed. We’ll keep you updated as soon as we know more.

And this hurts even more when you look at the context of Jacobs’ season. Because it’s been a stop-and-start year from the very beginning.

Jacobs missed the first six games of the season on the commissioner’s exempt list. He finally returned in week seven against Detroit. And in the two games between his return and the Patriots matchup, he ran for seventy-five yards and a touchdown. That’s two point three yards per carry. Not great. He admitted himself that he felt some rust, and LaFleur said he was still working his way back. CBS Sports had the Packers ranked dead last in the league in expected points added per carry.

But here’s the part people forget. Jacobs was doing a lot more than just running the ball. In those two games, he also caught eleven passes for sixty-eight yards and two receiving touchdowns. Two receiving touchdowns in two games. He was becoming a real weapon for Jordan Love out of the backfield.

And LaFleur has talked about what else Jacobs brings. He said Jacobs brings physicality, toughness, and an edge to the whole team when he’s around the guys. That kind of presence doesn’t show up in the box score. But it matters in a locker room.

So whoever replaces him has to do a lot. They have to run the ball. They have to catch passes. They have to protect Jordan Love in pass protection. And they have to execute their assignments on every single snap. That’s a lot to ask of a backup.

So who’s next up?

The Packers have MarShawn Lloyd, who’s been the team’s leading rusher this season, but has struggled to stay efficient. He’s had one hundred forty-nine rushing yards on fifty-seven carries, well under three yards a pop. LaFleur has given him plenty of chances. He did break off a touchdown run against Chicago when he bounced it outside.

They have Kaleb Johnson, who came over from Pittsburgh in a trade for a 2028 sixth-round pick on August thirtieth. LaFleur said a few weeks ago that Johnson needs to contribute on special teams and be rock solid in pass protection to earn more opportunities. Now he might get those opportunities.

They have Pierre Strong Jr., who’s barely been used this season. A lot of fans have been asking for him to get more chances. When nothing is working, you owe it to yourself to see if a different back can find something the others can’t.

And they have Chris Brooks, who’s been on injured reserve with an ankle injury. The Packers designated him to return from IR on November fourth. So he could be close to rejoining the active roster. And remember, before he got hurt, Brooks had a twenty-seven yard run earlier this season.

And don’t forget Savion Williams. He’s been used as a receiver, but he came out of college as more of a hybrid running back type. He ripped off an eighteen-yard run against Carolina, the team’s only run longer than six yards in that game. LaFleur told him he wants him to be the goon. A big, physical presence. Could Savion get a bigger role in the backfield now?

That’s a committee. And committees can work. We saw it against New England. But none of those backs have shown they can carry the load week after week. That’s the concern.

And that brings us to the other story. The trade deadline.

Because before the Jacobs injury, there was a lot of talk about Green Bay making a big move at running back. And most of that talk centered on Jonathan Taylor from Indianapolis. One of the most explosive backs in football. But also one of the most expensive. The Colts were reportedly asking for a first-round pick and more, or something like a second, third, fourth, and fifth-round package. And the Packers already gave up two first-round picks for Micah Parsons.

So writer Wendell Ferreira at A to Z Sports asked a different question in a November fourth analysis. What if the smarter answer wasn’t the expensive superstar? What if three much cheaper backs could give Green Bay exactly the help it needed, without sacrificing valuable draft picks?

He named three. Tony Pollard. Kimani Vidal. And Bam Knight.

Now let’s be clear. These were hypothetical, pre-deadline trade proposals made using a trade simulator. Not completed deals. And the article came out before the Jacobs injury. But they’re worth looking at, because they tell us what kind of options were out there.

Option number one. Tony Pollard from the Tennessee Titans.

Tennessee was one and seven at the time, and Pollard was in the final season of his three-year contract, heading toward free agency. According to Ferreira, Pollard’s compensation for the season is about seven point two six million dollars. That’s a six point seven four million base salary plus about five hundred ten thousand in per-game roster bonuses. Green Bay would have absorbed roughly four million in remaining salary.

The simulator proposed a Packers fifth-round pick in exchange for Pollard and Tennessee’s 2027 seventh-round pick, with no salary retention. And remember, earlier reporting from Packer Report said Tennessee’s asking price was closer to a third or fourth-round pick, and that Green Bay was among the teams that had contacted the Titans about him.

Pollard was the most expensive of the three options. But he brought the most experience. Through eight games, he’d run for five hundred ninety-five yards and six touchdowns at four and a half yards per carry. He’d been one of the better backs in football over a three-week stretch. And with Jacobs now facing surgery, a proven, experienced back like Pollard looks even more valuable.

Option number two. Kimani Vidal from the Los Angeles Chargers.

According to Ferreira, the Chargers were deep in a losing season, and Vidal was stuck behind Omarion Hampton and another back on the depth chart. But when he did get the ball, he produced. Twenty-nine carries for one hundred thirty-two yards. That’s four point six yards per carry.

And he was cheap. Vidal was on a one-year deal near the veteran minimum. Green Bay would have taken on less than six hundred thousand dollars in remaining salary. The simulator valued him at about a seventh-round pick.

The question with Vidal was whether he could handle a bigger workload and hold up in pass protection. But for a seventh-round pick and less than six hundred thousand dollars? That’s affordable upside.

Option number three. And this one is really interesting. Bam Knight from the Arizona Cardinals.

Knight is twenty-five years old, and he’s bounced around, with stops with the Jets and the Lions before landing in Arizona. And in limited touches, he’s been very effective. Twenty-eight carries for one hundred sixty-eight yards. That’s six yards per attempt. Six!

Ferreira estimated about six hundred forty thousand dollars in remaining salary, and the simulator suggested a seventh-round pick.

But here’s what makes Knight so intriguing for Green Bay. He already knows an offense very similar to the Packers’. Arizona’s head coach is Mike LaFleur. Matt LaFleur’s brother. And the two of them use related offensive concepts. So Knight would already understand the language, the zone running scheme, the motion, the play-action. He could learn the system quickly. In the middle of a season, that’s a huge advantage.

But there was a big obstacle. Arizona’s own injuries. Jeremiyah Love was heading to injured reserve. James Conner and Trey Benson were already there. That left Tyler Allgeier, Evan Hull, and Knight as the Cardinals’ active running backs. And Arizona was three and five, still hanging around. Trading away another running back could have left them dangerously thin. And Arizona never confirmed any willingness to trade Knight.

So let’s sum up those three options. Pollard offered experience. Vidal offered affordable upside. And Knight offered system familiarity. All three were far cheaper than Jonathan Taylor. But none of these deals were confirmed. They were ideas. And as of the reports we have, none of them happened.

So now the Packers have to figure it out with what they’ve got. At least for now.

And here’s what makes this such an important test. This offense just scored forty-five points in New England. Jordan Love had a career night, twenty-three of twenty-nine for three hundred seventy-two yards and three touchdown passes, plus a rushing touchdown. Matthew Golden and Christian Watson became the first duo in Packers history to each have seven catches, one hundred fifty yards, and a touchdown in the same game. The momentum is real.

The challenge now is keeping that momentum without Josh Jacobs.

And maybe that’s where the Patriots game gives us hope. Because remember what analysts said after that win. They said the Packers were true to who they are. A deep-ball passing team. Build the lead through the air. And then run the ball to hold it. That’s what happened in New England. The passing game created space, and the run game, even without Jacobs, took advantage. Thirty-two carries, one hundred twenty-nine yards, two touchdowns.

If LaFleur stays aggressive, if Jordan Love keeps attacking downfield, if Watson and Golden keep winning their matchups, then defenses can’t sit in the box to stop the run. And that makes life easier for whoever is carrying the football. MarShawn Lloyd. Kaleb Johnson. Pierre Strong. Chris Brooks. Savion Williams. It doesn’t have to be one guy. It just has to work.

And there’s another reason for hope. The defense. Four takeaways against New England. Four sacks. Xavier McKinney and Evan Williams with interceptions. Isaiah McDuffie with a forced fumble and a recovery. And Micah Parsons is back at practice, with his practice window running through November twenty-fifth. When the defense plays like this, the offense doesn’t have to be perfect. It just has to take care of the football and take advantage of the short fields.

Great teams respond to setbacks. Every contender loses key players at some point in a season. Jayden Reed went down in week two. Edgerrin Cooper and Javon Hargrave were lost in back-to-back weeks. The starting center and right guard went down. Christian Watson played through a broken rib. And this team kept winning. Five of the last six. Six and three overall.

Now Josh Jacobs is the next setback. And the Vikings are next on the schedule. This is Green Bay’s next test.

So here’s my take, Packers Nation. Losing Jacobs hurts. It hurts his teammates, it hurts the offense, and it hurts Jacobs himself, who’s been trying to get back on track all season. I hope his surgery goes perfectly and he’s back on the field as soon as possible. But this team has shown again and again that it can handle adversity. And with the way Jordan Love is playing right now, I believe it can handle this one too.
