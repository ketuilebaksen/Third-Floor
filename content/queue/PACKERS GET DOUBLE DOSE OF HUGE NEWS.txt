More than three hundred days. That’s how long Micah Parsons spent working toward one Wednesday practice. Three hundred days of rehab. Of crutches. Of watching his teammates play without him. And when he finally stepped back onto that practice field, the most interesting thing he said wasn’t that his knee feels great. It was that his conditioning is probably the worst it’s ever been in his NFL career.

That kind of honesty tells you a lot about where the Green Bay Packers stand right now. Their best pass rusher is finally walking back toward the field. Their run game is stuck at the very bottom of the league. And with the trade deadline just two days away, two different trade ideas have surfaced that could fix real problems on this roster.

Let’s start with the best news. Huge news number one. Micah Parsons.

After nearly eleven months recovering from a torn ACL and additional meniscus damage, Green Bay’s superstar pass rusher practiced on Wednesday for the first time since his injury last December. And let me be clear right away, because this matters. He’s returned to practice. Not to game action.

The Packers have opened his twenty-one-day practice window, which runs through November twenty-fifth. And that just happens to be the same day Green Bay is scheduled to face the Los Angeles Rams. A Thanksgiving Eve matchup that could become a target for his season debut. But nothing is confirmed.

And I love how Parsons handled the question. He said even his own family has been asking whether they should book flights to come watch him play. And he told them he’s not going to announce a date. He doesn’t want to raise everyone’s expectations and then find out his body needs more time.

Honestly, I respect that a lot. The Packers need Micah Parsons healthy for the most important part of the season. Not rushed back just to hit a date on a calendar.

Now let’s talk about that conditioning comment, because it really caught my attention. Parsons admitted his conditioning is probably the worst it’s ever been in the NFL. And remember who we’re talking about. A four-time All-Pro. A guy who had twelve and a half sacks in just fourteen games last season. The first player in league history to start his career with five straight seasons of at least twelve sacks. That’s the player Green Bay gave up two first-round picks to bring in. The player who was supposed to transform this defense.

And he knows he won’t come back at that level right away. Last season, he averaged around fifty defensive snaps a game. He doesn’t expect to handle that workload at first. And when you think about what he’s been through, it makes total sense. He missed all of training camp. He spent months rebuilding his knee. And the extra meniscus repairs kept him on crutches for seven or eight weeks. You don’t just bounce back from that overnight.

But here’s the encouraging part. He says his knee feels great. And he said he wasn’t nervous about returning to the practice field at all. After more than three hundred days of work, he’s just excited to take the next step.

And he’s being realistic. He expects mistakes. He said there will be plays where he looks like the old All-Pro and plays where he struggles. But the one thing he refuses to compromise is his effort. If he needs to come off the field to catch his breath, he’ll do it. But whenever he’s in there, he’s giving everything he has.

That’s exactly the attitude this defense needs.

And now put that next to the record. Green Bay is five and three without its biggest pass-rushing weapon. And that’s huge when you remember how last season ended. After Parsons got hurt, the Packers lost their final five games. This year, the defense hasn’t just survived without him. Jonathan Gannon has turned it into the identity of this team. Ty’Ron Hopper has emerged. Evan Williams is playing at an All-Pro level. Lukas Van Ness is having a career year. Devonte Wyatt and Karl Brooks have been steady inside.

Now imagine adding Micah Parsons to a unit that’s already holding its own.

Even with limited snaps, his presence changes how offenses attack you. Quarterbacks have to account for his speed on every dropback. Tackles have to prepare all week for his pass rush. And his teammates benefit from the extra attention he draws. Suddenly Van Ness is getting one-on-ones. Wyatt is seeing lighter double teams. Blitzers come free because the protection is sliding toward number one.

I’m not saying he’s going to dominate every snap right away. He’s warned against expecting that himself. But even a limited version of Micah Parsons can matter in the biggest passing situations. One third-down sack ends a drive. One forced fumble changes a game. And those moments become more and more important in November and December.

Parsons gets that better than anyone. He recently explained the importance of November football to a rookie teammate. His message was simple. November prepares teams for December. And December helps determine who they become heading into the playoffs.

That’s why he’s so excited to be back now. He wants to help Green Bay finish strong. And he ended his press conference with the perfect line.

Let the show begin.

So here’s my question for you. Would you rather see Parsons return as soon as he’s medically ready, even with limited snaps? Or give him more time to get stronger? Green Bay needs his talent, but it also needs him healthy for the long run. There’s no perfect answer here. And the good news is that the practice window gives the staff some room to decide.

Now let’s get to huge news number two. The deadline plan.

Because while the defense is preparing for a massive boost, the offense still has problems that might need outside help. And there are two trade ideas getting a lot of attention right now. One to fix the backfield. And one to fix something that’s quietly cost this team a lot of points.

Let’s start with the running back.

According to a writer at Packer Report, Green Bay is among the teams that have contacted the Tennessee Titans about Tony Pollard. And the same report says Tennessee’s asking price is roughly a third or fourth-round pick.

Now I want to be careful here. This is reported interest. Not a completed trade. There’s no confirmation that Green Bay has submitted a formal offer, or that Tennessee has agreed to move him. But the fit makes a lot of sense.

Through eight games, Green Bay’s rushing attack has ranked dead last in the league. Four hundred eighty-five rushing yards. The first team in NFL history with a winning record through eight games while running for fewer than five hundred yards. That’s a serious concern for a team that wants to play deep into January. As much as we love watching Jordan Love make big throws, this team can’t rely entirely on the passing game. It needs a run game that can control the clock, create manageable third downs, and keep defenses guessing.

And that’s where Pollard becomes really interesting. Through eight games with Tennessee, he’s carried the ball one hundred thirty-three times for five hundred ninety-five yards and six touchdowns. That’s four and a half yards per carry. Plus fourteen catches for seventy yards. Those are solid numbers. And when you compare them to what Green Bay has right now, they look even better.

And his price could be a lot more manageable than some of the bigger names out there. We’ve all heard about Jonathan Taylor. But Taylor would be a much bigger investment. One speculative package could involve a second-round pick, a third-round pick, and maybe another selection on top of that. That’s a ton of draft capital for a team that already gave up two first-rounders for Micah Parsons.

So this is where Brian Gutekunst has a big decision to make. Does he chase the biggest possible upgrade, knowing it could cost several valuable picks? Or does he target a more affordable player who can strengthen the backfield without sacrificing the future?

Personally, that’s what makes Pollard so intriguing to me. He might not create the same buzz as Taylor. But he doesn’t need to become the centerpiece of the offense to help. If he can just make the run game more consistent, give Jordan a reliable pass-catcher out of the backfield, and change the pace from what Green Bay already has, that alone makes him valuable.

But there’s another issue we can’t ignore. The offensive line. Adding a talented running back doesn’t automatically fix poor blocking. If defenders are already in the backfield before the runner can get going, changing who carries the ball won’t fix everything. That’s why the Packers have also been linked to guards like Landon Dickerson from Philadelphia. The whole rushing attack needs to be evaluated.

And remember, Josh Jacobs is already in this backfield. Any addition has to fit next to him. Pollard would give the Packers another experienced option, but that doesn’t mean he’d automatically become the lead back. His role would depend on how Matt LaFleur and the offensive staff decide to use him.

Now the second trade idea. And I’ll be honest, this one surprised me a little. But the more I think about it, the more it makes sense.

The kicking game.

ESPN analyst Seth Walder suggested that the Packers consider trading for Indianapolis Colts kicker Spencer Shrader. And again, let’s be clear. This is an analyst’s proposal. Not a report that Green Bay is negotiating with Indianapolis. But the reasoning is very easy to understand.

The Packers’ rookie kicker has struggled badly through his first eight games. He’s made eleven of sixteen field goal attempts. That’s five misses. And he’s missed three extra points on top of that. For a team competing in the NFC, that’s a real problem. Because close games get decided by a single kick.

And we’ve seen it happen. In Detroit, the Packers lost by three, and they missed two field goals in that game. Six points left on the field in a three-point loss. Then against Carolina, an extra point bounced off the upright. And late in that game, Matt LaFleur chose to punt instead of attempting a fifty-five yarder that could have sealed the win. When your head coach doesn’t trust your kicker in a big moment, it changes everything. Your fourth-down decisions. Your field goal range. How you manage the end of games.

Now compare that to Spencer Shrader. According to the report, he’s made nineteen of twenty-one field goals this season. He’s thirteen of fifteen from forty yards or longer. And he’s six of seven from fifty yards or more. Six of seven from fifty-plus! That’s the kind of consistency Green Bay desperately needs.

And there’s another reason this idea makes sense. Shrader is in a contract year. And Indianapolis has been making big roster changes, which raises the question of whether the Colts would consider more trades. If they’re not planning to extend him, moving him now could get them draft compensation before he hits free agency.

But that’s only one possibility. The Colts could decide a reliable kicker is worth more than a pick. And Green Bay would have to decide whether spending a draft pick on a kicker makes sense. It’s an unusual trade idea, for sure. But think about how many big games come down to three points or less. A dependable kicker can be the difference between a playoff spot and watching from home.

And none of this means the rookie can’t improve. He’s a rookie. The Packers invested real resources in him, trading up to get him. LaFleur has publicly backed him. But Green Bay is also trying to win right now. And that’s what makes this idea worth talking about.

So let me tie all of this together. Because these storylines share a theme.

The Packers are trying to win now without mortgaging what comes next.

Micah Parsons is the free addition. He’s already on the roster. He doesn’t cost a single pick at the deadline. And he could be the biggest upgrade any team in the league makes this month.

Tony Pollard is the affordable fix for the backfield. A third or fourth-round pick for a back who’s averaging four and a half yards a carry and catching the ball.

A guard like Landon Dickerson is the fix up front, the move that helps every back and protects Jordan Love.

And a kicker like Spencer Shrader is the fix for the points this team has been leaving on the field.

Every move has a cost. And Gutekunst has to decide which improvements give the biggest benefit without sacrificing too much of the future. But here’s what excites me. This team is five and three, with a defense playing at a high level and a quarterback who’s been carrying the offense. And the biggest problems on this roster are fixable. Not with a blockbuster. With smart, targeted moves.

Picture it. December at Lambeau. Micah Parsons is back to full speed, wrecking the pocket next to Van Ness. Jordan Love has a real run game behind him for the first time all season. And when it comes down to a fifty-yard kick to win the game, you actually feel confident watching it go up.

That’s a championship formula. And it’s closer than you think.
