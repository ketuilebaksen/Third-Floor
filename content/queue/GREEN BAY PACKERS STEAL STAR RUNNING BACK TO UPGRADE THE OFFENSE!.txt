And that brings us to the big story. The one that’s got everybody talking. Saquon Barkley.

So here’s what’s going on. On October twelfth, Heavy published a trade scenario where the Green Bay Packers would acquire Saquon Barkley from the Philadelphia Eagles in exchange for a conditional fourth-round draft pick. And that pick could be upgraded to a third-rounder if certain benchmarks are hit, like playing time or postseason success.

Now again, let’s be clear. This is hypothetical. There’s no agreement. There’s no report that the teams are talking. It’s a proposal, an idea. But it’s an idea that makes a lot of sense when you look at Green Bay’s situation at running back.

Think about where the Packers are right now. Caleb Johnson, MarShawn Lloyd, and Chris Brooks are the guys in the picture. And yes, we’ve seen flashes. Lloyd had that big touchdown run against Chicago. Johnson and Brooks have broken off some chunk plays. But this team has struggled to build any kind of consistent rushing attack. Coming into the Bears game, they were dead last in the league in rushing yards per game. And then there’s the Josh Jacobs situation. Jacobs is on the commissioner’s exempt list, and there’s real uncertainty about whether he’ll be available for the rest of the season. So there’s a hole in this backfield. A big one.

And that’s where Saquon Barkley comes in. If this ever happened, he would be an instant upgrade. The speed. The power. The ability to make people miss and break tackles. He’d give Green Bay a legitimate home-run threat on the ground, something this offense just hasn’t had this year. He can run between the tackles. He can bounce it outside and outrun everybody. And he can catch the football out of the backfield, which opens up a whole new set of options for Matt LaFleur.

And think about what that does for Jordan Love. Right now, defenses know the Packers can’t really run the ball, so they can sit back, play coverage, and dare Jordan to beat them. Put Saquon Barkley in that backfield, and suddenly every defense has to respect the run. Safeties have to creep up. Linebackers have to stay home. And what happens when safeties creep up? Christian Watson and Matthew Golden get one-on-one shots deep. Play-action becomes deadly. The offense becomes balanced, unpredictable, and way harder to defend. And Jordan doesn’t have to carry the whole offense on his arm every single week.

We all know what Barkley’s ceiling looks like. In 2024, he ran for two thousand and five yards in the regular season. Two thousand and five! And he helped the Eagles win Super Bowl fifty-nine. That’s not just a good running back. That’s a historic season. A championship-level weapon.

But, and this is a big but, Packers Nation, his current situation is a lot more complicated. According to the report, through his first four games in 2026, Barkley has thirty-six carries for one hundred eighty-two yards. That’s five point one yards per carry, which is solid, but he hasn’t scored a rushing touchdown. And he’s been dealing with a hamstring injury and reportedly left two of those games early. So the health question would be front and center for any team thinking about trading for him. You can’t take a guy whose body might not hold up and make him the centerpiece of your run game without checking every box.

Then there’s the contract. Barkley signed a two-year, forty-one point two million dollar extension back in 2025, running through the 2028 season, according to the report. That’s a lot of money for a veteran running back who’s dealing with a hamstring issue. Green Bay would have to think long and hard about that kind of financial commitment.
