Packers Nation, this is a big one. Week seven. The Green Bay Packers are heading into Ford Field to face the Detroit Lions, and Matt LaFleur just stepped up to the podium and dropped a whole lot of news. Injuries, lineup changes, a quarterback who’s banged up, and one decision that could change the entire look of this offense. Josh Jacobs is back on the practice field, and LaFleur says he believes he’s on track to play.

Welcome back to Third Floor, your home for everything Green Bay. And today we’re going through every single thing LaFleur said ahead of this Lions game. What’s going on with Jordan Love’s back. Who’s out at linebacker. Whether Christian Watson and Matthew Golden are good to go. What LaFleur thinks about Jared Goff. And of course, the Josh Jacobs situation, which is easily the biggest storyline of the week. Let’s get into it.

Alright, let’s start with the headline. Josh Jacobs.

We’ve talked about the Jacobs situation a lot on this channel. He’s been away from this team, and the uncertainty around him has hung over the Packers’ running game all season. Coming into the Bears game, Green Bay ranked dead last in the league in rushing. There were trade ideas flying around for Saquon Barkley. Fans were wondering if Jacobs would ever suit up for this team again this year.

And now? He’s back in the building. He got what LaFleur described as a real practice in this week. And when LaFleur was asked if Jacobs is on track to where the team wants him to be in order to play on Sunday, he said yes, he believes so. Now, he was careful. He said they’ll get through practice, see how Jacobs is feeling, and see the confidence he has in himself. And he added that he thinks that confidence is pretty strong. The final conversations, he said, will take place after practice.

So let’s be clear. It’s not officially locked in yet. But the head coach of the Green Bay Packers is telling you he believes Josh Jacobs is on track. That’s a huge statement.

LaFleur also explained how they’ve been ramping him up. Jacobs has been in the building working with the strength staff and the athletic trainers. But as LaFleur said, you can’t simulate football until you’re out there doing it. So these final practices are going to be big work for him. And when he was asked how the team will know if Jacobs is truly ready, LaFleur said there will be a lot of conversation, and it’s also a little bit of the eyeball test. They’re going to watch him move. They’re going to watch him cut. They’re going to see if he looks like Josh Jacobs.

And Packers Nation, think about what this could mean. This offense has been living and dying through the air. Jordan Love, Christian Watson, Matthew Golden, they’ve been carrying the load. The run game has had some flashes, MarShawn Lloyd’s big touchdown against Chicago, some chunk runs from Caleb Johnson and Chris Brooks. But there’s been no consistency. No identity. No guy who can grind out four and five yards a carry and wear a defense down in the fourth quarter.

That’s what Josh Jacobs does. That’s who he is. He’s a physical, downhill, tough runner who makes defenders pay for trying to tackle him. And if he’s even close to his old self, he gives this offense the balance it’s been missing all season. Defenses can’t just sit back in coverage and dare Jordan Love to beat them anymore. They have to respect the run. They have to bring a safety down. And when they bring that safety down, guess who’s waiting deep? Watson and Golden.

Now, the realistic expectation. Jacobs probably isn’t going to get thirty carries on Sunday. He’s been away. He’s ramping up. You’d expect the Packers to ease him in, maybe give him a portion of the work alongside the other backs. But even fifteen hard, physical carries against Detroit could change the entire feel of this offense. That’s why this decision matters so much.

Now let’s talk about the injury that’s going to hurt on defense. A Packers linebacker took a knee to the shin in a collision during practice, and LaFleur confirmed he’s going to be out for this game. When he was asked if it was a fracture or an IR situation, LaFleur said he didn’t believe so, that they were still working through it, but he doesn’t think it’ll be anything too long-term. He called it an unfortunate deal that happens when you play football, especially with how hard the guys have been practicing.

So who steps up? LaFleur said the team has a lot of confidence in Isaiah McDuffie, Ty’Ron Hopper, and Nick Niemann if needed, to go in and do a great job.

And he had a lot of praise for Hopper in particular. When asked what he’s seen from Hopper with these extra reps, LaFleur said Hopper has maximized them. He’s taken advantage of every opportunity. He called Hopper’s development over the years pretty cool to watch, and said the coaches got a sense early in training camp that he was on his way. Now he gets another big chance to prove it, against one of the best offenses in football. This is a massive opportunity for Ty’Ron Hopper. Ford Field. Lions. Big game. Let’s see what he’s got.

Next up, the big man in the middle. Jordan Riley. LaFleur was asked what he saw from Riley last year before the injury, and he lit up. He called Riley a big, stout, physical defensive lineman, exactly what this team needs. He said Riley brings great size, and that his energy and attitude are contagious. He said the work Riley puts in doesn’t go unnoticed by anybody in that building.

And on Riley’s status now, LaFleur said he’s shown a ton of progress. It was good to get him out there last week, just to get his feet wet. And if he keeps progressing, he’ll be ready to go. With Javon Hargrave gone for the season, a guy like Jordan Riley could become really important. A big body who can eat up blocks and help stop the run. Against Detroit, a team that loves to run the football, that’s exactly what you need.

Lukas Van Ness was limited earlier in the week, but LaFleur said yes, he’ll practice. That’s good news. Van Ness has been playing really well this season, with sixteen pressures and two and a half sacks through his first five games. You need him getting after Jared Goff on Sunday.

And then there was a curveball. One of the Packers’ players had been playing through pain for a while, and it finally reached a point where he couldn’t keep going. LaFleur called it a curveball, but said that’s the NFL. These things happen all the time. You just keep moving.

Now let’s talk about the question every Packers fan was waiting on. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden. Are there any concerns about them for Sunday?

LaFleur’s answer? One word.

No.

That’s it. Love it. Watson and Golden have been the heartbeat of this passing game. Watson has been unbelievable, and Golden has become a legit number two receiver. Having both of them healthy and ready to go against Detroit is huge. Breathe easy, Packers Nation.

But here’s the one that made everybody nervous. Jordan Love’s back.

LaFleur was asked when Jordan came to him about his back. And he said it was after the game. Jordan was pretty beat up. He took some shots. And LaFleur said he saw Jordan in there with the chiropractor after the game, so he knew something was up.

And when he was asked whether Love would be limited in practice, LaFleur didn’t give a straight answer. He just said they’ll see when they get out there on the field.

Now, let’s not panic. Nothing LaFleur said sounded like Jordan is in danger of missing this game. Quarterbacks take hits. They get sore. They see the chiropractor. That’s life in the NFL. But it is something to watch. A quarterback playing through back soreness might not have the same zip on the deep ball, might not be able to escape the pocket as smoothly. And against a Detroit team that will absolutely try to get after him, the offensive line has to protect Jordan Love like their lives depend on it.

And that’s where Josh Jacobs coming back matters even more. If Jacobs can give this offense a real run game, that means fewer long dropbacks, fewer hits on Jordan, and more manageable third downs. A strong run game protects your quarterback. It’s that simple.

Speaking of the offensive line, LaFleur was asked about Kevin Zeitler, and what he still wants to see from him before deciding whether he’ll start or play. LaFleur said Zeitler just has to keep working and showing improvement. And then he gushed about him. He said he loves the guy. He said Zeitler is wired the right way. His preparation, his effort on the field, being a great teammate, being supportive, helping the other guys. LaFleur said he’s everything you want in a player.

When a head coach talks about a veteran like that, you know he trusts him. And with all the shuffling on the interior of this offensive line this season, Zeitler’s experience could be exactly what this group needs to settle down.

LaFleur was also asked about Luke Musgrave, and whether he expects Musgrave to play this season. And this one was a little sobering. LaFleur said it’s tough to say right now. Until proven otherwise, he’d say no. But he added that he would never close the door on it. So don’t count on Musgrave anytime soon, but there’s still a sliver of hope.

Now let’s turn to the opponent. The Detroit Lions. At Ford Field.

LaFleur was asked about the Detroit crowd, and he gave them real respect. He said they’re a passionate fan base and it’s always a great atmosphere at Ford Field. He said it’s changed a lot over the years, from when he first got to Green Bay to where it is now. It’s a hostile environment. A tough place to play. And he expects it to be exactly that on Sunday.

He’s right. That place is going to be rocking. Detroit fans have been waiting for this one. And the Packers have to go in there, block out the noise, and play their game.

And there’s another challenge this week. The Lions are coming in rested, while the Packers are dealing with less preparation time and a lot of bumps and bruises. LaFleur was asked if that was a challenge. And he said it’s really more about getting his players’ bodies in the right position. Every team has had plenty of reps by now. Reps are always at a premium, but you need your guys feeling good when they step on the grass. He said when you face a team coming off extra rest, there are going to be some unscouted looks, similar to early in the season. And that’s when you have to trust your rules and trust your training.

Translation: Detroit is going to throw some new stuff at the Packers. Some wrinkles they haven’t shown on tape. And the Packers have to react on the fly and stay disciplined. That’s where coaching and preparation really show up.

And there’s a big name returning for Detroit too. Brian Branch. LaFleur was asked how Branch’s return changes the Lions’ defense, and he didn’t hold back. He called Branch a tremendous football player and one of the best safeties in the league. He said Branch can do things that others can’t. The Lions are comfortable playing him man to man on wide receivers. And he brings physicality and toughness. Just a great player.

That’s a real test for Watson and Golden. Branch is the kind of safety who can take things away and hit you hard when you catch the ball. Jordan Love will need to know where number thirty-two is on every single snap.

And then, the matchup with Jared Goff. LaFleur was asked about the key to slowing him down. And his answer was simple: it’s hard for a quarterback to throw when he’s on his back. You have to move him off his spot. LaFleur said Goff doesn’t need a lot of space to let the ball go. He’s confident in what he sees, he’ll let it rip, and he trusts his playmakers. He called Goff a decisive quarterback who handles pressure really well and stays poised.

And there’s a personal connection here. LaFleur worked with Goff for a year earlier in his career, and he said he has a tremendous amount of love and respect for him and how he goes about his business.

A reporter pointed out that Goff hasn’t thrown an interception this season. And LaFleur said the key is that Goff doesn’t predetermine his reads. Quarterbacks who decide where they’re going before the snap usually make bad decisions. Goff doesn’t do that. He understands coverages, he understands what he’s seeing, and he knows how to manipulate defenders.

So this defense has a job to do. Remember, against the Bears, this unit forced five turnovers. Five! Now they face a quarterback who hasn’t thrown a single pick all year. Something has to give. And I’ll tell you what, if Lukas Van Ness, Jordan Riley, and this pass rush can get Goff off his spot, and if Xavier McKinney is lurking in the back end like he did against Chicago, this defense can make Goff uncomfortable.

LaFleur was also asked about Jahmyr Gibbs and how twitchy and dynamic he is in both the run game and the pass game. And that’s another huge challenge. Gibbs can beat you on the ground, he can beat you catching it out of the backfield, and he can take any touch to the house. This linebacker group, already missing a starter, has to swarm to him every single time. McDuffie, Hopper, everybody. All eleven. Multiple hats to the ball. You’ve heard LaFleur say it before.

And there was one more interesting question about the coaching staff. Someone brought up a comment from an opposing coach, who said that with Jonathan Gannon’s defense, the Packers got bigger on special teams. And LaFleur said honestly, he doesn’t think much about it. He’s so focused on his own team, and on putting players in the right positions, whether it’s the front office or the coaches, so they can compete at the best of their ability. Classic LaFleur. Block out the noise. Focus on us.

So let’s put it all together. Here’s what we know heading into Packers at Lions.

Josh Jacobs is back practicing, and LaFleur believes he’s on track to play. Final call after practice. That’s the decision that could change everything.

Jordan Love is dealing with some back soreness after taking hits, but there’s no sign he’ll miss the game.

Christian Watson and Matthew Golden? No concerns. Both ready to go.

A starting linebacker is out with a shin injury, and Isaiah McDuffie, Ty’Ron Hopper, and Nick Niemann have to step up.

Jordan Riley is progressing and could be a big piece on the defensive line.

Lukas Van Ness will practice.

Kevin Zeitler has LaFleur’s full trust as the coaches decide his role.

And Luke Musgrave probably isn’t coming back this season, though the door isn’t shut.

On the other side, Detroit gets Brian Branch back, Jared Goff hasn’t thrown a pick all year, Jahmyr Gibbs is as dangerous as ever, and Ford Field is going to be loud.

This is a huge game, Packers Nation. A division road game against the Lions. The kind of game that tells you who you really are. And here’s why I’m excited. If Josh Jacobs comes back and gives this offense a real ground game, with Jordan Love throwing to Watson and Golden, this Packers offense could look more complete than it has all season. Balanced. Physical. Explosive. That’s the formula for beating Detroit at home. Run the ball, protect the quarterback, take your shots, and let the defense hunt.

And if this team goes into Ford Field and wins? Oh, the whole league is going to take notice.
