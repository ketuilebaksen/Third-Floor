Let me walk you through how injured reserve actually works. Because it rarely gets explained, and it matters a lot for this Patriots team.

When a player goes on injured reserve, he has to miss at least four games. That’s the rule. No exceptions.

Once those four games are over, the player becomes eligible to return. And that’s when the team can open his practice window. He gets back on the field with his teammates. And that window is the real test.

Because here’s the thing about injuries. A calf can feel fine on a Tuesday and fail on a Sunday. Running in a straight line on a treadmill is one thing. Cutting at full speed in practice against live defenders is another. So coaches watch everything. Speed. Cutting. Conditioning. Can he explode off the line? Can he stop and change direction? Can he run route after route without the injury flaring up?

That ramp-up protects the player. It gives him time to get back to football shape without risking a setback. And only once the coaches and trainers are confident do they make it official and activate him to the 53-man roster.

But the clock is always ticking. Once that practice window opens, the team has 21 days to make a decision. Activate him, or lose him for the year.

And there’s another layer. Teams only get a limited number of these IR returns per season. That’s why, earlier this season, the Patriots released punter Bryce Baringer instead of using one of those precious return slots on him. With around 20 players on injured reserve at one point, every return slot is gold.

So when the Patriots use one, you know they believe the player can make a real difference.
