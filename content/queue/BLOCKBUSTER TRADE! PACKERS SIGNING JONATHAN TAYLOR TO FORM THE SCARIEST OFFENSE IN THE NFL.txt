Packers Nation, the trade deadline is getting closer, and the name connected to Green Bay right now is about as big as it gets. Jonathan Taylor. One of the most explosive, most productive running backs in the entire National Football League. And according to reporting from Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport, the Packers are among the teams that have considered, or could consider, going after him.

Picture it. Jordan Love under center. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden on the outside. Tucker Kraft finding his groove again in the middle. And Jonathan Taylor in the backfield. That’s not just a good offense. That could be the scariest offense in football.

Welcome back to Third Floor. And before I get you too fired up, let me keep it honest like always. Nothing is done. There’s no agreed trade. There’s no offer that’s been reported. What we have is interest and speculation. But it’s real enough that we need to talk about it, especially after what happened in Detroit. Because that loss showed exactly why this team might need to make a big move.

Today we’re breaking down the twenty to seventeen loss to the Lions, what went wrong, who actually stepped up, and then we’re going deep on Jonathan Taylor. What it would cost, what it would mean for Josh Jacobs, and whether this move could turn Green Bay into a juggernaut.

Alright. Let’s start with the game, because it still hurts. Packers lose to the Lions, twenty to seventeen, at Ford Field. A three-point loss. In a game Green Bay had multiple chances to win. And the frustrating part is that the Packers made enough mistakes on their own to lose it. Yes, there were some officiating calls we’ll get to. But this one was largely self-inflicted.

And it starts with the kicker. Trey Smack. Two missed field goals in a three-point loss. That’s brutal. On the very first drive, Smack missed a twenty-six yarder. Inside a dome. There was some talk that the snap was a little high, but that’s a kick an NFL kicker has to make. And then in the fourth quarter, with the game tied at seventeen, he missed again. This time from thirty-five yards. The ball came off low and got tipped at the line. Another wasted chance.

That’s six points left on the field in a game you lose by three. Do the math, Packers Nation. That’s the ballgame.

And it might have affected the coaching too. Earlier in the game, Matt LaFleur went for it on fourth and four near the goal line instead of kicking a field goal that would have tied it. The Packers didn’t convert. And afterward, LaFleur admitted he regretted that call. Now, we can’t say for sure that decision came from a lack of confidence in his kicker. But when your kicking game becomes a question mark, it changes how you approach every big moment. LaFleur has publicly backed his rookie, and I’m not saying the Packers should give up on Smack after one bad day. But in close division games, you need to be able to trust your special teams. Period.

And then the offensive line. Another rough afternoon. Jordan Morgan had his hands full with Aidan Hutchinson. And look, Hutchinson is an elite pass rusher. Some tough snaps were expected. But Morgan still allowed three pressures, and early grades from Pro Football Focus had him as Green Bay’s lowest-graded run blocker. That’s not what you want from a former first-round pick.

Travis Glover had a really tough day at right guard too. One breakdown credited him with seven pressures allowed. Whatever the exact number, he struggled a lot in pass protection. And here’s the head-scratcher. Kevin Zeitler was a healthy scratch. Aaron Banks was available as a backup. So at some point, LaFleur and the staff have to ask the question: are we putting our best five offensive linemen on the field? Because Jordan Love can’t operate when the protection keeps falling apart.

And the run game didn’t help enough either. Green Bay came into week seven ranked twentieth in ESPN’s run block win rate. Even with Josh Jacobs back, the blocking just hasn’t been consistent. And when you can’t run the football, your offense becomes way too dependent on explosive plays. That works against some teams. Against the best teams in the league, it’s a dangerous way to live.

But it wasn’t all bad. There were some real bright spots, and they matter.

Tucker Kraft was one of the biggest positives. Four catches, forty-eight yards, and a touchdown. And more importantly, he’s starting to look like himself again after the catch. Breaking tackles. Turning short throws into real gains. Remember, LaFleur told us weeks ago that guys coming back from major injuries need time. Well, Kraft looks like he’s finding his rhythm. That’s huge for Jordan Love, because when your tight end can turn a quick throw into a big play, the whole offense gets easier.

On defense, Javon Bullard deserves some love. He had a tough assignment in the slot against Jameson Williams and Amon-Ra St. Brown, two guys who create matchup problems all game long. He finished with two tackles and made some important plays in coverage. Sam LaPorta was quiet too, although that wasn’t all on Bullard. Still, an encouraging day against a dangerous offense.

A young defensive back made a huge play as well. Yes, he had some tough moments against St. Brown. But in the third quarter, he came up with a crucial fourth-down stop that prevented a touchdown. That’s potentially a seven-point swing right there. And he showed a willingness to tackle and help in the run game. Those are the moments that build a young player’s confidence.

And on offense, Savion Williams made the most of his chances. Three targets, three catches, forty-seven yards. While other receivers, including Matthew Golden, dealt with drops, Savion was reliable every time the ball came his way. He’s not getting a huge workload, but he’s showing he can be a dependable third option.

Now, the officiating. And listen, I don’t want Packers Nation to become a fan base that blames every loss on the refs. But some of these roughing the passer calls are getting ridiculous. Brenton Cox got flagged on third down after he appeared to be pushed into Jared Goff. Detroit didn’t score on that drive, but it was still frustrating. And then Karl Brooks got flagged on Detroit’s final drive, in a situation with much bigger consequences. And when you compare that to some of the hits Jordan Love has taken without a flag, you understand why fans are upset.

But here’s the bottom line. Bad officiating deserves criticism. It doesn’t erase two missed field goals, poor blocking, and wasted opportunities. Those are things this team can fix. And if the Packers want to win the NFC North, they have to fix them now.

Which brings us to the big story. Jonathan Taylor.

So here’s what’s going on. The Indianapolis Colts recently fired general manager Chris Ballard. That’s created a lot of uncertainty about where that franchise is headed. And after that change, reports said the Colts could consider trading some veteran players. Jonathan Taylor is one of the names being discussed, along with guard Quenton Nelson and defensive tackle DeForest Buckner. And according to Schefter and Rapoport, the Packers are among several teams that have considered, or could consider, pursuing Taylor.

But again, let’s be clear. This is reported interest. It’s not a done deal. There’s no confirmation that the Colts will move Taylor, and there’s no report that Green Bay has made an offer. In fact, the reporting says Indianapolis sees Taylor as an important part of its future, so prying him loose could take a lot. And the Packers wouldn’t be alone. The Seahawks, Broncos, and Vikings were also mentioned as teams that have considered or might consider going after him. Imagine Taylor ending up in Minnesota. That’s a reason all by itself for Green Bay to at least make the call.

Now let’s talk about why Taylor is such a big deal. He’s twenty-seven years old. He’s proven. He’s explosive. He’s one of the most productive backs in the league, and he can carry a huge workload. When he’s rolling, he’s the kind of runner who can take over a game by himself. Break tackles. Hit the hole. Hit the home run.

And now imagine what that does to this offense. Jordan Love has been one of the best deep passers in football. Watson and Golden have been one of the best receiving duos in the league. Kraft is coming back to life. Savion Williams is emerging as a reliable target. The one thing missing? A consistent, dominant run game.

Put Jonathan Taylor in that backfield and suddenly defenses have no good answers. If they play two safeties deep to stop Watson and Golden, Taylor gashes them for six yards a carry all day long. If they bring a safety down to stop Taylor, Watson and Golden get one-on-one shots deep against a single high safety. And Jordan Love will make you pay. Play-action becomes deadly. Third downs become shorter. The offense stays on the field. The defense stays fresh. That’s how you build a balanced, complete, terrifying offense.

And think about what it does for the offensive line problem too. When defenses have to respect the run, pass rushers can’t just pin their ears back and tee off on Jordan Love every snap. Linebackers have to stay home. Play-action slows down the rush. A great running back can actually make an offensive line look better, by keeping the defense honest.

That’s the dream. Now let’s talk about the cost. Because this is where it gets complicated.

First, the money. According to the report, acquiring Taylor at the deadline would mean about five and a half million in cap charges for the rest of this season. Then his scheduled pay includes sixteen million next year and seventeen point nine million in 2028. He signed a two-year extension in the offseason. So this would not be a short-term rental. It’s a real financial commitment.

Second, Josh Jacobs. Green Bay already has Jacobs under contract, although reportedly he has no remaining guaranteed salary. If you bring in Taylor, the Packers would have to rethink how they’re spending money at running back. One idea raised in the report was including Jacobs in a trade package, if Indianapolis wanted him. Another would be moving on from Jacobs later. But neither of those scenarios is confirmed. Nothing has been reported about trading Jacobs.

Third, the draft picks. Remember, the Packers don’t have their 2027 first-round pick because of the Micah Parsons trade. So every pick they still own matters a lot. If the Colts see Taylor as part of their future, they’re going to want real compensation. That could mean a second-rounder or more.

And here’s an interesting piece of history. This isn’t the first time Green Bay has looked at Jonathan Taylor. Back in 2023, the Packers explored a trade for him, and the Colts reportedly wanted Christian Watson plus more. Green Bay said no. And honestly, looking at Watson now? Thank goodness they said no. But three years later, Taylor is back in the conversation. This time, a deal would probably have to be built around draft picks.

So where do I stand?

Here’s my honest take. I love the idea. I absolutely love it. Jonathan Taylor in green and gold, next to Jordan Love, with Watson, Golden, and Kraft? That’s the kind of offense that could make the whole NFC nervous. That’s the kind of offense that wins in January. And with Micah Parsons eventually coming back on defense, a move like this could make Green Bay one of the most complete teams in the league.

But I also have a real question. After watching the offensive line struggle against Detroit, would changing running backs actually fix the biggest problem? If the blocking isn’t there, even a superstar running back is going to have a hard time finding space. So I don’t want Brian Gutekunst throwing away major resources for Taylor without also making sure the offensive line is in a better place. Get the best five linemen on the field. Figure out the Zeitler situation. Then, if you can land Taylor at a fair price, go for it.

Because if both of those things happen, a fixed line and Jonathan Taylor? Look out, NFL.

Let me paint the picture one more time. It’s December. The Packers are hosting a playoff-caliber team at Lambeau. Snow on the ground. First drive of the game. Jonathan Taylor takes a handoff, cuts back, and rips off a twenty-yard run. The safety creeps up. Next play, play-action, Jordan Love fakes it to Taylor, and hits Christian Watson deep for a touchdown. Lambeau goes crazy. The defense has no idea what to do. That’s the vision. That’s the scariest offense in the NFL.

And honestly, after losing a game in Detroit by three points, I think this team is closer than it looks. Fix the kicking. Fix the line. Add one more weapon. And this is a team that can absolutely compete for the NFC North and beyond.

The trade deadline is November tenth. The clock is ticking. And the next two weeks could decide what this season becomes.
