And after the game, Drake Maye stood at the podium and did something that a lot of young quarterbacks would never do. He didn’t hide. He didn’t make excuses. He didn’t blame his receivers, his offensive line, or the coaching staff.

He looked straight at the cameras, took the blame, and made a decision. A decision about how he’s going to play from here on out. And it could change the entire second half of this season.

The Patriots are 5 and 4. They’re about to fly across the world to Munich, Germany, to face the Detroit Lions. And how Maye follows through on what he said could decide whether this season gets back on track, or keeps sliding.

Let’s start with how Maye felt. Because you could see it on his face.

He was asked how disappointing this performance was. And he didn’t sugarcoat it. He said he’s very disappointed in himself.

And then he explained why. He said you can’t put the team in a hole right off the bat. On the very first possession of the game, he threw an interception. And that put the defense in a bad spot from the opening drive.

And it didn’t stop there. Maye said that in the first half, and at times when the Patriots had chances to get back in the game, he messed them up. He said the Patriots had chances multiple times to get back within three points. And he couldn’t make it happen.

He said it was frustrating. And he put it on himself. He said he has to do a better job, from a huddle perspective, from a communication perspective, and with the little things. And he said that just wasn’t good on Sunday.

But then he said something important. He said the team has to bounce back. They’re about to get on a long flight to Germany. But there are still plenty of games left. And they have to keep battling.

That’s the mindset. Own it. Then move on.

Now let’s talk about the biggest issue. Turnovers.

A reporter pointed out that Maye talks about turnovers every single week. And he had them again on Sunday. Three interceptions.

And Maye admitted something that should get every Patriots fan’s attention. He said the team hasn’t had a clean, turnover-free game yet this season. Not one.

And he said it starts with him. He called turnovers one of the biggest differences between winning and losing football games. And on Sunday, he said, the first one put the defense in a bad spot right away, and gave Green Bay the football.

And he admitted he missed other chances down the field. He said he has to see the field in front of him better. He felt like he was a little late sometimes.

And then he explained how the game got away from them. He said they got into a throwing game, partly because of how things went offensively. They couldn’t build off the run game. And that made things harder.

And remember, they lost Rhamondre Stevenson to a knee injury in the third quarter of this game. With TreVeyon Henderson already out recovering from a serious health situation, the backfield was thin before Stevenson went down. And when you can’t run the ball, the whole offense falls on the quarterback’s shoulders.

But Maye didn’t use that as an excuse. He said the team, and him specifically, has to start games better.

That’s the first part of his decision. Start better. No more digging holes in the first quarter.

And then a reporter asked Maye to walk through all three interceptions. What he saw before the snap, and what he saw after. And this was one of the most honest breakdowns you’ll ever hear from a quarterback.

He said, simply, he threw the football into spots where he shouldn’t have thrown it.

On the first interception, he said he has to see the deep safeties better. He has to see where the safeties are positioned.

On the second one, he said maybe he needed to throw it a little further.

And on the third one, he said the Packers were jumping the crossing routes. So he has to see the defenders in front of him.

Think about that, Patriots Nation. He didn’t say the receiver ran the wrong route. He didn’t say the protection broke down. He said, those were my throws, and here’s exactly what I have to do better.

That’s a quarterback who studies his mistakes. And quarterbacks who study their mistakes get better.

And this matters, because we’ve seen what happens when Maye fixes his mistakes. Remember the start of this season? In his first three games, he threw one touchdown and six interceptions. His PFF passing grade through Week 3 was the lowest among 26 qualified quarterbacks.

And then he went into Buffalo and turned everything around. Three touchdowns, the dagger in the final two minutes, and a 29 to 26 win. Then 374 yards and five total touchdowns against the Raiders with zero interceptions. Then wins over the Jets and Miami. He put up at least three total touchdowns in five straight games. He was named AFC Offensive Player of the Month.

So we know what he’s capable of when he plays decisive, clean football. The question is whether he can get back there.

And Mike Vrabel said earlier this season what changed in Buffalo. One word. Decisiveness. Maye was out in front of things. If it was there, he let it go. If it wasn’t, he found somewhere else to go with the football.

That’s the version of Maye this team needs in Germany.

Then a reporter asked Maye about his health. Because he took some big hits against the Packers.

And Maye said, that’s football. Everybody’s taking hits out there. He said he feels good.

But then he turned the conversation toward the team. He said he knows this team is battling, with some guys not feeling great and a lot of injuries. And he knows those guys are trying to step up and get in there.

And he said the team has to band together. They have to be better throughout the week. He said it starts in practice. And he repeated something the coaches always say. Your best players have to play well. And on Sunday, he said, they didn’t.

Patriots Nation, that’s a leader talking. He includes himself in that group. The best players have to play well. And he knows he’s one of them.

Now, here’s where the conversation got really interesting. A reporter asked about the game plan.

The Packers play a lot of two-high safety looks. They play a ton of zone coverage. They feast on turnovers. And they try to take away big plays. Before the game, Vrabel himself said 17 percent of opponents’ drives against Green Bay end in a turnover. That leads the NFL.

So the question was, did the Patriots think they could beat the Packers with big plays, which is their strength? Or was the plan to take what Green Bay gave them?

And Maye gave a really thoughtful answer. He said it depends on the feel of the game. He said the Patriots knew the Packers were strong in the back end, especially at safety. He called their safeties ball-aware. And he said Green Bay has hunted a lot of turnovers by keeping their eyes on the quarterback in zone coverage.

And he said he knew that coming into the game. And then he forced the first one anyway. And later, the Packers jumped some crossing routes. And he said, again, that’s on him.

But he also pointed out the Patriots did hit some big plays in this game. So there’s some give and take. But he said the bad ones hurt a lot more than the good ones helped.

And that’s the second part of his decision. Make the right play, not the big play. Against defenses that hunt turnovers, the smart throw beats the hero throw.

And then came the biggest question of the press conference. Halfway through the season, who is this team?

And Maye took a deep breath. He said, we’ve been through a lot. Nine games in, and it’s crazy to think there’s still pretty much half the season left. He said there have been a lot of bumps and bruises. Some big wins. Some bad losses.

And then he said the key word. Consistent. He said the team has to find a way to be consistent. And he said the coach preaches that being consistent is the answer.

And he said when the team faces adversity, he has to do a better job handling it. And he said the biggest thing offensively is playing games from ahead.

Think about that. Playing from ahead. Against Green Bay, the Patriots were down 17 to nothing. When you’re behind like that, the defense knows you have to throw. They sit back. They wait for you to force it. And that’s exactly how turnovers happen.

When you play from ahead, you can run the ball. You can take what the defense gives you. The pressure is on the other team.

That’s the third part of his decision. Play from ahead. Start fast. Take control early.

And then Maye said something that every Patriots fan should hear. He said the team has a target on its back.

He said the Patriots won the AFC last season. They played in the Super Bowl. They had a top offense and a top defense. So every team is coming after them. Every week, they’re getting the other team’s best shot.

And he said the team has to respond.

Patriots Nation, that’s a quarterback who understands the moment. This isn’t last year. Nobody’s sneaking up on anybody. The Patriots are the hunted now. And they have to play like it.

And Maye shut that down. He said no. He said some players from the reunion were around at practice on Friday, and the guys said hello. But nothing was mandatory for the current players. He said the team was focused on the Packers, and he doesn’t think there’s any connection between the celebration and the result.

That’s the right answer. Because the problems on Sunday weren’t about distractions. They were about execution.

And finally, Maye was asked if it was demoralizing to be down 17 to nothing, watching Jordan Love light it up on the other side.

And Maye showed some real class. He said he’s gotten to know Jordan Love over the years, and he’s a big fan of his game. The way he throws the football. The way he can throw it downfield.

But he said he wasn’t really watching Love during the game. He was focused on what the Patriots’ offense could do to win. And he gave Green Bay credit. He said Love had a big day. And then he gave props to the Packers’ offense, defense, and special teams for the plan they had.

And then he said the line that sums up this whole loss. He said the Packers out-planned them, out-schemed them, and out-efforted them.

Out-efforted. That’s a strong word from a quarterback. And it lines up with what Mack Hollins said after the game, when he basically told the locker room that if anybody’s okay with not giving full effort, there are 31 other teams they can go play for.

So the message from the leaders on this team is the same. Effort has to be better. Execution has to be better. And it starts now.
