It all starts with the knee.

Rhamondre Stevenson got hurt with 3 minutes and 29 seconds left in the third quarter against Green Bay. And he never came back.

On Monday, Mike Vrabel didn’t have an update. In his words, the team has been wearing out the MRI machine. And the expectation now is that Stevenson will be out for multiple weeks. Exactly how many, nobody has said yet.

And this is a big loss. Stevenson is the Patriots’ leading rusher this season, with 399 yards. He just had one of his best games of the year in Miami, with 82 yards on 19 carries. And he’s been more involved as a receiver lately too. Over his last five games, he’s averaged 33.6 receiving yards per game, up from 22.5 before that. Remember his 48-yard touchdown catch in Chicago?

He’s paid like a lead back too. He’s on a three-year deal worth about 9.5 million dollars a year, with a 7.4-million-dollar cap hit this season. And his deal runs through 2028.

So losing him isn’t small. It’s a real hole.

Now let’s look at what’s left behind him. Because this is where the crisis really shows up.

Cory Ker is next among the running backs, with 244 rushing yards. Then TreVeyon Henderson, with 203. But Henderson is recovering from a frightening health situation. He spent several days in the hospital with unexplained neurological symptoms that affected his strength and coordination. His health comes first, and there’s no timeline for his return.

So put Ker and Henderson together, and they have 447 yards. Stevenson alone has 399. The Patriots are losing almost an entire backfield’s worth of production, at a time when they really can’t afford it.

And here’s another wrinkle. The Patriots’ third-leading rusher this season? It’s the quarterback. Drake Maye has 219 rushing yards and three rushing touchdowns. That’s more than Henderson’s 203.

That tells you how much this ground game has leaned on players who aren’t named Rhamondre Stevenson. And it tells you that without Stevenson, defenses might start keying even more on Maye.

There’s also Lan Larison, who was signed to the 53-man roster before the Miami game. A guy who ran for over 3,600 yards and 41 touchdowns in college. But he’s barely played in the NFL.

So for a team that’s 5 and 4, two games behind Buffalo in the AFC East, the big problem is simple. They haven’t figured out who’s going to carry the ball next week, in Munich, against the Detroit Lions.

And right when the Patriots lost their lead back, one of the best running backs in football, on a team that just lost its fifth straight game, got linked to New England.

So who exactly are they chasing?

Jonathan Taylor has 656 rushing yards this season. That’s 72.9 yards per game. And he has eight rushing touchdowns.

He’s catching the ball more too. Over his last five games, he’s averaging 30.2 receiving yards per game, up from 17.2 before that.

The one blemish? Two lost fumbles. And for a Patriots team that’s struggled with turnovers all season, that’s something to watch.

But overall, this is a 27-year-old, three-time Pro Bowler. Last season, he ran for 1,585 yards and led the NFL with 18 rushing touchdowns. He’s one of the most dangerous backs in the league.

And the Colts are struggling badly. They just lost their fifth straight game. They fired their general manager, Chris Ballard. And they have about 25 million dollars in cap space, so they’re not forced to sell. If they trade Taylor, it’ll be because they chose to.

One Colts writer, James Boyd, put the big question for Indianapolis plainly. What matters is whether Josh Downs and Jonathan Taylor are still on the team after the deadline.

And listen to what Taylor said after Sunday’s game. He said you play every game like it’s your last. But at the end of the day, Indianapolis has been the place where he grew up.

You can hear it in his words. He doesn’t sound like a man who knows exactly what’s going to happen in the next couple of days.

Now, let’s look at the board. Because the market has moved fast.

In that prediction market, the Patriots are now at about 34 to 35 percent. The Colts keeping him is at 28 percent. The Giants are at 17 percent. And the Broncos are at 9 percent. More than 3 million dollars has been traded on it.

Now, remember what 34 percent means. It means the market thinks this happens about one time in three. So even the favorite can lose. This is a market price, not a report. But the movement tells you something. On October 31st, New England was at 5 percent. Now they’re at the top.

And here’s what we actually know from reporters.

The Colts are open to dealing Taylor. They’re active in ongoing talks. They immediately rejected an offer of a second-round pick plus another pick. And they want a second-round pick, and possibly more.

According to Adam Schefter, the Giants have reportedly offered a second-round pick, a fifth-round pick, and an undisclosed player.

And Mike Florio said the current belief around the league is that the Colts will take the best offer on the table as the deadline gets closer.

The Giants actually led this market on November 4th, at 31 percent. Now they’re down to 17. And Giants reporter Jordan Raanan said it would be a surprise at this point if Taylor goes to New York. He said an interior defensive lineman is the more likely move for the Giants.

So think about what the Colts have already turned down. A second plus another pick. And the Giants are reportedly at a second, a fifth, and a player. So the real price is somewhere above that.

And that’s where New England has something other teams might not. The Patriots still hold their own 2027 first-round pick. And a first-round pick would beat everything we’ve just talked about.

And there’s more. On October 29th, there was a report that the Patriots had recently called the Colts about Taylor, with TreVeyon Henderson and draft picks possibly part of the conversation. Now, to be clear, nothing about that has been confirmed. And with Henderson’s health situation, that idea looks very different now.

But if the Patriots did call, the question becomes, can they actually afford him?

Let’s look at the money.

Taylor’s cap hit is 16.2 million dollars this season. Then 21 million next season. And 23 million in 2028.

So next year alone, he’d cost more than double what Stevenson does.

Right now, the Patriots have about 6.1 million dollars in cap space, before any restructures. And they have more than 42 million dollars of their cap tied up in dead money.

Where does that dead money come from? Players who aren’t even here anymore. The Kyle Dugger trade left 12.2 million. Cutting Stefon Diggs left 9.7 million. And two other departed players left about 3.5 and 3 million each.

That’s a lot of money going to players who aren’t on the roster.

But here’s the thing. The Boston Globe says the Patriots could easily create about 25 million dollars more in space, with some contract restructures.

Think of a restructure like paying off one credit card with another. You move the money into later years. The space shows up today, and the bill comes due in 2027 and 2028. NFL teams do it all the time. It’s how the Patriots created room by restructuring Hunter Henry’s contract earlier this season.

So the money is possible. It would just take a couple of moves.

And then there are the draft picks.

The Patriots don’t have their 2028 first-round pick. That went to Philadelphia in the A.J. Brown trade back in June. But they still own their 2027 and 2029 first-rounders.

And here’s the thing about picks. A team doesn’t give up a first-round pick for a running back on a hunch. It does it when the hole is real, the player is proven, and the clock is running.

The hole is real. Stevenson is hurt. Henderson is out. The player is proven. Taylor has 656 yards and eight touchdowns this season, and he’s a former rushing champion. And the clock runs out today at 4 p.m.

So all the pieces fit. And it would be a very New England kind of move. This front office already traded a first-round pick for A.J. Brown in June. It’s shown it will pay for a star when it decides it needs one.

And Mike Vrabel said last week that the team will do everything it can to help, within reason, and with what makes sense for them. A star running back, with the starter stuck in an MRI tube, certainly makes sense.

But it’s not that simple. And I want to be fair.

Earlier this season, I had doubts about trading big for Taylor. Because the biggest problem for this offense has been the offensive line. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker is on injured reserve. Ben Brown has gone down. Backup guards have been starting. And Taylor can’t make a guard block better just by showing up.

Those concerns are still real. And there are cheaper options out there too. AtoZ Sports reported interest in Derrick Henry, who played for Vrabel in Tennessee, and who would probably cost around a third-round pick, with only about 600,000 dollars left on his salary.

But with Stevenson now hurt for multiple weeks, the backfield has gone from a weakness to a crisis. And that changes the math.

So here’s what could happen before 4 p.m. today. Three scenarios.

Scenario one. The Giants, or another team, win out. Taylor goes somewhere else. And the Patriots are left with Cory Ker, Lan Larison, a hopeful return for Henderson whenever he’s healthy, and Stevenson out for a few weeks. They might still pivot to a cheaper back like Derrick Henry.

Scenario two. The Colts decide to keep Taylor. Nothing changes except the market price. And the Patriots have to figure out the backfield from within, or find another option.

And scenario three. New England pays up. A first-round pick, maybe more. And the backfield becomes the biggest story in the NFL.

If it’s scenario three, and a first-round pick is how they win it, get ready. Because the reactions are going to be mixed. Some fans will love the aggressiveness. Others will worry about the long-term cost.

And the timing matters. The Patriots are 5 and 4, two games behind Buffalo. The Lions are waiting in Munich, Germany, on November 15th. And after that, the Patriots have their bye week.

So it’s a short window to find out how long Stevenson will be gone. And a short window to fix it.
