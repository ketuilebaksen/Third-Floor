This wasn’t just another tough loss. This was the kind of performance that makes you stop and question everything about a team that was supposed to be fighting for a championship. Eight sacks. Four turnovers. Dropped passes. Botched snaps. A missed field goal. And a head coach who walked up to the podium and admitted his team got humbled.

And now, for the first time this season, there’s a question being asked out loud around Green Bay. Is Matt LaFleur still the right man for this job?

Let’s start with the result. Green Bay fell thirty to ten to Minnesota at Lambeau Field, dropping to six and four. And the Vikings have now beaten the Packers twice this season.

Remember the first one back in September? The Packers had a lead in Minnesota, and then the Vikings took over late in the game, scoring point after point in the fourth quarter to win by seventeen. That loss stuck with this team. And Sunday’s rematch was supposed to show that Green Bay had learned something.

Instead, the offense collapsed again.

In his winners and losers breakdown for SB Nation, Sam Calio couldn’t name a single winner for the Packers. Not one. That’s his editorial opinion, but after this performance, you can understand why.

So let’s go through the losers. And it starts with someone who had a historic game just one week earlier.

Matthew Golden.

Now, to be fair, his afternoon wasn’t a total loss. He actually led the Packers with five catches for seventy yards on nine targets. But Calio believed he saw at least three drops, while admitting the official count might be different. And two of them came back to back. On second and six, Golden let a potential first down slip right through his hands. And on the very next play, another possible conversion ended when he couldn’t finish the catch.

And that’s been the story with Golden all season. The talent is undeniable. One week earlier, he had six catches for one hundred twenty-six yards in the first quarter alone against New England. The most by a Packer in an opening quarter since Antonio Freeman in 1998. He and Christian Watson made franchise history together. But he came into that Patriots game leading the entire NFL in drops. And against Minnesota, those drops came back at the worst possible time.

Next, Tucker Kraft. And this one was concerning.

Jordan Love targeted Kraft four times. And Kraft caught just one of them, for nineteen yards. Calio questioned his energy and his body language on the field. And he wondered whether confidence might be an issue, or whether there could be lingering effects from last year’s torn ACL. Now, those are the writer’s impressions, not confirmed explanations. But the production speaks for itself.

Remember what LaFleur said earlier this season about Kraft? He said players coming back from major injuries need time. That we all want instant gratification, but it takes a period of time to get back to who you were. And Kraft looked like he was rounding into form against Detroit, with four catches for forty-eight yards and a touchdown. But against Minnesota, Green Bay needed more from a tight end who’s normally so good at creating yards after contact and giving Love a reliable option. And it didn’t get it.

Then there’s Trey Smack. Calio criticized the kicker after a missed fifty-three yard field goal. And look, a fifty-three yarder isn’t an easy kick. But in a game where points were at a premium, against what LaFleur called maybe the best defense in the NFL, you need those points. And it’s another miss in a season full of them. After going seven for seven against New England, Smack just can’t seem to put together consistent games. LaFleur said after the game he still has a lot of confidence in him. But that confidence is being tested.

Mark Redman also made the losers list. According to Calio, he fumbled the Packers’ very first completed pass of the game. LaFleur mentioned it in his press conference too. He said the team fumbled on the second play of the game, and it set the Vikings up for three points right away. That’s no way to start a division game.

But the biggest failure of all was up front.

Minnesota sacked Jordan Love eight times. Eight. That’s not one bad block. That’s not one blown assignment. That’s a complete protection breakdown that affects every single part of the offense.

And Calio singled out center John Williams for multiple botched snaps. One of them came on Green Bay’s opening drive of the second half. And Minnesota recovered the football.

Now, let’s be fair to John Williams. He’s a guard. He got thrown into emergency center duty when both Sean Rhyan and Jacob Monk went down. LaFleur praised him for how he handled it at first. He said it was impressive to go in there for the first time and play the way he did. But against the most aggressive blitz scheme in the league, playing a position you’ve barely played in your career, the cracks started to show.

And then there’s the coaching staff. And this is where it gets uncomfortable.

Matt LaFleur, offensive coordinator Adam Stenavich, and offensive line coach Luke Butkus had already seen this Vikings defense once this season. They knew Minnesota would bring relentless pressure. They knew Brian Flores would fire blitzers from every angle. And yet, Green Bay still couldn’t protect Jordan Love.

LaFleur even admitted it after the game. He said it was obviously a bad decision not to go to the quick passing game earlier, because his quarterback was getting abused, just like he was in the first game. He said he was racking his brain during the game trying to find anything that would get the ball out of Jordan’s hands in time. And he said, plainly, that the team didn’t coach well enough.

And that’s what makes this loss feel different. It wasn’t just bad luck. It was a problem the staff had already seen, and still couldn’t solve.

Add it all up, and the Packers turned the ball over four times. Against a Vikings team that came in six and oh in turnover margin. Jordan Love said the team stressed all week that it couldn’t lose the turnover battle. And it lost it four to one.

Now, the good news. Because there is some. At six and four, Green Bay still has a real path into the NFC North race and the Wild Card picture. There are seven games left. And LaFleur said it himself. The season isn’t over. There’s a lot of football in front of this team.

And the next test comes fast. After a short break, the Packers face the Los Angeles Rams on Wednesday, November twenty-fifth. Thanksgiving Eve. An eight o’clock Eastern kickoff on Netflix. A prime-time stage. A chance to show the whole country that this loss was a wake-up call, not the start of a collapse.

And that date matters for another reason. That’s the day Micah Parsons’ practice window closes. The Packers have to activate him by the end of that window. Now, his availability for the Rams game hasn’t been confirmed. But the clock is ticking. And if he plays, it could be the boost this team desperately needs.

Now, let’s get to the second story. The bigger one. Matt LaFleur’s future.

There’s been a report arguing that the Vikings loss has intensified the scrutiny on LaFleur’s leadership. Green Bay came into this season with championship expectations. And now, after this loss, the Packers are sitting at the bottom of the NFC North. Eight sacks and four turnovers are tough numbers for any coaching staff to explain. Especially against an opponent you’ve already faced.

But let me say it again, because it’s important. LaFleur has not been fired. There’s no confirmed coaching search. No reported interview with a replacement. No evidence of any negotiations. Everything from here on is a discussion of possible future choices. Not an announcement that the Packers are making a change.

And there are good reasons to believe LaFleur is going to get time to turn this around.

First, according to the report, LaFleur and general manager Brian Gutekunst both received multi-year contract extensions in January of 2026. That’s a big commitment from the organization. Teams don’t usually extend a head coach and then fire him ten months later.

Second, the Packers have a long history of avoiding mid-season head coaching changes. This is a franchise that values stability. That values patience. We just watched Gutekunst sit out the trade deadline completely because he believes in the long-term plan. That same patience applies to the head coach.

And third, think about what LaFleur has done this season. He led this team through an incredible amount of adversity. Jayden Reed lost for the year. Edgerrin Cooper and Javon Hargrave lost in back-to-back weeks. Micah Parsons out the whole season. Josh Jacobs out for six games, then hurt again. The offensive line reshuffled over and over. And this team still went five and one over a six-game stretch, including a forty-five point performance in New England. And he hired Jonathan Gannon, who’s turned this defense into one of the best in the league. That’s not a coach who’s lost his team.

So the bigger question isn’t about this week. It’s about what happens if Green Bay misses the playoffs, or suffers another early exit in the postseason. That’s when the pressure would really be on.

So let’s talk about the names. These are five possibilities that were floated in that report. None of them are confirmed targets. None of them have been contacted. This is pure speculation about what a future search could look like, if it ever happened.

Name number one. Brian Flores.

The report identifies him as Minnesota’s defensive coordinator. And Packers fans just got a very up-close look at what he can do. His aggressive, pressure-heavy defense just gave Green Bay enormous trouble. Eight sacks. Four takeaways. LaFleur himself praised Flores after the game, saying you can tell he does a great job with that unit, with all those dual-call plays and different looks. He’s been a head coach before. And he clearly knows how to build a defense that terrorizes quarterbacks. But again, there’s no confirmed Packers contact with Flores.

Name number two. Marcus Freeman.

Notre Dame’s head coach. His appeal is all about leadership, accountability, and the culture he’s built in college football. If Green Bay ever wanted a fresh voice in the locker room, that profile could be attractive. But the report notes that he’s never been an NFL head coach. So he’s a speculative option. Not a known target.

Name number three. Nathan Scheelhaase.

The report identifies him as the Los Angeles Rams’ offensive coordinator. And that’s interesting for two reasons. One, he comes from Sean McVay’s coaching tree, which is the same tree Matt LaFleur came from. LaFleur worked under McVay with the Rams before coming to Green Bay. So the offensive philosophy would feel familiar. And two, the Packers are about to play the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve. So Packers fans will get a look at his offense up close.

Name number four. Sean McDermott.

According to the report, he’s away from coaching in 2026 after leaving Buffalo. He built a long run of winning seasons with the Bills. He’d bring a ton of head coaching experience, and a defensive background. Again, no reported contact.

And name number five. Klayton Adams.

Identified in the report as the Dallas Cowboys’ offensive coordinator. And his background might connect most directly to Green Bay’s biggest problem on Sunday. Adams has a background in offensive line development and the running game. Think about that. The two things that have hurt the Packers most all season. The offensive line and the run game. A coach who specializes in exactly those areas.

So those are five very different ideas. Flores and defensive aggression. Freeman and culture. Scheelhaase and offensive innovation. McDermott and experience. Adams and toughness in the trenches.

But here’s the central issue behind every single one of those names. Jordan Love.

Whoever coaches this team has to build around Jordan Love. A franchise quarterback making fifty-five million dollars a year. A quarterback who was in the MVP conversation just one week ago. A quarterback whose growth LaFleur has guided for six years, from sitting behind Aaron Rodgers to throwing his one hundredth career touchdown pass. A quarterback LaFleur said has stayed the same guy every single day.

A defensive specialist would need the right offensive partner to develop Jordan. A college coach would need an NFL-ready staff around him. A young coordinator would have to prove he can lead an entire team. And even an experienced coach would have to build a system that fits this roster.

Those are exactly the reasons why a coaching change is never as simple as picking the most exciting name on a list. Changing coaches means changing systems. It means changing terminology. It means starting over in a lot of ways. And with a quarterback like Jordan Love in his prime, starting over carries real risk.

So here’s where I land, Packers Nation.

This isn’t about demanding that LaFleur be fired after one ugly Sunday. That would be an overreaction. This team is six and four with a lot of talent. It just went five and one over six games. It’s got a franchise quarterback, two dynamic receivers, an elite defensive coordinator, and Micah Parsons about to return.

But it is fair to ask whether this coaching staff can fix the mistakes we’ve now seen more than once. Because the problems against Minnesota weren’t new. The pass protection breakdowns. The turnovers. The drops. The kicking. The slow adjustment to the blitz. We’ve seen all of these before this season.

So here’s what LaFleur and his staff have to show over these final seven games.

Can the offensive line protect Jordan Love? Can they settle on the best five and give him time?

Can Matthew Golden and Tucker Kraft turn their opportunities into consistent production?

Can this team stop handing away possessions?

Can the coaching staff adjust faster when a defense brings heavy pressure? Get to the quick game from the very first drive?

And can LaFleur deliver a convincing response against the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve, in front of a national audience?

If the answers are yes, this conversation goes away fast. If the answers are no, and this season ends in disappointment, then the pressure is going to be real. And that’s when these names might start coming up for real.

I’m not giving up on a six and four team with this much talent. But belief isn’t enough. The Packers have to show discipline. They have to show preparation. And they have to show an offense that can protect its quarterback.

The Rams game is their next chance to prove this disaster was a turning point. Not the start of a collapse.
