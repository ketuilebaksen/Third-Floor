“This is a humbling league. And today, we got humbled.”

Packers Nation, those were the very first words out of Matt LaFleur’s mouth after the loss to the Minnesota Vikings at Lambeau Field. And honestly, that tells you everything about how this one went.

One week after scoring forty-five points in New England. One week after a locker room full of game balls and celebration. One week after national analysts were warning the whole NFC to beware of the Packers. And Green Bay came home and got knocked down. Hard.

Four turnovers. Eight sacks on Jordan Love. A lineup decision on the offensive line that raised eyebrows. And a moment in his press conference where LaFleur did something you don’t see very often from a head coach. He flat-out called one of his own decisions a bad decision.

Welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’re going through every single thing LaFleur and Jordan Love said after this loss. What went wrong. Why it went wrong. The decisions that backfired. And whether this team can bounce back. Because the season isn’t over. Not even close. Let’s get into it.

Before we start, smash that like button, subscribe to Third Floor, and turn on notifications. Through the wins and the tough losses, we stick together. And drop a comment right now: what was the most frustrating part of this game for you?

Let’s start with the big picture. LaFleur gave the Vikings a ton of credit. He said they’re a really good football team. And then he got right to the heart of it. He said anytime you spot them and turn the ball over four times to their one, that’s a recipe for disaster.

Four turnovers to one. Packers Nation, you know how this team has been winning all season. The defense taking the ball away. Five takeaways against the Bears. Four against the Patriots. Winning the turnover battle has been this team’s identity. And today, the script got completely flipped.

And LaFleur didn’t make excuses. He said, obviously, we didn’t coach well enough, and we certainly didn’t go out there and execute well enough. Both. Coaching and execution. He put it on himself, and he put it on the players.

Now let’s talk about the biggest problem. Pass protection. Because Jordan Love got sacked eight times.

Eight sacks. Let that sink in.

LaFleur was asked why the pass protection was so bad. And he said he has to look at the tape, but he knows there were a couple of busts. He said there was one play where the Packers just didn’t block Andrew Van Ginkel off the edge. And he pointed out that Van Ginkel is a known big. He’s not some surprise rusher. He’s a guy you know you have to account for. And the Packers left him unblocked.

LaFleur said the Vikings do a good job with what they do. They’re going to fire guys at you from every angle. And he said the Packers didn’t have their players coached up well enough.

And then a reporter asked the big question. Is this offensive line, whether it’s run blocking or pass protection, sustainable enough to get where this team wants to go?

LaFleur didn’t give a confident yes. He said, we’ll see. He said it’s a week-to-week league, and the team has to focus on the things it can control. And he said there has to be urgency to improve in those areas.

That’s not exactly a ringing endorsement. And honestly, it’s an honest answer. Because after eight sacks, you can’t stand up there and say everything’s fine.

But here’s what really stood out. A reporter pointed out that even on plays when the Vikings didn’t blitz, the Packers looked out of sync. And asked if just the fear of the blitz was a problem in itself.

And LaFleur gave one of the most candid answers I’ve ever heard from him. He said, I’ll be honest with you, I was trying to rack my brain during the game, trying to call anything that could get the ball out of Jordan’s hands in a timely fashion.

Think about that. The head coach, in the middle of the game, searching for anything that would work. That’s how much pressure Minnesota was bringing.

And LaFleur admitted some of the problems were on the play design itself. Some of the throws were longer-developing. And against a defense blitzing like that, longer-developing throws are a death sentence.

He explained what the Vikings were doing on the back end too. He said they were trying to keep an umbrella on the defense. Keep everything in front of them. And they were playing hard outside leverage on the Packers’ wide receivers. Almost like a double team on those guys on the outside. So the deep shots to Christian Watson and Matthew Golden that worked so well against New England? Minnesota took them away. And LaFleur said the Packers didn’t find the holes inside.

And now we get to the moment I mentioned at the top. The bad decision.

A reporter asked why the Packers didn’t go to the quick passing game until the second half. Quick throws. Get the ball out fast. Beat the blitz with timing.

And LaFleur said, it’s obviously a bad decision. Because our quarterback was getting abused, just like he did in the first game.

Packers Nation, that’s a head coach publicly owning his mistake. He said the team had called some of that quick game early, but it wasn’t really successful until the second half. But he didn’t hide behind that. He called it what it was. A bad decision.

And he brought up the first game against Minnesota. Remember week one? Green Bay led twenty-two to ten, and then the Vikings scored twenty-nine unanswered points. And LaFleur said Jordan was getting abused in that game too. So this is the second time this season the Vikings’ pressure has wrecked this offense. And that’s something LaFleur and his staff have to solve, because the Packers will likely have to face this kind of pressure again.

Jordan Love agreed. He was asked if he would’ve preferred to go to the quick game earlier. And he said, definitely. He said it’s something they’ll look at. He pointed out they had some success in the second half when they started getting the ball out quicker. And he said, when you’re facing a defense that’s pressuring that way, you’ve got to be able to get the ball out quick, have those answers, and find the holes in the defense. Because there are holes out there when a defense is bringing that kind of pressure.

And then Jordan said something that really tells you how tough this game was. He said, you don’t stand a chance sometimes when you’re getting pressure and the routes are still developing.

That’s exactly it. You can be the best quarterback in the world. But if a free rusher is in your face before your receiver is out of his break, there’s nothing you can do.

Now let’s talk about the decision that a lot of Packers fans are going to debate all week. The penalty decision.

There was a third down where the Vikings blitzed, and there was a facemask penalty. The Packers had a choice. Take the result of the play and kick a field goal. Or accept the fifteen-yard penalty and push the Vikings back.

LaFleur explained his thinking. He said if it had been a five or ten-yard penalty, he probably would have declined it. But fifteen yards would have made it a fifty-six yard field goal attempt. So he said, you’ve got to roll the dice there.

And LaFleur admitted it was a pivotal play in the game. Because after the penalty, the Packers had the Vikings in third and twenty-three. Third and twenty-three! And LaFleur said the odds of converting that are very, very low. But Minnesota converted it. And they made the Packers pay.

That’s a gut punch. You make an aggressive decision, you put your opponent in an almost impossible spot, and they convert anyway. And it wasn’t the only one. LaFleur said there were numerous third and ten-plus situations that the Vikings converted. Numerous. That’s a problem for a defense that had been so good on third down. Against Carolina, the Panthers went five for eighteen on third down. Today, Minnesota kept moving the chains in long-yardage situations.

And a big reason for that? Kirk Cousins.

LaFleur was asked about Cousins, who the Vikings acquired from the Raiders right at the trade deadline and named their starter for this game. And LaFleur called him a quarterback who has seen it all. He said the Vikings were much more efficient offensively with him, and they hit the Packers in some of those critical situations.

Think about how fast that turnaround was. Cousins arrived on Tuesday. He practiced for the first time on Thursday. And on Sunday, he walked into Lambeau Field and beat the Packers by converting third and longs. That’s a veteran quarterback who knows exactly how to win these games.

Now let’s talk about the turnovers. Because this is what really cost the Packers.

LaFleur said turnovers can’t happen. And he pointed to how the game started. On the second play of the game, the Packers fumbled the football. And that set the Vikings up. LaFleur said that was giving them three points right away.

And he said the defense did a pretty good job of responding to those difficult situations. But you can’t give the ball away, whether you’re deep in your own territory or in scoring position. And the Packers had turnovers in critical spots, including when they were in Minnesota territory and had a chance to put points on the board.

And LaFleur said the team knew points were probably going to be at a premium. Because he called the Vikings one of the best defenses, if not the best defense, in the National Football League right now. So you can’t take yourself out of scoring opportunities. And that’s exactly what the Packers did.

And here’s the frustrating part. The team knew this was coming. Jordan Love told reporters that the Packers stressed not losing the turnover margin all week, because Minnesota was six and oh in that category coming into the game. They knew the Vikings were great at forcing turnovers. They talked about it. They prepared for it. And it still happened.

LaFleur said a lot of it is execution. But he also said you have to be mindful of the ball when you’re carrying it. You can’t cough it up or let somebody punch it out. He said the Vikings do a good job at doing that, and the Packers let them do it.

And with Josh Jacobs out after finger surgery, the young running backs were carrying the football against one of the most aggressive defenses in the league. And ball security has been a question for some of them all season. That’s a lesson the Packers have to learn fast.

A reporter also asked if, after dominating New England, anyone in the building was feeling themselves a little too much. And LaFleur shut that down. He said no, he didn’t feel that at all. He said this is a week-to-week league, and the Vikings came in at six and two as one of the better teams in the NFL. He said the team definitely wasn’t taking them lightly. And he brought up the week one game again. He said they knew what happened last time, when they were up twenty-two to ten and the game got away from them. And then he said, unfortunately, it’s the same story today.

The same story. Against the same team. That’s going to sting for a while.

Now let’s talk about the lineup decision. Because a lot of people noticed that Aaron Banks didn’t start.

LaFleur was asked about it. And he said they’re going to shuffle up the offensive line a little bit. He said Laken Tomlinson has been doing a pretty good job, so they put him back at his natural position, left guard. And they’re going to let Banks compete at right guard.

Now here’s the catch. LaFleur admitted Banks doesn’t have a ton of time at right guard. He’s a career left guard. So that’s a real adjustment.

And with Sean Rhyan out at center, Kevin Zeitler banged up with ankle and triceps injuries, Travis Glover dealing with a knee injury, and John Williams snapping the ball, this line has been reshuffled over and over all season. A reporter asked if all that shuffling contributed to the mistakes up front. And LaFleur said, we could make that excuse, but no.

No excuses. I respect that. But it’s hard to ignore that this offensive line has been a revolving door, and against a defense like Minnesota’s, communication and continuity matter more than ever.

And then there’s Trey Smack. He had another miss today. After going seven for seven against New England, he missed again in a game where points were at a premium. LaFleur was asked where his confidence in Smack stands. And he said he still has a lot of confidence in him. But he added that, especially in a game like this, where points are scarce, you have to convert in those situations.

And then the question everybody was thinking. How frustrated was Jordan with the eight sacks?

And LaFleur said, I think we’re all frustrated.

Now let’s hear from Jordan Love himself. Because he handled this loss like a leader.

He was asked how it felt back there with that much pressure. And he was honest. He said it wasn’t great. He said they weren’t good enough up front, and they weren’t good enough at getting the ball out. And when you do those things, you’re going to take some hits. He said they knew what this Vikings defense brings, and they weren’t good enough against it.

Notice what he did there. He included himself. Not just the line. Himself too. Not good enough at getting the ball out. That’s a franchise quarterback taking responsibility.

He explained what makes Minnesota’s blitz scheme so tough. He said the Vikings bring a lot of different personnel groupings. A lot of different looks up front. So the challenge is identifying who you’re sliding the protection to, and where the big guys are lined up. He said they mix it up. It’s a very unique defense. They line up in fronts and looks you’d normally see from a different personnel group, and that throws you off. They bring internal pressure. They drop the defensive end into coverage. And you have to be great with communication up front.

And then he said something important. He said it’s not like the Vikings did anything new that the Packers hadn’t seen on film. So you’ve got to have a plan for it and execute against it. And he said, we just didn’t execute our plan well enough tonight.

LaFleur echoed that. He praised Minnesota’s defensive coordinator, Brian Flores. He said the Vikings’ approach is ultra-aggressive, they’ve got a lot of smart players, and you can tell Flores does a great job with them. He said they have a lot of dual-call plays, where they adjust based on whether you’re under center or in the gun, in a two-by-two or a three-by-one set. And he said they were getting into the right looks and making the Packers pay.

And when Jordan was asked how banged up he was after taking eight sacks, he said he’s all right. He took some shots for sure, but he’ll be all right. There was one play in the third quarter when he got stepped on pretty good, and he said it was just a bruise. He’ll be fine. That’s a relief, because the Packers absolutely cannot afford to lose Jordan Love.

So where do the Packers go from here?

Now they’re six and four. And in the NFC North, where every team is winning, that’s a dangerous spot. The Bears, Lions, and Vikings are all right there. Every division game is a playoff game, and the Packers just lost one at home.

But LaFleur made it clear. The season isn’t over. He said there’s a lot of football left. Seven more guaranteed games. And he said it’s one week at a time. It’s staying together. It’s trying to learn. He said there’s a lot to learn from a game where you get beaten like that. And the team has to regroup and stay together.

And honestly, that’s the right message. Because this team has already shown it can bounce back. Remember the start of this season? Two and two. Written off. People saying the offense was broken. And then this team ripped off three straight wins. Then it lost a tough one in Detroit and came right back to beat Carolina and destroy New England. This team has responded to adversity all season.

And there’s help coming. Micah Parsons is getting closer. He has to be activated by the end of his practice window on November twenty-fifth, which is the Rams game on Thanksgiving Eve. Josh Jacobs could be back in two to three weeks after his surgery. Zaire Franklin should be back soon too. And Sean Rhyan is working his way back at center.

So here’s what has to happen. The offensive line has to settle down and figure out its best five. The coaching staff has to have the quick game ready from the very first drive against blitz-heavy teams. The running backs have to hold onto the football. Trey Smack has to make his kicks. And the defense has to get off the field on third and long.

None of that is impossible. All of it is fixable.

This was a humbling loss. LaFleur said it himself. But humbling losses can also be the ones that wake a team up. The best teams in the NFL take a punch like this, learn from it, and come back stronger.

And with Jordan Love playing at the level he’s been playing at most of this season, with Watson and Golden on the outside, with Jonathan Gannon’s defense, and with Micah Parsons on the way, this team still has everything it needs to make a serious run.
