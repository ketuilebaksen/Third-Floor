Packers Nation, it’s a short week, the bumps and bruises are piling up, and Matt LaFleur just stepped up to the podium with some news you need to hear. A starting offensive lineman is banged up. A key linebacker is out. A thirty-six-year-old veteran might be about to step into the lineup for the first time this season. And then, at the very end, LaFleur said something about Micah Parsons that was different from anything he’s said all year.

Welcome back to Third Floor, your home for everything Green Bay. We’ve got the Carolina Panthers coming to town on Thursday, and after that frustrating twenty to seventeen loss in Detroit, this team has to bounce back fast. So today we’re going through everything LaFleur said. The injuries, the lineup decisions, Josh Jacobs, the rookie cornerback, Trey Smack, Savion Williams, Bryce Young, the officiating, and of course, the Micah Parsons update that got everybody talking. Let’s get into it.

Before we start, smash that like button, subscribe to Third Floor, and turn on notifications. And drop a comment right now: what’s the single biggest thing this team has to fix against Carolina? I want to hear it.

Let’s start with the big blow. Travis Glover.

Glover played every single snap at right guard against Detroit. And it wasn’t until the next morning that LaFleur found out Glover had hurt his knee. Now he hasn’t been able to practice. And LaFleur was honest about it. He said anytime a guy can’t practice, you’re worried about it. But that’s life on a short week, and both teams are dealing with the same thing.

He said he sure hopes Glover can go, but they’re still working through it. And on a short week, there really aren’t any real reps. Everything is pretty much a walkthrough. The team was set to have a short individual period to move guys around and see how they look. But no promises.

And that brings us straight to the decision everybody’s been waiting on. Kevin Zeitler.

We’ve asked about Zeitler for weeks. He hasn’t played yet this season. And he was a healthy scratch in Detroit, which a lot of fans didn’t understand after watching the line struggle. So the question came right away: if Glover isn’t ready for Thursday, is Zeitler the guy?

And LaFleur’s answer? Three words.

You shall see.

Love it. Classic LaFleur. He’s not giving away anything before kickoff. But then he was pressed a little more, asked if Zeitler is ready to go if needed. And LaFleur said absolutely. He said he feels confident with him.

That’s a big statement. Because think about Zeitler’s situation. He’s thirty-six years old. He came into the building without an offseason program. Without a training camp. He’s been gradually working his way back into football shape. LaFleur said he’s done a nice job. And when he was asked if part of the plan was to save Zeitler for the stretch run instead of rushing him back, LaFleur said they always knew it would be fluid. When you bring in a new guy, you have to let it happen naturally. And when he’s ready to roll, he’ll be out there.

Well, Packers Nation, if Glover can’t go, “ready to roll” might be right now. And honestly? This could be a blessing in disguise. A veteran with that much experience, that much mileage, a guy LaFleur said a few weeks ago is wired the right way and is everything you want in a player. Stepping in against a Carolina defense that loves to keep a lid on you and make you earn everything? That might be exactly what this line needs.

And LaFleur also gave some love to Mekhi Becton, who’s now on the fifty-three-man roster. When he was asked if Becton could step in and help, LaFleur said he absolutely could. He said Becton has done a nice job learning the offense, practicing hard, and doing good work. So there’s depth here. There are veteran options. The line isn’t out of answers.

Now the other big blow. Zaire Franklin. When LaFleur was asked if Franklin was going to be out again or if he had a chance to return soon, the answer was short. He’ll be out.

That hurts. Franklin has been a big part of this defense. But here’s the good news. Isaiah McDuffie wore the green dot against Detroit, meaning he was the one calling the defense on the field. And LaFleur said McDuffie did a great job. A really good job. That’s huge. When your backup linebacker steps in, wears the green dot, and the head coach praises him publicly, that tells you there’s trust there. McDuffie has to carry that again against Carolina.

There’s also another player whose status for IR is still being figured out. LaFleur said they still have some time before they determine that. So we’ll keep an eye on it.

Alright, let’s talk about Josh Jacobs. Because this was his first game back, and everybody wanted to know how it looked.

LaFleur said he was happy with Jacobs’ performance. He said Jacobs is just getting back into it. And he loved what Jacobs brought. Physicality. Toughness. He said Jacobs brings an edge to the whole team when he’s around the guys. That’s the kind of thing that doesn’t always show up in the box score but matters a ton in the locker room.

But yeah, the run numbers still weren’t where anybody wants them. Jacobs had eleven carries against Detroit, and he admitted himself that he felt some rust. So was it Jacobs, or was it the line? LaFleur said there are a lot of factors. It’s never just one guy. It’s all eleven. Sometimes you get out-leveraged on a couple of play calls. Sometimes you run into bad looks. And he gave credit to the Lions, saying they do a good job when they commit to stopping the run, and that’s what happened. But he said Jacobs went in there, ran hard, ran physical, and got what he could.

And when he was asked how to get more out of the running game, LaFleur kept hammering the same point. It doesn’t fall on any one person. It’s play calls, it’s the guys up front, it’s the tight ends, it’s the receivers. You need all eleven doing their job at a high level on every play if you want to be efficient running the ball. And he admitted there have been breakdowns, missed targets, and bad looks. So it’s on everybody to fix it.

He was also asked about how Jacobs is being managed, whether they’re watching his workload coming off his return. LaFleur said who’s in there could be play dependent. So expect a rotation, especially on a short week.

And what about Caleb Johnson? A reporter asked what he has to do to get back in LaFleur’s good graces. And LaFleur laid it out. There are a lot of factors that decide whether you’re suiting up and getting chances. When you’re not the starter, it’s about where else you’re contributing, especially on special teams. And you have to be rock solid in pass protection. So that’s the message to Caleb. Contribute on special teams, and protect the quarterback. Do that, and the opportunities will come.

Now, let’s talk about a young player who’s dealing with some adversity. The rookie cornerback who got his first real action against Detroit. LaFleur said it was pretty impressive to see him go in there for the first time and play the way he did. He said he was really impressed. And when he was asked if the rookie would start on Thursday, LaFleur said, we’ll see, they’re working through everything.

There was a tough break for him in the game, a play in the end zone that didn’t go his way. But LaFleur defended him. He said this kid is mentally tough. He said he’s wired the right way. And he said that’s life in this league at any position, but especially at cornerback. Whether a flag gets thrown on you or somebody catches a ball on you, you have to be resilient and bounce back. That’s the right message. Corners have to have short memories.

Same thing with Trey Smack. After two missed field goals in a three-point loss, everybody wanted to know if LaFleur still trusts him. And LaFleur said they’ve seen enough of a sample size, and they’ve watched him progress all through the offseason, to feel good about where he’s at. He called the Detroit game unfortunate, but said it is what it is, and it’s a next-game mentality. So Smack is the guy. Now he has to go out and prove it.

Now let’s talk about one of my favorite parts of this press conference. Savion Williams.

LaFleur said Savion has given this team a spark. He’s made some critical plays on multiple third downs. And he’s a big physical presence. And then LaFleur told a great story. He said he told Savion that day, hey man, you’ve got to be the goon. Because Savion is a big, physical guy, and the team needs that from him.

I love that. Be the goon. Be the enforcer. Be the guy who sets the tone with physicality.

And when he was asked if it surprised him that Savion, who came out of college as more of a hybrid running back type, has been making real wide receiver plays, LaFleur said no. He said they were pretty confident he could do that. Remember, against Detroit, Savion caught all three of his targets for forty-seven yards, while other receivers were dealing with drops. He’s becoming a real weapon.

And then there was a fun one. The hook and ladder. Against Detroit, the Packers ran a hook and ladder involving Matthew Golden. And a reporter jokingly asked LaFleur if that meant donations to the “Shaky Boots fund” were down. LaFleur laughed and said he never should have said that in the press room. But he explained the play. He said you see it quite often around the league now, it seems like somebody runs it every week, and it gives you a chance to pick up a first down in long-yardage situations. It didn’t work out quite as well as they hoped, but they still got a positive play out of it. So LaFleur is willing to get creative. That’s good to see.

Now let’s look at Thursday’s opponent. The Carolina Panthers.

Carolina had played pretty well in recent weeks, and then they had a rough Sunday. LaFleur said a lot of that came down to the turnover battle. When you turn the ball over that much, no matter who you are, you put yourself behind the eight ball and it piles up fast. But he expects them to rebound completely. He said Green Bay is going to face a very motivated team that they know is dangerous.

And he reminded everybody of something important. Carolina came into Green Bay last year and beat the Packers. So there’s no overlooking this team. LaFleur said the Packers are motivated too, and they have to bounce back.

He also talked about Bryce Young, and he had real praise for him. LaFleur said Young is playing well. He said the thing that jumps off the tape is how he anticipates throws. He’s not scared. He’ll stand in there, take a big hit, and still get the ball out. He throws into tight windows with a lot of anticipation. And he’s got people to throw to. LaFleur said Carolina has a lot of weapons and they’re good up front.

On defense, Carolina has been hit with a lot of injuries, but LaFleur said it’s still a tough defense, and he praised their coordinator, Ejiro Evero. He said the system itself is difficult, and the players play really hard. And when he was asked about how inconsistent Carolina’s defense has been, giving up a ton of points some weeks and almost nothing in others, LaFleur said it’s a tough defense to play because they try to keep a lid on you and make you earn it. You have to be consistent with all eleven, stay patient, and when you get your shots, take them.

That’s the game plan, Packers Nation. Be patient. Don’t force the deep ball. Grind it out. And when Jordan Love gets the look he wants, let Watson or Golden go win it.

Now, the officiating. You remember how frustrated everybody was after Detroit. Players were vocal about it after the game. LaFleur has made it clear he doesn’t comment on officiating, but he explained how the process works. The team submits clips to the league along with a question about each one, and then someone at the league office responds and says whether they agree or not.

And when he was asked how good it feels when the league says yes, we agree with you, LaFleur joked that it makes him sleep so much better at night. And then he got real. He said it doesn’t actually matter at the end of the day. You move on. The biggest benefit is when there’s something you truly want to coach better. It’s about educating your players so they don’t put themselves in those positions.

He also gave the officials some respect. He said officiating in this league isn’t easy. Things happen fast, and there are bang-bang, split-second decisions. All a coach wants is consistency across the board, as best as it can be. Sometimes you get it, sometimes you don’t.

And on whether the frustration from Detroit could carry over, LaFleur said no. He told the team to flip the script and move on. You’re only as good as your last game in this league, and he admitted they didn’t play very well last time out. So they have to wipe it clean, come out with great energy, and attack this game.

And now. The moment that got everybody’s attention. The Micah Parsons update.

Every week, a reporter asks LaFleur about Micah. And every week, the answer has been cautious. This time, the reporter asked if LaFleur expects Parsons to start practicing next week.

And LaFleur said, I sure hope so.

The reporter immediately pointed out that it was a different answer than he’d given before. And LaFleur admitted, yes, it is. He said at the end of the day, it’s up to the medical staff. But he said he’s itching for it, for sure.

And then the reporter asked, so you’re saying there’s a chance?

And LaFleur said, I’m saying there’s a chance.

Packers Nation, that is the most optimistic LaFleur has sounded about Micah Parsons all season. And think about what that means. This defense has been playing at a high level without him. Lukas Van Ness has stepped up. Jonathan Gannon’s scheme has been confusing quarterbacks. And now, potentially, the best pass rusher on the roster could start practicing next week and be back for the stretch run.

That changes everything. When Parsons comes back, this defense goes from good to scary. A pass rush with Parsons and Van Ness coming off the edges. McKinney and Evan Williams in the back end. That’s the kind of defense that wins in December and January.

So let’s put it all together. Here’s where things stand heading into Carolina.

Travis Glover is dealing with a knee injury and hasn’t practiced. If he can’t go, Kevin Zeitler could make his first start of the season, and LaFleur says he feels confident in him.

Zaire Franklin is out, and Isaiah McDuffie, who wore the green dot and played great in Detroit, has to step up again.

Josh Jacobs is shaking off the rust, and LaFleur loves the edge and toughness he brings.

Caleb Johnson needs to contribute on special teams and in pass protection to earn more chances.

The rookie corner is mentally tough and could see a bigger role.

Trey Smack is still the kicker, and it’s a next-game mentality.

Savion Williams is becoming a spark plug, and LaFleur wants him to be the goon.

Carolina is motivated, dangerous, and beat Green Bay last year. Bryce Young is playing well.

And Micah Parsons? There’s a chance he starts practicing next week.

This is a must-win game, Packers Nation. After the loss in Detroit, you can’t drop two in a row, especially at home. The short week is tough. The injuries are real. But this team has bounced back before, and I believe they’ll do it again. Clean up the mistakes. Run the ball with purpose. Protect Jordan Love. Get off the field on third down. And send Carolina home with a loss.
