Alright. Let’s start with the man himself, because we have to talk about who Tyreek Hill is, and where he’s at right now.

Tyreek Hill is one of the most electric players this sport has ever seen. Eight Pro Bowls. Five first-team All-Pro selections. A Super Bowl ring with the Kansas City Chiefs. And in 2023 with Miami, he led the entire NFL with seventeen hundred ninety-nine receiving yards and thirteen touchdown catches. His first two years with the Dolphins were insane, two straight seasons with around seventeen hundred yards. This is a guy who doesn’t just beat cornerbacks. He embarrasses them. He turns a five-yard slant into a seventy-yard touchdown before the safety even knows what happened.

But here’s the reality. In week four of the 2025 season, Hill suffered a brutal knee injury. A dislocated knee and a torn ACL, along with other ligament damage. Season over. And in February, the Dolphins released him. That made him an unrestricted free agent for the first time in his entire career.

Since then, he’s been grinding through rehab. He’s been posting workout videos, including a clip of him cutting and running on the beach with no sign of problems from that knee. His doctors recommended that he wait until around October before attempting a comeback. And now we’re in October. And earlier this month, his agent Drew Rosenhaus said he expected Hill could sign with a team within days, and that Hill wants to play only for a contender. Teams like the Chiefs, the Giants, and the Ravens have been linked to him. But as of the latest reports, he still hasn’t signed anywhere.

And that’s where Green Bay comes in. There have already been voices around the league saying the Packers should go get him. One writer argued that signing a healthy Tyreek Hill could be the move that wipes away the rest of this team’s questions on offense and makes Green Bay one of the most dangerous offenses in the NFL. And honestly? After what we just saw against the Bears, it’s hard to argue with that.

A passer rating of one thirty-seven point five. Ten point eight yards per attempt. Analysts are openly asking if he’s a top three deep ball thrower in the NFL. Christian Watson put up one hundred forty-three yards and two touchdowns, including a spectacular fifty-three yard catch, and he’s reportedly on pace for around sixteen hundred yards. Matthew Golden had a career-high one hundred twenty-three yards and is becoming the clear number two receiver.

Now take that offense, and add Tyreek Hill.

On the other, Matthew Golden, who’s been winning contested catches all over the field. And in the slot or moving all over the formation, Tyreek Hill, maybe the fastest football player alive. There aren’t enough safeties in the world. You can’t double everybody. You can’t roll coverage to all three. Somebody is going to be one-on-one every single snap. And when you’ve got Jordan Love throwing the ball the way he’s throwing it right now, one-on-one means touchdown.

When a defense is terrified of getting beat over the top, they back up. And when they back up, the middle of the field opens up. Now, I’m not going to just hype you up without telling you the risks, because that’s not how we do it on Third Floor.

First, the knee. Hill is coming off a devastating injury. We don’t know yet if he’ll have the same explosive burst he had before. Rehab videos are one thing. NFL Sundays are another. Any team that signs him has to be confident his knee can hold up and that he can still separate from cornerbacks.

Second, age. Hill is thirty-two. And even before the injury, his production had started to dip from those monster seasons. Wide receivers who rely on speed don’t always age the way teams hope. So you’d have to be realistic about what you’re getting. Maybe not the seventeen hundred yard guy every week. But even a slightly slower Tyreek Hill is still faster than almost everybody on the field.

Third, the off-field history. Hill has had a lot of off-field issues follow him throughout his career. And the Packers have always been careful about the kind of people they bring into that locker room. That would absolutely be part of the conversation.

And fourth, the Packers’ history. Let’s be honest, Green Bay is not known for big, splashy moves like this. Signing Tyreek Hill would be out of character for this front office. But then again, they’ve surprised us before. And if they believe they’ve got a real Super Bowl window right now, with Jordan Love playing like this, sometimes you have to swing big.

Alright, let’s switch gears, because there’s another big story around this team, and it’s all about money and the future. The Packers have seven players who could be in line for new contracts during the 2026 season or in the 2027 offseason. And general manager Brian Gutekunst and executive vice president Russ Ball have a tough job ahead. They have to reward their best players without killing the financial flexibility they need to build a championship roster. And if they ever wanted to make a move for a big name like Hill, every dollar matters.

Let’s start with Javon Bullard and Evan Williams. Both were drafted in 2024, and both are making strong cases for extensions. Bullard was a second-round pick who moved from deep safety to starting slot cornerback in the middle of his rookie season. And he’s improved a ton. He’s allowing a passer rating of just seventy-six point nine when targeted. He’s eligible for an extension in the 2027 offseason, and honestly, Green Bay should think seriously about locking him up before his price goes up.

Evan Williams was a fourth-round pick who became a starting safety early in his rookie season and never looked back. This season, he’s allowing a passer rating of just sixty-one point five, with six stops and five pressures. He just had twelve tackles against the Bears. He can cover, he can tackle, he can do it all, and he has All-Pro potential. The problem? Both Bullard and Williams could become eligible for extensions at the same time. I want the Packers to keep both. But the money has to be managed carefully.

Then there’s Lukas Van Ness. The Packers picked up his fifth-year option, which keeps him under contract through 2027 at thirteen point seven five million. But an extension could still make sense, because he’s showing real progress. Through five games, he has sixteen pressures and two and a half sacks, including some strong performances while Micah Parsons was out. If Van Ness keeps getting better, Green Bay might be smart to negotiate now, before his price climbs even higher. A new deal could also create cap flexibility in 2027.

Xavier McKinney is a different situation. He’s twenty-eight, he came to Green Bay in 2024, and he’s earned All-Pro honors in both of his first two seasons here, first team in 2024 and second team in 2025. He already has three interceptions this season, including two against the Bears. He’s under contract through 2027, but his projected cap hit that year is twenty-four point eight million after a restructure. A two or three-year extension could lower that number and keep an elite player in Green Bay. But here’s the big question: can the Packers afford McKinney, Bullard, and Williams all together? That’s the puzzle the front office has to solve.

Keisean Nixon is also coming up on a contract decision. 2026 is the last year of his three-year extension, and he’s earning about seven point one six six million. After taking a lot of criticism late in 2025, Nixon has bounced back with a better season. Through five games, he’s allowing a passer rating of seventy-seven point two, with two pass breakups, an interception, and nine stops. But Green Bay has Brandon Sims as a long-term option, they signed Benjamin St-Juste to a two-year deal, and they could lose Carrington Valentine in free agency. A short-term extension for Nixon is possible, but the rest of his season will probably decide it.

Defensive tackle Karl Brooks is entering the final year of his rookie deal too. The former sixth-round pick is earning three point seven million thanks to the proven performance escalator. He’s turned into a strong run defender, with five pressures and four stops in a rotational role. Devonte Wyatt is under contract for multiple years, and the Packers drafted a defensive tackle in the third round. But with Javon Hargrave suffering a torn Achilles and possibly becoming a release candidate because of his age and contract, Brooks could see a bigger role. A team-friendly short-term extension is possible.

And the seventh player is Skyy Moore, a wide receiver and kick returner on a one-year, two and a half million dollar deal. He suffered an ankle injury against the Bears, but it doesn’t look like a long-term problem. Before the injury, he was averaging sixteen yards per punt return and twenty-seven and a half yards per kickoff return. At twenty-six, he could earn a decent raise if he keeps that up.

And there’s one honorable mention that hurts. Edgerrin Cooper. The 2024 second-round pick has already shown star potential at just twenty-four years old. He’s eligible for an extension in the 2027 offseason, but the ruptured Achilles will keep him out for the rest of this season. He’s done enough to deserve serious extension talks. The tough question is whether Green Bay negotiates before 2027 or waits until he’s back on the field.

These contract decisions could shape this defense for years. But as we’ve said, the front office also has to think about the offense. And that brings us back to the trade market.

There’s still the Saquon Barkley idea out there. TJ French of Heavy floated a trade scenario where Green Bay could get Barkley from the Eagles for a conditional fourth-round pick that could turn into a third. Again, it’s hypothetical, not a real deal. The run game needs consistency, Caleb Johnson hasn’t solved it, and Josh Jacobs remains on the commissioner’s exempt list with his availability uncertain. Barkley could give Jordan Love another dangerous weapon. But he’s dealing with a hamstring injury, he’s twenty-nine, he’s got thirty-six carries for one hundred eighty-two yards and no rushing touchdowns this season, and he’s on a two-year, forty-one point two million dollar extension through 2028. So there are real risks.

And honestly, when I compare the two ideas, here’s what I keep coming back to. Barkley would cost draft capital and a big contract. Tyreek Hill is a free agent. No trade. No draft picks going out the door. If he’s healthy and the price is right, that could actually be the more interesting move. Both come with risk. But the upside on both is enormous.

So let’s bring it all together. This Packers team is at a crossroads. On one hand, there are young stars on defense who need to be paid: Bullard, Williams, Van Ness, McKinney, and eventually Cooper. On the other hand, this offense is one big piece away from being truly terrifying. The front office has to find the balance. Be aggressive, but don’t wreck the future.

But Packers Nation, if they do decide to swing big, and if that swing ends up being Tyreek Hill? I’m telling you, buckle up.
