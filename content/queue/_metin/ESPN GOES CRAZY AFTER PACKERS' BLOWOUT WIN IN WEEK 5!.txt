Now let’s get to what everybody was talking about. Jordan Love.

When the analysts sat down in the studio after this game, the first thing that jumped off the screen was the box score. And Packers Nation, let me give you the full line, because it deserves to be read slowly. Jordan Love completed twenty-five of thirty-one passes. Three hundred sixty-seven yards. Two touchdowns. Zero interceptions. That’s a completion rate over eighty percent. A passer rating of one thirty-seven point five. And the Packers averaged ten point eight yards per pass attempt.

Ten point eight yards per attempt. Let that sink in for a second. That’s not a team inching down the field with little checkdowns. That’s a team taking huge chunks out of a defense every single time the quarterback drops back. That’s a team going vertical, attacking downfield, and making Chicago pay for every opportunity it gave them.

And the analysts noticed something specific about how Jordan played this game. Everybody knows he can throw the deep ball. That’s never been a secret. But against the Bears on Sunday, it felt like he was hunting it. He wasn’t waiting for the deep shot to come to him. He was going out there looking for it. And the breakdown in studio made it clear that this was the plan all along. The Packers’ mindset going in was to attack the Bears with splash plays, because they believed their wide receivers were simply better than the defenders who were going to be covering them. And then came the question. The big one. The one that’s been floating around ever since. At this point, can we say Jordan Love is a top three deep ball thrower in the NFL?

Think about that. A year or two ago, people were still debating if Jordan was the long-term answer. Now national analysts are openly asking if he’s one of the three best deep passers in the entire league. That’s how much this game changed the conversation.

When Jordan gets in the pocket and starts drifting a little to the left or a little to the right, and he throws that ball off his back foot, you know Matthew Golden is going to be down there somewhere. Maybe the receivers weren’t winning. Maybe the trust wasn’t fully there. But this year, when Jordan lets it rip, you can feel the confidence that his playmakers are going to make a play on the ball. That’s the difference. That’s growth. That’s a quarterback and a receiving room that are finally on the same page.

There’s another piece of this that I absolutely loved. The protection. One of the most encouraging parts of Sunday was how clean this offense looked when the line held up. Jordan was sacked twice, and both of those came at the end of the first half. Chicago managed only five quarterback hits the entire game. Five. Against a Bears defense that came in with a reputation for blitzing and getting after the quarterback. That’s a massive improvement over what we saw from this offensive line in the previous game.

And when Jordan Love has time? Forget about it. He can stand in the pocket, read the coverage, and deliver the football wherever he wants. We know he has the arm. Now let’s talk about the two men who made the studio go wild. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden.

Christian Watson finished with six catches for one hundred forty-three yards and two touchdowns. He averaged more than twenty-three yards every time he caught the ball. Every touch was a problem for Chicago. Every route was a threat. The analysts kept coming back to one deep shot to Watson in particular, a play they said the Bears could have easily avoided, but didn’t. And that’s the thing about Christian Watson. You make one little mistake against him, one bad angle, one hesitation, and he’s gone. On Sunday, the Bears made that mistake more than once, and Watson punished them for it.

And Matthew Golden. Six catches for one hundred twenty-three yards, about twenty yards per catch. The analysts said Golden is starting to find his niche in this offense, and I could not agree more.
