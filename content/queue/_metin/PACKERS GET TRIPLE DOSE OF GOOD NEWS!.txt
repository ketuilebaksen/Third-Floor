Let’s start with good news number one. Zaire Franklin.

A few weeks ago, after a really solid start to his season, Franklin took a knee to the shin during practice. It was an accident. But it turned out to be a hairline fracture in his leg. And he’s missed the last three games because of it.

Now, a hairline fracture in the shin is the kind of injury that needs time to heal. Teams don’t want to rush a player back from it, because if you push it too fast, a small crack can turn into a much bigger break. That’s why the Packers never put him on injured reserve, but they also didn’t rush him. Three games is a pretty normal stretch for an injury like that. And the bye week gave him a little extra time to make sure he’s fully healed.

And now his return against the Rams looks probable. LaFleur had said before that Franklin should be good to go around week twelve. And with that Instagram post, it sure sounds like he’s ready. I’d expect to see him back at practice in a full capacity this weekend. And I’m really excited to see the green dot back on the field.

Remember, Franklin came to Green Bay from Indianapolis in a trade for Colby Wooden this offseason, and he quickly became a defensive captain. The green dot is the helmet with the radio, the player who gets the calls from the sideline and lines everybody up. That’s a big responsibility. And it’s a role Franklin was born to play.

Now, let’s give credit where it’s due. Isaiah McDuffie and Ty’Ron Hopper did a really good job while Franklin was out. McDuffie wore the green dot against Detroit, and LaFleur said he did a great job. Hopper had one of the best games of his career against Carolina, with eight tackles, a sack, an interception, three passes defended, and two quarterback hits. And they did all of that while the team was also without Edgerrin Cooper, who’s done for the season with a torn Achilles.

But here’s the problem. The depth behind them. When McDuffie or Hopper came out for a play or two, Nick Niemann got shuffled into the rotation. And in some of those snaps, opposing offenses went right at him. Now, Niemann has been a great teammate. LaFleur even gave him a game ball after the Patriots game for playing every defensive snap and every special teams snap. But having your starting linebacker back means the Packers don’t have to lean on that depth as much.

And the timing couldn’t be better. Because the Packers play the Los Angeles Rams in just a few days. Wednesday. Thanksgiving Eve. In Los Angeles. It’s a strange day to play football, but having your green dot linebacker back for that game is massive.

And here’s why. Because of what the Rams do.

I know what you’re thinking. The Rams have Matthew Stafford. They’ve got Sean McVay. People assume McVay just throws the ball all over the field. But that’s not the case. Especially this year.

The Rams have a running back duo that’s been phenomenal. Kyren Williams has one hundred thirty-nine carries for six hundred six yards and six touchdowns. Blake Corum has one hundred three carries for five hundred forty-seven yards and five touchdowns. That’s two backs, both over five hundred yards, combining for eleven rushing touchdowns.

As a team, the Rams have run the football two hundred eighty-eight times. That’s top five in the National Football League. And they’re top five in almost every rushing metric.

And part of the reason is the state of their receiver room. It’s been hit hard by major injuries. Puka Nacua might not be back for the rest of the season. Davante Adams has missed the last four or five weeks with a hamstring strain. So Tutu Atwell has become their number one receiver, and their number two is a player most fans have probably never heard of.

Think about how much that changes things. When an offense can’t count on its top receivers, it leans on the run and on short throws. And that means the defense has to be ready to stop the ground game first.

Look at last week’s game against the Arizona Cardinals. The Rams won thirty-four to six. And all of their wide receivers combined for just ten targets. Seven catches. One hundred seventy-four yards. And a touchdown. That’s the brand of football they’re playing right now. Run it down your throat. Win at the line of scrimmage. Pound the rock with Williams and Corum. And then let Stafford take his shots when the opportunity shows up.

So that’s why Franklin’s return isn’t just a feel-good story. It’s a practical answer to the way the Rams are built.

Think about what a linebacker does against a running team. The linebackers read the blocking. They fill the gaps. They make the tackle in the open field. And a player who calls the defense and lines everyone up becomes even more important, because a run-heavy offense is going to test every single alignment. That’s why the green dot matters. It’s not just about talent. It’s about getting all eleven players to see the same picture at the same time.

And Franklin helps the pass defense too. The linebacker in the middle is often responsible for covering running backs and tight ends on short passes. If a defense is getting beat by running backs catching the ball underneath, its coverage gets stretched thin. A veteran who knows his assignments makes the whole group faster to react.

And remember, the Packers got gashed on the ground a few times this season. In their loss to Atlanta early on, the run defense struggled. But against Chicago, they held the league’s best rushing team to forty-nine yards on fourteen carries from their running backs. Against Detroit, they held Jahmyr Gibbs to under four yards a carry. This front can stop the run. And with Franklin back calling the shots, it should be even better against Williams and Corum.

And here’s one more reason his return matters so much this week. Because the game is on a Wednesday, the usual rhythm of a week is compressed. Teams get fewer practice days to adjust. That makes every available veteran even more valuable. Especially the one who’s responsible for getting everyone lined up correctly.

The fireman is coming. And the Packers are going to need him.

Now, good news number two. And this one is about help from outside Green Bay. Because the standings matter.

Baker Mayfield practiced this week, and he looks fine. He should be trending toward playing on Sunday against the Detroit Lions. Rick Stroud, who covers the Buccaneers, reported after practice that Mayfield said if he continues on this path, he should be able to play on Sunday.

Why should Packers fans care? Because the Lions are hosting the Buccaneers this Sunday. And right now, Green Bay is sitting in last place in the NFC North at six and four.

Let’s look at the division picture. The Vikings are going to be really hard to catch. They’ve beaten the Packers twice this season, and they only have two losses. That’s a big gap to close, and Green Bay doesn’t have the head-to-head tiebreaker anymore.

But the Lions and the Bears? Those are realistic teams to catch. And the Packers still have a game to play against each of them. So if those teams start dropping some games, Green Bay can climb right back into the race.

Now, I don’t want to oversell this. One result in another stadium doesn’t decide the Packers’ season. Green Bay still has to win its own games. But when you’re in last place in your division, even small help matters. And watching the other games is part of the job.

And Tampa Bay has a better shot than most to beat Detroit this weekend. Because the Lions are banged up. Aidan Hutchinson isn’t going to play. And neither is Jack Campbell. Detroit has lost two of its best defenders over the last couple of weeks. And the Hutchinson injury was just brutal. He got hurt celebrating a sack. That’s how unlucky it was.

Tampa Bay has a really good defense. And if Mayfield plays, that gives the Bucs a better chance. And if Mayfield helps hand Detroit an extra loss? That helps the Packers.

And here’s why division losses matter so much. When teams in the same division finish with similar records, tiebreakers are decided by head-to-head results first, and then by records against common opponents and division opponents. The Packers are one and three in the division right now. That’s going to hurt them in tiebreakers. So they need their rivals to lose games against other teams too. That’s why every Lions loss, every Bears loss, every Vikings loss matters for Green Bay down the stretch.

So Packers Nation, this Sunday, become temporary Buccaneers fans. Go Baker!

Now before we get to good news number three, let’s clear something up. Because there’s a Micah Parsons rumor going around social media that’s not what it looks like.

A lot of people on social media thought Micah Parsons was announcing that he’s back and ready to play. He posted the Netflix promo for the Rams versus Packers game on his Instagram story. And the text at the beginning of the video says that he’s ready.

People saw that and assumed Parsons had added that text himself. A lot of fans got really excited. And honestly, I did too, for about half a second.

But then if you look at the story closely, that text was just the opening of the promo itself. Parsons was simply sharing a video about who the Packers play next. Now, it could mean he’s excited and wants to let everyone know he’s coming back. Or it could just be that he reposted it without thinking about the fact that it says “I’m ready” at the start.

Think about it this way. A promo video is a marketing product. The language in it is written for the audience, not by the player. A player who reposts it is probably just sharing something the platform sent him. That’s the most likely explanation. So if you see that on Twitter or Instagram today, don’t treat it like an official announcement. Wait for real news.

What the rumor really shows is how hungry this fan base is for any sign of Micah Parsons. And that’s understandable. But the injury report is the official document. The team decides what goes on it. Anything else, a caption, a promo, a photo, is a clue at best. And a player posting something doesn’t mean the team has cleared him.

Here’s what we actually know. LaFleur said on Monday that his plan is for Parsons to play against the Rams. But he also said there are still a couple more hurdles to clear before they can definitively say he’s a go. The Packers waived Pierre Strong this week, which opens up a roster spot that could be used to activate Parsons. And his practice window closes on November twenty-fifth, the day of the game. So the signs are pointing in the right direction. But it’s not official until it’s official.

And if Parsons does play, think about the matchup. The Rams just lost two centers against Arizona. Coleman Shelton is out for weeks, and Dylan McMahon went to injured reserve. A Rams offensive line scrambling in the middle, against Micah Parsons and Zaire Franklin both coming back? That’s a big problem for Los Angeles.

Now, good news number three. The rookie report card. Because this 2026 draft class is starting to show what it can become.

And remember, this draft class came without a first-round pick. The Packers sent that pick to Dallas in the Micah Parsons trade. Brian Gutekunst still made six selections, and four of them went to defense. And a couple of them are already making a real impact.

First up, cornerback Brandon Cisse. And I’m giving him an A.

Now, here’s the thing. If we’d done this report card after the first four weeks of the season, it probably would’ve been a C, maybe even a D. He got picked on early. Opposing quarterbacks went right after the rookie. And that’s not easy. It’s not easy to come into the NFL and get targeted for four straight weeks.

But over the last six weeks? Cisse has been one of the best rookie defensive backs in football. And one of the best players at his position, period. He’s turned it all around.

He’s a playmaker. He’s got multiple pass breakups and an interception. And he sticks in coverage, which is everything you want from a corner. Against Detroit, he prevented two potential touchdowns against Jameson Williams, including a diving breakup in the end zone. And he was just named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Month.

And at only twenty-one years old. LaFleur said it’s pretty remarkable. He said Cisse is wired the right way. He loves football. He’s always studying. And he gave credit to the coaching staff for getting him up to speed, because the NFL is a very different game than college.

And this grade is a reminder of how quickly a rookie can change his story. A player who got picked on for four straight weeks could have lost his confidence. Instead, he got better every single week. That’s the sign of a young player who learns from his mistakes. And it’s a big reason his grade is so high.

Taken at pick fifty-two. Starting since week one. Rookie of the Month. That’s an A.

Next, defensive tackle Chris McClellan. He gets a B-plus.

Remember, the Packers traded picks eighty-four and one sixty to Tampa Bay to move up and get McClellan at pick seventy-seven. And that move looks smarter every week, especially since Javon Hargrave went down with his season-ending Achilles injury.

McClellan has had a good, solid season. Really good. To me, an A means a player who flashes game in and game out and could be considered one of the biggest steals at his position in the draft. McClellan has been good, and I’m not complaining. When I was a kid, if I got a B-plus on my report card, I was never mad about it.

And I think he’s been more impactful than expected, since Hargrave’s injury pushed him into a bigger role. I’d still like to see a few more splash plays. But I give him the benefit of the doubt. Because defensive tackle isn’t a splash-play position. When you grade a defensive tackle, you have to remember what he’s asked to do. Occupy blockers. Stop the run. Let the linebackers and edge rushers make plays. Those jobs don’t always show up in the box score. LaFleur said it himself earlier this season. The defensive tackles’ stats might look modest, but they’re doing their job, playing selfless team defense and eating up blockers.

And against a Rams team that runs the ball as much as anyone, McClellan’s job on Wednesday is going to be huge.

And the rest of this class has shown promise too. Edge rusher Dani Dennis-Sutton, a fourth-round pick who posted an incredible relative athletic score of nine point nine six out of ten, has been making plays in the backfield. He combined with Lukas Van Ness on a crucial third-down sack against Carolina. And center Jager Burton, who played center at Kentucky, is a long-term piece the Packers are developing. This is exactly the kind of cheap, young talent that Gutekunst’s whole philosophy is built on.
