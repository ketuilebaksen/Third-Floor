Packers Nation, something is seriously wrong in Green Bay right now. And the fallout from that brutal loss to the Minnesota Vikings is already being felt inside the building.

There’s been a roster exit. There’s a possible defensive boost on the way. And there’s one very expensive offseason signing that’s quickly turning into a massive headache for this organization. A seventy-seven million dollar guard who didn’t play a single offensive snap against Minnesota.

Welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’ve got two big stories. First, a full breakdown of the Vikings loss, including the two players who actually gave us something to smile about, and the five areas that completely fell apart. And then we’ll get into the roster moves, the Micah Parsons update, and the uncomfortable question surrounding Aaron Banks’ future in Green Bay. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the loss. Green Bay fell hard to the Vikings at Lambeau Field. The Packers are now six and four overall. But here’s the number that really stings. They’re one and three against NFC North opponents. In the best division in football, where every team is winning, a one and three division record is a big problem when it comes to tiebreakers.

In his breakdown for Athlon Sports, Mike Santa Barbara named two winners and five losers from this game. So let’s start with the good. Because there was some.

Winner number one. Xavier McKinney.

Remember, McKinney was a surprise addition to the injury report last week with a new foot injury. He was questionable all the way up to kickoff. And not only did he play, he made a big play. In the second quarter, Kirk Cousins overthrew a pass, and McKinney was right there to pick it off.

And get this. That was McKinney’s sixth interception of the season. According to Santa Barbara, that leads the entire NFL. And it was his third interception in just two games, after his first-play pick of Drake Maye against New England.

Six interceptions. Leading the league. On a bad foot. Xavier McKinney is a two-time All-Pro for a reason, and he keeps proving it. When everything else around him is falling apart, he’s still taking the football away.

Winner number two. Kaleb Johnson.

After his career day against the Patriots, when he ran fourteen times for sixty-eight yards and a touchdown, Johnson kept producing. He ran ten times for forty-two yards against Minnesota. That’s four point two yards per carry. Against what LaFleur called maybe the best defense in the NFL.

And here’s the context that makes it even more impressive. MarShawn Lloyd and Chris Brooks combined for just fourteen yards on six carries. So Johnson was clearly the most effective back on the field. Matt Schneidman of The Athletic said before the game that the Packers should lean on Johnson, because he has the vision and the burst. And he was right.

But here’s the problem. Packers running backs got just sixteen carries the entire game. Sixteen. Johnson showed he can contribute. But the team largely abandoned the run, while its quarterback was under siege. And when you abandon the run against a blitz-heavy defense, you make it even easier for them to pin their ears back and come after the quarterback.

Now, let’s go to the losers. And there are a lot of them.

Loser number one. Matt LaFleur.

Green Bay managed just two hundred twenty yards of offense. That’s its lowest total since the one hundred ninety-nine yards it had back in week two. The Packers converted just three of ten third downs. And the offense was responsible for three of the team’s four turnovers.

But Santa Barbara’s biggest criticism was the timing of LaFleur’s adjustments. Jordan Love was facing immediate pressure from the very start of the game. But the offense didn’t make enough changes until the second half.

And LaFleur basically admitted that himself after the game. He said he was searching for anything that would get the ball out of Jordan’s hands quickly. He called it a bad decision not to go to the quick game earlier, because his quarterback was getting abused, just like in the first game against Minnesota. And he said he needed to go back and look at the protection. When a head coach is publicly calling his own decisions bad decisions, you know it was a rough day at the office.

Loser number two. The entire offensive line. Every single member.

Here’s who started. Jordan Morgan at left tackle. Laken Tomlinson at left guard. John Williams at center. Travis Glover at right guard. And Anthony Belton at right tackle. Aaron Banks was active, but he didn’t start.

And the Vikings feasted. Minnesota generated twenty-one pressures. Twelve quarterback hits. And eight sacks. Santa Barbara described those eight sacks as an NFL season high. Eight sacks. The most any team has had in a single game all season long.

Jordan Love couldn’t settle into the pocket. He couldn’t let routes develop. Every time he dropped back, there was someone in his face. And it wasn’t just the sacks. Twelve quarterback hits means Jordan was getting knocked down over and over again, even on the plays where he got the ball out. After the game, he said he got stepped on pretty good in the third quarter and had a bruise. He said he’ll be fine. But no team can win when its quarterback takes that kind of beating.

And this offensive line story gets even more uncomfortable when we get to the second half of this video. Because one name was missing from that starting lineup.

Loser number three. Matthew Golden.

Golden caught five passes for seventy yards. But he also dropped two passes. And one of those drops erased a possible third-down conversion. And according to the report, Golden now leads the entire NFL with ten drops this season.

Now here’s the frustrating part. He didn’t have a single drop in weeks eight and nine. He’d been clean. And in week nine against New England, he and Christian Watson became the first duo in Packers history to each have seven catches, one hundred fifty yards, and a touchdown in the same game. So we know what he’s capable of. But against Minnesota, the drops came back. And in a game where every possession mattered, those drops hurt.

Loser number four. Trey Smack.

Smack missed a fifty-three yard field goal in the first quarter. And Santa Barbara pointed out how much that mattered. If he makes it, the game is tied at three. Instead, Minnesota scored on its very next drive to go up ten to nothing. That’s a ten-point swing in the momentum of the game. And it’s another miss in a season where Smack has struggled. LaFleur said he still has a lot of confidence in him. But games like this make that confidence harder and harder to justify.

Loser number five. Christian Watson.

Watson got only five targets, catching three passes for thirty-seven yards. Santa Barbara called it his lowest receiving output since 2024. Now, this one isn’t really on Watson himself. LaFleur explained after the game that the Vikings were playing hard outside leverage on the receivers, almost like a double team, while keeping an umbrella over the defense. So Minnesota made a decision to take Watson away. And with Jordan Love under pressure on almost every snap, there wasn’t time to find him anyway.

So here’s the full picture. McKinney and Johnson gave fans some reasons for hope. But the protection, the coaching response, the drops, the missed kick, and the lack of big plays in the passing game all combined into a miserable division loss.

Now let’s get to the second story. And it starts with a confirmed roster move.

On November sixteenth, Brian Gutekunst announced the release of rookie defensive tackle David Blay Jr. from the practice squad. Blay played college football at the University of Miami. He’s six-foot-two and two hundred ninety-two pounds. And he’d joined Green Bay’s practice squad on September thirtieth, after earlier stops elsewhere around the league.

Now, let’s keep this in perspective. This was a depth move. It wasn’t the release of a starting defender. And the announcement didn’t connect it to the Vikings loss. The team followed up by adding wide receiver Coleman Owen and another defensive tackle to the practice squad. So this is about shuffling the bottom of the roster, not a dramatic shake-up.

But there’s a much bigger potential reinforcement on the way. Micah Parsons.

According to a report from Heavy, Green Bay is targeting a possible week twelve return for Parsons against the Los Angeles Rams, after the week eleven bye. That’s the Thanksgiving Eve game on Wednesday, November twenty-fifth.

Remember, Parsons is coming back from a torn ACL and meniscus damage. He opened his twenty-one-day practice window earlier this month, and he’s been a limited participant. Last week, he even got work in eleven-on-eleven periods, and he bull-rushed Tucker Kraft in practice. But LaFleur held him out of the Vikings game, saying Parsons looks great but just doesn’t have enough reps under his belt yet. And he was also dealing with an illness.

Now, LaFleur has described getting Parsons back as the goal. But he hasn’t guaranteed it. Parsons still has some hurdles to clear before he gets the green light. And if he does play against the Rams, the report suggests he’d likely be on a snap count at first, as he adjusts to game speed and shakes off the rust.

And remember, the Packers have to activate Parsons by the end of his practice window, which closes on November twenty-fifth. The same day as the Rams game. So the timing lines up perfectly.

Think about what Parsons could mean for this team right now. A defense that’s already leading the league in takeaways in some games, with McKinney leading the NFL in interceptions. Add a four-time All-Pro pass rusher, even on limited snaps, and suddenly opposing quarterbacks have to worry about him on every single dropback. That’s the kind of boost that could turn this season back around.

And now, the most uncomfortable roster question of all. Aaron Banks.

In a November sixteenth article for Acme Packing Company, John Meerdink criticized the performance of Green Bay’s seventy-seven million dollar guard signing. And the numbers against Minnesota are eye-opening.

Banks played just three snaps against the Vikings. All three were on special teams. Two field goal attempts and one extra point. He didn’t play a single snap on offense.

A seventy-seven million dollar offensive lineman. In a game where the offensive line gave up eight sacks. And he didn’t take a single offensive snap.

So what happened? LaFleur explained it after the game. He said Laken Tomlinson had been playing well, so they moved him back to his natural position at left guard. And Banks was supposed to get a chance to compete at right guard, especially with Kevin Zeitler dealing with ankle and triceps injuries. But the coaching staff chose Travis Glover instead. And LaFleur pointed out that Banks doesn’t have much experience at right guard. He’s a career left guard. When asked if all the shuffling contributed to the problems up front, LaFleur said they could make that excuse, but no.

And Meerdink points out that this isn’t just a one-week issue. Banks has appeared in only four games this season. In one of those games, he played no offensive snaps at all. In another, he couldn’t finish the game. In total, he’s played just one hundred thirty-seven offensive snaps all season.

One hundred thirty-seven snaps. That’s a tiny return for a player Brian Gutekunst praised as entering his prime when Green Bay signed him in 2025.

And the contract makes the next decision complicated.

Banks is due a nine point five million dollar roster bonus on March fifteenth, 2027. And Meerdink speculates that Green Bay might want to avoid paying that bonus. One way to do that would be a post-June first release designation, which would spread the salary cap consequences across 2027 and 2028.

But here’s the catch. Meerdink also notes that an outright release might actually cost slightly more against the cap than keeping Banks, because of a contract restructure the team already did. So it’s not as simple as just cutting him. The money gets messy.

Now let me be absolutely clear, because this is important. No Banks release has been announced. The Packers have not confirmed any plan to move on from him. Everything about a possible release is Meerdink’s projection. Not a completed transaction.

But right now, a seventy-seven million dollar investment is struggling to even get on the field. While Green Bay desperately needs better blocking.

And that’s what ties these two stories together. Think about it. The offensive line just gave up an NFL season-high eight sacks. And the most expensive guard on the roster was watching from the sideline on offense. That’s a problem for the coaching staff. It’s a problem for the front office. And it raises real questions about how this line gets fixed.

Because this front office has built its whole philosophy around smart spending. We talked about it with the quiet trade deadline. With Jordan Love making fifty-five million a year and Micah Parsons making more than forty million, the Packers need every other contract on the roster to deliver value. Players like Evan Williams, a fourth-rounder graded as one of the best safeties in football. Players like Brandon Cisse, the Defensive Rookie of the Month. That’s how the math works. And a seventy-seven million dollar guard who plays one hundred thirty-seven snaps in ten games is the opposite of that. It’s money that isn’t helping the team win.

So what should the Packers do? In the short term, they have to find the best five offensive linemen and stick with them. The constant shuffling hasn’t worked. Whether that includes Banks at right guard, or Glover, or a healthy Zeitler, or eventually Sean Rhyan back at center, this team needs continuity up front. And they have the bye week to figure it out.

In the long term, the Banks decision will come in March. And that’s when we’ll find out if Green Bay is willing to eat some cap pain to move on, or if it believes he can still be the player it paid for.

So let’s put it all together.

The Packers are six and four, with a damaging one and three division record. Xavier McKinney keeps making plays and now leads the NFL with six interceptions. Kaleb Johnson is earning a bigger role. Micah Parsons could return against the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve. Those are real positives.

But the offensive line is a major concern. Eight sacks. Twenty-one pressures. Twelve quarterback hits. And Aaron Banks’ uncertain role makes that problem harder to ignore.

The practice squad changes are confirmed. Parsons’ return is a target, not a guarantee. And a Banks departure is still just speculation.

The bye week is coming at the perfect time. The question is whether Green Bay can use it to fix the protection, sharpen the play-calling, and come out firing against the Rams. Because there are seven games left, and this team still has a real shot at the playoffs. But it has to start protecting its quarterback. Now.
