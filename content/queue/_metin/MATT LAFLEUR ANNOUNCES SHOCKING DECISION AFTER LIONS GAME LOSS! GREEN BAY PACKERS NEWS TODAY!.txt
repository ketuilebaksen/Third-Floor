And I know a lot of you woke up this morning wondering if the hot streak was just a mirage.

Alright. Let’s rewind for a second, because context matters. Just a few weeks ago, this team was a mess. People on national TV were saying the offense looked broken and the defense looked even worse. Analysts were saying the offensive line was awful and that Jordan Love had no chance to succeed. One of them even said it looked like the Packers defense had quit. That’s where this team was. Written off. Buried. Done.

And then what happened? The Packers edged out the Buccaneers. Then they obliterated the Bears thirty-one to seven at Lambeau, when Chicago came in with its chest puffed out, ready to take over the NFC North. And then they went into Sunday Night Football and beat the Dallas Cowboys twenty-three to thirteen, in a game that honestly could have been a lot bigger. Three straight wins. A four and two record. From busted flush to hot streak in about two weeks.

So what changed? This is the key. The Packers didn’t suddenly get healthy. They didn’t magically fix every weakness. What changed was how they started dealing with those weaknesses. And that came down to a decision. A big one.

Let’s talk about the offensive line, because that’s where it all started. For weeks, this line was a disaster. Green Bay ranked dead last in the entire NFL in pass block win rate. Dead last. Plays were breaking down before they could even develop. Jordan Love was getting hit constantly. And he was being forced to play hero ball just to keep the team competitive. You can’t win like that. Nobody can.

So the front office and the coaching staff made a call. They brought in veterans. Laken Tomlinson. Mekhi Becton. Kevin Zeitler. And they started shuffling things around. Tomlinson went in at left guard. Travis Glover, who had been overmatched at tackle, moved inside to right guard, where he’s been a lot more effective. And just like that, the whole thing started to stabilize. Remember what LaFleur said about Zeitler last week? That he loves the guy, that he’s wired the right way, that he’s everything you want in a player. That’s the kind of veteran presence this line was missing.

Now, is the line perfect? No. Jordan is still getting hit more than you’d like. But here’s the genius part. The coaching staff didn’t just change the personnel. They changed the approach. They got the ball out of Jordan’s hands faster. Quicker reads. Quicker throws. On time and on rhythm. And the results have been incredible.

Listen to this. Over a three-game stretch, Jordan Love put up fifteen big-time throws according to PFF. That led the entire NFL. He’s completing passes at a much more efficient rate, he’s throwing touchdowns, and he’s finally slinging it like himself again. And his average time to throw dropped to two point four eight seconds. That’s the lowest of his career as a starter, and the lowest among all eligible quarterbacks in the league this year. Think about that. The quarterback who was getting killed in the pocket is now the fastest decision-maker in football.

And against Dallas, on a different night, he could have had as many as five touchdown passes. Five! He’s playing with confidence. He looks happy again in LaFleur’s offense. And it really started the week before, when he shredded Chicago for three hundred sixty-seven yards and two touchdowns at an eighty percent completion rate. One analyst said Jordan played absolutely out of his mind in that game and pointed out that the Bears came in with the third-ranked passing defense in the NFL. And Jordan was surgical against it.

That’s the decision that saved this season. Fix the line with veterans. Shuffle the pieces. Speed up the quarterback. And it worked.

Now let’s talk about the reason this offense has become so dangerous. Christian Watson and Matthew Golden.

Packers Nation, this might be the best young receiver duo in all of football right now. Coming into the Lions game, Watson ranked fifth in the NFL in receiving yards. Golden ranked ninth. And at one point, their combined eight hundred seventy-one yards was the best of any receiving duo in the entire league. Between them, they’ve put up nine touchdowns and well over a thousand yards. That’s not one guy carrying the load. That’s two guys taking turns embarrassing defensive backs.

And remember the criticism? When the Packers let Romeo Doubs walk in the offseason and extended Christian Watson instead, a lot of people laughed. They said Watson hadn’t earned a big contract. They said he couldn’t stay healthy. They pointed out that his career high was six hundred twenty yards in a season and seven touchdowns. Well, those comments look pretty regrettable now, don’t they? Watson has stayed healthy, and he’s proven every single dollar of that extension.

And then there’s Golden. After a quiet rookie season, he’s shaken that off and become receiver one-B in this offense. He’s already blown past last year’s total of forty-four targets. Packers fans finally remember why they were so excited when Green Bay drafted a receiver in the first round.

But here’s what makes them so scary. One word. Versatility. Both of them can go deep. Their average depth of target is exactly the same, fourteen point nine yards. But they can both win at all three levels of the field. Short, intermediate, deep. So defenses have to respect the deep ball from both sides of the field. And if you sit back to protect against the deep shot, you open up the underneath routes and the middle of the field. It’s basically a schoolyard game plan. If he doesn’t beat you, the other guy will.

And Jordan Love thinks there’s even more to come. After the Dallas game, he said his confidence is through the roof, and it comes from those guys making plays day in and day out. He said there were still plays they left out there, but every week they keep growing and getting better. And then he said the sky is the limit for this duo.

The sky is the limit. And now think about what happens with Josh Jacobs back. MarShawn Lloyd and Caleb Johnson have tried to hold the run game together, but neither has really sparkled. Adding Jacobs to Love, Golden, and Watson pushes this offense to a completely different level. It won’t stay in the middle of the pack for long. And remember, this team won four games while the offense was still sputtering. What happens when it’s fully firing?

Now the defense. Because this is the part that really makes me believe.

Before Detroit, the Packers had allowed fourteen points or fewer in three straight games. That was their longest streak since 2016. And it’s not about who they played. It’s about the man calling the plays. Jonathan Gannon has found something with this defense. Coming into Detroit, they ranked tenth in total defense, tenth in points allowed, and sixth in passing yards allowed. But even more important, they were strangling high-paid, high-hype offenses like Dallas and Chicago and finding ways to win.

Gannon pulls out every trick in the book. The Packers blitz at a thirty-three percent clip, twelfth in the league, and they mix it with confusing simulated pressures and different coverages on the back end. They’re making quarterbacks’ heads spin. That’s how you win battles.

And look at Lukas Van Ness. This guy looks like a completely different player. His three and a half sacks are already just half a sack behind his career best. A lot of people thought he was on his way out of Green Bay. Instead, he’s helped save the pass rush while Micah Parsons has been out. He’s adapted to the new scheme beautifully. That’s found money for the Packers.

On the back end, the safety duo of Xavier McKinney and Evan Williams looks fantastic. And the secondary even picked off Dak Prescott in that Dallas game. However you slice it, this is a defense good enough to scare anyone.

So why did Detroit happen? Look, Ford Field is one of the toughest places to play in this league. LaFleur said it himself before the game, it’s a hostile environment. The Lions were coming off a bye with extra rest. Jordan Love was dealing with a sore back after taking hits against Dallas. A starting linebacker was out. The NFC North is a bloodbath, and the Lions are one of the best teams in it. Losses like that happen to good teams. Even great teams.

But here’s what I need you to remember. A loss doesn’t undo the decision that turned this season around. The offensive line is still more stable than it was a month ago. Jordan Love is still getting the ball out faster than any quarterback in the league. Watson and Golden are still one of the best duos in football. Gannon’s defense is still one of the best units this team has had in years. Josh Jacobs is back. None of that disappeared in Detroit.

And there’s one more thing. The biggest thing of all. Micah Parsons.

Think about this. When the Packers swung that massive trade for Parsons, they were in everybody’s Super Bowl predictions. And so far, there’s been no real impact from him this season because he’s been out injured. He’s not quite ready to come back yet. But one analyst said it perfectly: when Micah comes back, that’s all you’re going to need. You’ve got enough on offense. You’ve got enough on defense. And then you add one of the best pass rushers in the entire NFL for the stretch run. For the divisional games. For the tiebreakers. For the playoffs.

Can you imagine this defense, already playing at a high level without him, with Micah Parsons coming off the edge? Opposite Lukas Van Ness, who’s having a career year? With McKinney and Williams behind them? That’s a nightmare for every quarterback in the NFC.

And one analyst asked the perfect question. What’s this team going to look like in December, when they play the Bears again on Christmas? With Jacobs running the ball, with the offense fully clicking, with Parsons back and healthy? That’s a scary thought for the rest of the league.

Another analyst said he loves what LaFleur is doing offensively, and that if Jonathan Gannon keeps it going, this team is going to be a big surprise down the stretch. But at what point does this team stop being a surprise? At what point do we just call them what they are?

A contender.

Because think about all the narratives we’ve heard this season. They can’t protect the quarterback. They can’t run the ball. They’re not getting enough from Jordan Love. The defense is missing something. One by one, those narratives have been disappearing. The quarterback is playing great. The line is better. The receivers are elite. The defense is legit. And the reinforcements, Jacobs and Parsons, are coming.

Will it be easy? No. The NFC North is brutal. Detroit just reminded us of that. Every division game is going to be a war. But this team has already proven that it can respond when it gets knocked down. A few weeks ago, everybody had them buried, and they ripped off three straight wins. Now they’ve taken a punch in Detroit. And I fully expect them to respond the same way.

So here’s what I want to see next. I want to see Josh Jacobs get more involved and give this offense the balance it needs. I want to see the offensive line keep building chemistry. I want to see Jordan Love keep getting the ball out quickly and keep trusting Watson and Golden. I want Gannon’s defense to keep dialing up pressure. And I want this team to come out angry. Because that’s what good teams do after a loss. They get mad, and then they get better.

The decision LaFleur and this staff made to fix the line, speed up the offense, and lean into what this team does well? It turned the season around once. It can carry them the rest of the way. One loss in Detroit doesn’t change who this team is.
