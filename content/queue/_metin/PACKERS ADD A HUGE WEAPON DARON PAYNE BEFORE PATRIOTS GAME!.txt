Packers Nation, the moment we’ve been waiting for since last December is finally here. Micah Parsons is back on the practice field.

After nearly eleven months of recovering from a torn ACL and additional meniscus damage, Green Bay’s superstar pass rusher practiced on Wednesday for the first time since his injury. And when he talked to reporters afterward, he ended with a message that should have every Packers fan fired up.

Let’s start with Micah. And let me be clear right away. Parsons has returned to practice. Not to game action. There’s a difference, and it matters.

The Packers have opened his twenty-one-day practice window, which runs through November twenty-fifth. And here’s the interesting part. That’s the same day Green Bay is scheduled to face the Los Angeles Rams. That Thanksgiving Eve matchup could become a possible target for his season debut. But nothing has been confirmed.

And Parsons himself isn’t making any promises. He explained that even his family has been asking if they should book flights to come watch him play. But he doesn’t want to announce a return date, build up everyone’s expectations, and then find out his body needs more time.

Honestly? I respect that a lot. The Packers need Micah Parsons healthy for the most important part of the season. Not rushing back just to hit a date on the calendar.

And then he said something that really caught my attention. Parsons admitted that his conditioning is probably the worst it’s been during his entire NFL career.

Now think about who we’re talking about here. A four-time All-Pro. A guy who had twelve and a half sacks in fourteen games last season. The first player in NFL history to start his career with five straight seasons of at least twelve sacks. That’s the player Green Bay gave up two first-round picks to bring in. That’s the guy who was supposed to transform this defense.

And he knows he’s not going to come back at that level right away. Last season, he averaged around fifty defensive snaps per game. He doesn’t expect to handle that kind of workload when he first returns. And when you think about everything he’s been through, that makes total sense. He missed training camp. He spent months rebuilding his knee. And the additional meniscus repairs had him on crutches for roughly seven or eight weeks. You can’t just flip a switch after all that.

But here’s the encouraging news. Parsons says his knee feels great. He said he wasn’t nervous about returning to the practice field at all. After more than three hundred days working toward this moment, he’s just excited to finally take the next step.

And he’s being realistic about what comes next. He expects some mistakes. He knows there will be plays where he looks like his old All-Pro self, and other plays where he struggles. But there’s one thing he refuses to compromise. His effort. If he needs to come off the field to catch his breath, he’ll do it. But whenever he’s out there, he’s going to give everything he has.

That’s exactly the attitude this defense needs.

And think about what this means for the team. Green Bay is five and three without its biggest pass-rushing weapon. And that’s especially encouraging when you remember how last season ended. After Parsons got hurt, the Packers lost their final five games. Five straight. This year, the defense hasn’t just survived without him. It’s thrived. Jonathan Gannon’s unit has allowed under fifteen points per game over the last four weeks. Ty’Ron Hopper is emerging as a star. Evan Williams is playing like the best safety in football. Lukas Van Ness is having a career year. Devonte Wyatt just had his best game of the season against Carolina.

Now imagine adding Micah Parsons to that.

Even with limited snaps, his presence changes everything. Quarterbacks have to account for his speed on every single dropback. Offensive tackles have to game-plan around him all week. Protection schemes get built around stopping him. And that means his teammates get more one-on-ones. Van Ness gets a little more room. Wyatt gets a softer double team. Blitzers come free because the line is too busy worrying about number one.

I’m not saying Parsons is going to dominate every snap the moment he steps back on the field. He’s the first one to warn against that. But even a limited version of Micah Parsons can make a difference in the biggest moments. One third-down sack ends a drive. One strip sack changes a game. And those moments become even more important in November and December.

Parsons understands that better than anybody. He recently explained to rookie Dani Dennis-Sutton why November football matters so much. His message was simple. November prepares teams for December. And December helps determine who they become heading into the playoffs.

That’s why he’s so excited about coming back now. He wants to help Green Bay finish strong. He wants to be part of the run.

Or would you rather give him a little extra time to get his conditioning back, so he’s at full strength for December and January? Alright, now let’s flip to the offense. Because while the defense is about to get a massive boost, the offense still has a problem that might need outside help. The run game.

We’ve talked about it a lot on this channel. Through eight games, Green Bay’s rushing attack ranks dead last in the league. Four hundred eighty-five rushing yards. The first team in NFL history to have a winning record through eight games while running for fewer than five hundred yards. As much as we all love watching Jordan Love sling it, the Packers can’t depend on the passing game forever. They need a run game that can control the clock, create manageable third downs, and keep defenses guessing.

And with the November tenth trade deadline right around the corner, the Packers are reportedly exploring their options at running back.

According to Easton Butler of Packer Report, Green Bay is among the teams that have contacted the Tennessee Titans about Tony Pollard. And Butler also reported Tennessee’s asking price. Roughly a third or fourth-round draft pick.

Now, let’s be clear. This is reported interest. Not a completed trade. There’s no confirmation that Green Bay has made a formal offer, or that Tennessee has agreed to move him. But the fit makes a lot of sense.

Look at what Pollard has done this season. Through eight games with Tennessee, he’s carried the ball one hundred thirty-three times for five hundred ninety-five yards and six touchdowns. That’s four and a half yards per carry. And he’s added fourteen catches for seventy yards. Those are solid numbers, and over the last few weeks, he’s been one of the better backs in the league.

Now compare that to what Green Bay has right now. MarShawn Lloyd leads the team with one hundred forty-nine rushing yards. Josh Jacobs is still working his way back into football shape. Chris Brooks is hurt. Pollard alone has more than the Packers’ leading rusher by over four hundred yards.

And the price is the most attractive part. A third or a fourth-round pick. That’s a lot more realistic than some of the blockbuster deals we’ve been hearing about.

Because of course, there’s still Jonathan Taylor. The Colts are reportedly willing to move him, and Green Bay is among the interested teams. But Taylor would be a much bigger investment. One speculative package could involve a second-round pick, a third-round pick, and potentially another selection on top of that. That’s a lot of draft capital. And remember, the Packers already gave up two first-round picks to get Micah Parsons.

So this is the decision Brian Gutekunst faces. Do you go for the biggest possible upgrade, knowing it could cost several valuable picks? Or do you target a more affordable player who can help the backfield without sacrificing the future?

Personally, that’s what makes Pollard so interesting to me. He doesn’t need to be the centerpiece of the offense to help this team. He doesn’t need to carry the ball twenty-five times a game. If he can just bring some consistency to the ground game, give Jordan Love a reliable option out of the backfield, and be a change of pace from what Green Bay already has, that alone makes him valuable.

But we can’t ignore the offensive line. Adding a talented back doesn’t automatically fix poor blocking. If defenders keep getting into the backfield before the runner can build any momentum, changing who’s carrying the ball won’t solve everything. That’s why the Packers have also been linked to Eagles guard Landon Dickerson. Fix the line and add a back like Pollard, and this run game could finally become respectable.

And remember, Josh Jacobs is already part of this backfield. Any addition has to fit next to him. Pollard would give the Packers another experienced option, but that doesn’t mean he’d automatically become the featured back. His role would depend on how Matt LaFleur and the offensive staff decide to use him. And with the deadline just days away, Gutekunst doesn’t have a lot of time to decide.

Now, while we’re talking about the trade market, there’s some news on the defensive side that changes things. One of the defensive tackles Green Bay was connected to is no longer an option. Washington’s Daron Payne suffered a serious foot injury in practice, and he’s been placed on injured reserve. Reports say Washington had been talking to one or more teams about a Payne trade before the injury. And the Packers were among the teams linked to him. So that door is closed.

That’s tough, because the Packers do need interior depth with Javon Hargrave out for the year. But there are still other names out there. Vita Vea from Tampa Bay. Moro Ojomo from the Eagles. Grover Stewart from the Colts. Roy Lopez, who already played for Jonathan Gannon in Arizona. So the defensive line could still get some help. And honestly, the biggest defensive addition might be the one that’s already in the building. Micah Parsons.

And then there’s one more interesting idea. The kicking game.

It might not generate the same excitement as a big offensive trade, but in close games, it could matter just as much. ESPN analyst Seth Walder suggested the Packers consider trading for the Indianapolis Colts’ kicker.

And after the last two games, can you blame him? Trey Smack missed field goals from twenty-six and thirty-five yards in the three-point loss to Detroit. Then against Carolina, he missed his first extra point off the upright. And late in that game, LaFleur chose to punt instead of trying a fifty-five yarder that could have sealed it. When your coach doesn’t trust your kicker in a big moment, that’s a problem.

LaFleur has publicly backed Smack. He’s said they’ve seen enough of a sample size to believe in him. And the team gave up two draft picks to move up and get him. But in a season where so many games come down to a field goal, the Packers can’t afford to keep leaving points on the field. Whether it’s a trade or just Smack getting back on track, this has to get fixed.

So let’s put it all together. Here’s where things stand.

Micah Parsons is back at practice. His practice window runs through November twenty-fifth, and the Rams game on Thanksgiving Eve could be a target for his debut, but nothing is confirmed. His conditioning isn’t where he wants it yet, and he won’t play his usual workload right away. But his knee feels great, his effort will be one hundred percent, and he’s ready to go.

The Packers have reportedly contacted Tennessee about Tony Pollard, and the asking price is around a third or fourth-round pick. That’s a realistic, affordable way to help the worst run game in the league.

Jonathan Taylor is still out there, but at a much higher price.

Daron Payne is off the market after a season-ending injury.

And the kicking situation is still a question mark.

And with the Patriots up next, the timing couldn’t be bigger. This is a team that’s five and three, with an elite defense, a quarterback who’s been carrying the offense, and a superstar pass rusher on his way back. If Gutekunst can add one or two pieces to fix the run game before the deadline, this team could be getting stronger on both sides of the ball at exactly the right time.

November prepares you for December. December decides who you are in January.
