Let’s start with the scene. LaFleur walks into the locker room. The players are cheering. And the first thing he hits them with is history. Most points ever scored by an opposing team in Gillette Stadium history.

Think about that building, Packers Nation. Gillette Stadium. Where the Patriots built a dynasty. Where they won game after game for two decades. Where opponents went to lose. And the Green Bay Packers walked in there and scored more points than any visiting team ever has. That locker room had every right to celebrate.

And then LaFleur went through the night, piece by piece. And as he did, three huge storylines jumped out.

Great news number one. This offense just made franchise history.

LaFleur called out his quarterback first. And he called it what it was. A career night. Jordan Love went twenty-three of twenty-nine for three hundred seventy-two yards and three touchdown passes. The locker room exploded when LaFleur read those numbers.

And remember, that’s not even counting the rushing touchdown. Jordan scrambled for a thirteen-yard score on the opening drive, right after Xavier McKinney picked off Drake Maye on New England’s very first offensive play. Four total touchdowns for Jordan Love. And on his five-yard touchdown pass to Matthew Golden, Jordan reached one hundred career touchdown passes.

Three hundred seventy-two yards. Nearly eighty percent completions. A career night against a Mike Vrabel defense that was supposed to take away the deep ball. And on throws that traveled at least twenty yards in the air, he went six of nine for two hundred thirty-five yards.

After the game, an analyst on national TV who admitted he’d criticized Jordan Love more than anybody actually apologized on air. He said Jordan is delivering the goods. And he said Jordan is playing himself into the MVP conversation.

But here’s the stat from the locker room that really blew me away. LaFleur turned to his two receivers. Matthew Golden and Christian Watson. And he told the team that they became the first duo in Packers history to each have seven or more receptions, one hundred fifty or more yards, and a touchdown in the same game.

The first duo in team history, Packers Nation.

Think about all the great receivers who’ve worn green and gold. All the great receiving pairs. Sterling Sharpe. Antonio Freeman. Robert Brooks. Donald Driver. Greg Jennings. Jordy Nelson. Randall Cobb. Davante Adams. And none of them ever did this as a pair in the same game. Until Matthew Golden and Christian Watson did it in Foxborough.

And think about where both of them have come from this season. Matthew Golden came into this game leading the entire NFL in drops. Nine of them. He’d been dealing with shoulder and foot injuries. People were questioning him. And on Sunday, he had six catches for one hundred twenty-six yards and a touchdown in the first quarter alone. That’s the most receiving yards by a Packer in an opening quarter since Antonio Freeman back in 1998. Jordan hit him for gains of forty-three and forty-nine yards before that milestone touchdown.

And Christian Watson? Less than two weeks ago, he broke a rib against Carolina. He was taken to the hospital in an ambulance. He came back and finished that game with over a hundred yards. And then, after a ten-day break, he came out against New England and scored a thirty-nine yard touchdown in the third quarter, right after the Packers recovered a Rhamondre Stevenson fumble. That’s how you punish a defense after a turnover.

So now you’ve got a quarterback in the MVP conversation, and two receivers who just made franchise history together. That’s great news number one.

Great news number two. And this is the one I’ve been waiting all season to talk about. The run game finally showed up.

You know how bad it’s been. We’ve talked about it in almost every video. The Packers came into this game dead last in the entire NFL in rushing. Four hundred eighty-five rushing yards through eight games. The first team in NFL history with a winning record through eight games while running for fewer than five hundred yards. Against Carolina, the worst run defense in the league, Green Bay ran twenty-six times for sixty-six yards. Half of those carries gained one yard or less. CBS Sports ranked the Packers dead last in expected points added per carry.

And in the locker room, LaFleur even made a joke about it. He started by saying, how about this, we rush for zero. And everybody laughed. Because for most of this season, that’s what it’s felt like.

But then he gave them the real number. Thirty-two attempts. One hundred twenty-nine yards. Two rushing touchdowns.

Thirty-two carries for one hundred twenty-nine yards and two touchdowns, Packers Nation! Against a New England defense whose whole identity is building a wall up front. That locker room went crazy, and it should have.

Is it perfect? No. That’s about four yards a carry. But compare that to what we’ve been watching all year. Compare it to two and a half yards a carry against Carolina. Compare it to seventeen rushing yards against Atlanta back in week three. This is a massive step forward.

And it matters more than just the yards. When the Packers can run the ball even a little bit, everything else gets easier. Play-action starts working. Safeties have to creep up. And that’s exactly when Golden and Watson get loose deep. That’s how you end up with a quarterback throwing for three hundred seventy-two yards and two receivers making franchise history. The run and the pass work together. That’s the whole idea behind Matt LaFleur’s offense.

And LaFleur also pointed out that someone scored the first touchdown of his career in this game. The locker room loved that. Those are the moments that build a team.

Now, there’s one concern we have to mention. Josh Jacobs left the game early with a hand injury. And that has to be watched. Jacobs brings physicality and toughness, and LaFleur has talked about the edge he brings to this team. But here’s the encouraging part. Even after Jacobs went out, the Packers kept running the football. The backs behind him stepped up. That tells you something about how this team is starting to come together.

And today is trade deadline day. The Packers have been linked to Tony Pollard from Tennessee, with a reported asking price around a third or fourth-round pick. They’ve been linked to Jonathan Taylor, Tyjae Spears, and on the offensive line, Landon Dickerson. After one hundred twenty-nine rushing yards against the Patriots, maybe Brian Gutekunst feels a little less pressure. But with Jacobs’ hand to monitor, adding another back could still make a lot of sense. We’ll see what happens.

But for now, that’s great news number two. The run game is finally breathing.

Great news number three. And Packers Nation, I genuinely did not expect to be saying this. The special teams were perfect.

LaFleur said he couldn’t say enough about his special teams. And he started with Trey Smack.

And when he said Smack’s name, the whole locker room started cheering. Because they all know what this kid has been through.

Think about Trey Smack’s last few weeks. Against Detroit, he missed a twenty-six yard field goal and a thirty-five yarder that got blocked, in a game the Packers lost by three. Six points left on the field in a three-point loss. Then against Carolina, his first extra point clanked off the upright. And late in that game, LaFleur chose to punt instead of trying a fifty-five yarder. Coming into the Patriots game, Smack had made eleven of sixteen field goals and missed three extra points. ESPN analysts were openly suggesting the Packers trade for another kicker. Fans were calling for a change.

And what did Trey Smack do in New England?

Seven for seven.

Perfect. Every single kick went through. LaFleur stood in front of the team and said it out loud, seven for seven today. And his teammates celebrated him.

That’s huge. Because kickers are some of the most psychologically fragile players in football. Their entire job is to succeed in isolated moments with everyone watching. A rookie who’s missing kicks can carry that doubt into his next attempt. But a rookie who goes seven for seven on the road, in a big game, with his teammates cheering for him afterward? That’s how confidence gets rebuilt. LaFleur said a couple of weeks ago that they’d seen enough of a sample size to believe in Smack, and that it was a next-game mentality. Well, the next game came. And Smack was perfect.

And it wasn’t just Smack. LaFleur shouted out the punter for a fifty-three and a half yard average. That’s flipping the field every time. And there was a forty-eight yard return that gave the offense a short field. Special teams can feel like a footnote sometimes. But when you’re winning field position, scoring on every kick, and making big returns, it adds up. It wins games.

So that’s your triple dose of great news. A historic offensive night. A run game that finally showed up. And a perfect special teams performance.
