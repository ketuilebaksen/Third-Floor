Packers Nation, it’s bye week. No game this Sunday. But don’t let that fool you. Because behind the scenes in Green Bay, Matt LaFleur and Brian Gutekunst have been busy. Very busy. And after that thirty to ten beatdown by the Minnesota Vikings at Lambeau Field, the decisions this team makes over these next few days could decide whether this season gets back on track, or keeps sliding.

A familiar face cut from the roster and then brought right back. A dynamic returner activated from injured reserve, but with a question mark still hanging over his ankle. A six-foot-seven defensive tackle from Nigeria with one of the most fascinating journeys you’ll ever hear. A three-day break that LaFleur gave this team right after the loss. And the biggest question of all. Will Micah Parsons finally make his debut on Thanksgiving Eve?

Welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’re breaking down every bye week move, what each one means, and what has to happen before the Los Angeles Rams come to town. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with where this team stands. Because after the Vikings game, the mood around Green Bay is heavy.

Thirty to ten. At home. Against a division rival that already beat the Packers once this season. Back in September, Minnesota took over late, piling up points in the fourth quarter to win by seventeen. This rematch was supposed to be the moment Green Bay showed it had learned something. Instead, the Packers fell to six and four. And they’re one and three in the NFC North.

We went through the full breakdown earlier this week, but let’s quickly recap what went wrong. Because these are the problems LaFleur has to fix during this bye.

Matthew Golden dropped at least three balls, according to SB Nation’s count. Two of them came back to back, on second and six and then on the very next play, both chances to move the chains. Golden still led the team with five catches for seventy yards on nine targets. That’s respectable production on paper. But when Jordan Love is fighting for every single clean throwing window, the missed catches matter just as much as the completed ones. Golden has the talent to be a reliable weapon. Remember, one week earlier he had one of the best first quarters in Packers history. Now he needs the consistency to match.

Tucker Kraft was targeted four times and caught only one, for nineteen yards. And SB Nation questioned his energy and intensity. Now, we shouldn’t pretend we know what’s going on inside Tucker Kraft’s head. He’s coming back from last year’s torn ACL, but there’s no confirmed explanation connecting that injury to Sunday’s struggles. Still, one catch is nowhere near the impact this offense needs from him.

Mark Redman fumbled the Packers’ first completed pass of the game. Another costly mistake right out of the gate, when the offense desperately needed stability.

Trey Smack missed a fifty-three yard field goal. Nobody expects every long kick to be automatic. But in a game where points were so hard to come by, leaving three on the field hurt. Especially when Minnesota scored on its very next drive.

And the biggest concern of all. The offensive line. Minnesota sacked Jordan Love eight times. Eight. It’s almost impossible to build a passing game when your quarterback barely has time to set his feet. Routes get disrupted. Drives stall. Even manageable situations turn into emergencies.

Center John Williams had an especially rough afternoon, including multiple botched snaps. One of them came on Green Bay’s opening possession of the second half, and the Vikings recovered it. Imagine coming out of halftime needing a spark, and handing the ball right back to your opponent instead. That’s how a bad game turns into a disaster.

And this can’t fall entirely on the players. LaFleur, offensive coordinator Adam Stenavich, and offensive line coach Luke Butkus had already seen Minnesota’s pressure package earlier this season. They knew the challenge was coming. So why did the protection collapse again? Was it communication? Assignments? Individual matchups? Or all of the above? LaFleur admitted after the game that the team didn’t coach well enough. He called it a bad decision not to go to the quick passing game earlier. Now the coaches owe this team answers.

And that’s where the bye week comes in.

Now let’s get to the decisions. And the first one involves a guy you might not know a lot about, but who’s already made an impact this season. Coleman Owen.

Here’s what happened. On Saturday, the Packers waived wide receiver Coleman Owen from the fifty-three-man roster. Part of the reason was to make room for Skyy Moore, who was being activated from injured reserve.

And Owen had been stepping up while Moore was out. When Moore went down with an ankle injury against the Bears, Owen filled in. He contributed as a receiver, and as both a kick returner and a punt returner.

So Owen was waived. And then he cleared waivers, meaning no other team claimed him. And right after that, reporter Easton Moscua noted that Green Bay had opened up a spot on the practice squad by releasing rookie defensive lineman David Blay Jr. At that point, bringing Owen back made a lot of sense. But it wasn’t a done deal.

Then on Tuesday, November seventeenth, the Packers made it official. Coleman Owen re-signed with the practice squad. He’s back in the organization.

Now, that doesn’t guarantee he’ll play against the Rams. But here’s why it matters. The return game still needs reliable options.

Look at what Owen did in four games for Green Bay this season. He returned kickoffs for one hundred five yards, an average of twenty-six point three yards per return. And he returned punts for thirty-six yards, averaging nine yards per return. Those aren’t superstar numbers. But they show that he’s already handled a job the Packers might need filled again. And soon.

Because here’s the thing about Skyy Moore. He’s back on the active roster. But his ankle is still a concern after the Minnesota game.

And this is where LaFleur’s decision comes in. On Monday, LaFleur said the team would give the players a three-day break. And he said that after that break, the team expected to have a clearer medical picture on Moore by Friday.

Think about the reasoning behind that decision. After a loss like that, a lot of coaches would want to grind their team into the ground at practice. Make them pay for it. But LaFleur chose to give his guys three days off. To rest. To heal. To reset mentally. Because this team is beat up. Jordan Love took eight sacks and twelve hits. The offensive line is banged up. Skyy Moore’s ankle needs time. And the stretch run is coming, with seven games left and every one of them mattering.

That’s a smart decision. You can’t fix anything with a broken-down roster. You get healthy first. Then you go to work.

And if Moore’s ankle can’t handle return duties against the Rams, Owen is right there as an option. Now, that’s a possible game-day role. Not an announced decision. But it gives the Packers a backup plan.

And remember how much LaFleur loves Skyy Moore as a returner. Before the Vikings game, he called him a big-time weapon. He said if Moore’s able to go, it’ll definitely boost the return game. Before Moore got hurt, he was averaging sixteen yards per punt return and twenty-seven and a half yards per kickoff return. In a close game, a big return can flip everything. So getting Moore healthy is a big deal. But having Owen as insurance is smart planning.

And there’s another detail that makes the Owen move interesting. It’s about practice squad elevations.

Here’s how it works. Teams can elevate a player from the practice squad to the game-day roster a limited number of times, without having to sign him to the active roster. Each player gets three standard elevations. Owen was elevated once before he joined the active roster. So he still has two standard elevations left. That means if Green Bay needs him on game day, they can call him up without taking up a permanent roster spot. That’s real flexibility.

But other practice squad players are running low. Tight end Drake Dabney was elevated in weeks nine and ten, so he only has one standard call-up left. And edge defender Arron Mosby was elevated three times, in weeks six through eight. He’s used up all of his standard elevations. So if the Packers want to use him again, they’d need a different roster solution, like signing him to the active roster.

These little rules might seem boring. But when injuries start piling up, and this team has had a ton of them, these small details can have big consequences. Every roster spot matters.

Now let’s talk about the most fascinating move of the week. Because Green Bay didn’t just bring back Coleman Owen.

On November seventeenth, the Packers also officially signed Nigerian defensive tackle Haggai Ndubuisi to the practice squad, in a corresponding move. And general manager Brian Gutekunst announced the transactions.

So let’s be clear. This isn’t just a workout guy anymore. Ndubuisi is a confirmed addition. He’s part of the NFL’s International Player Pathway Program, which brings in players from around the world to develop in the league.

And his story is incredible.

At the program’s Pro Day back in 2023, a source measured him at six-foot-seven, three hundred twenty-three pounds, with thirty-six-inch arms. Thirty-six-inch arms! That’s an enormous wingspan. The Packers’ current roster lists him at six-foot-seven and two hundred ninety-six pounds. Those numbers are from different points in time, so it looks like he’s reshaped his body since then.

And here’s the journey. He trained in London for six months, working on both sides of the line. He actually entered the NFL as an offensive lineman in 2022. And then in 2023, he switched over to defense.

And since then? He’s been with the Arizona Cardinals. The Denver Broncos. The Washington Commanders. The New England Patriots. The Houston Texans. The Tampa Bay Buccaneers. And the San Antonio Brahmas of the UFL. That’s seven organizations. He was active for one Texans game last season and recorded an assisted tackle.

Think about that perseverance. From Nigeria to London to a whole string of NFL teams and the UFL, switching positions along the way, and still fighting for his spot. That’s somebody who loves football and refuses to quit. And now he’s in Green Bay.

Why does this matter for the Packers? Because the defensive line depth has been hit hard. Javon Hargrave is out for the season with a torn Achilles. Warren Brinson has been dealing with a long-term injury. And LaFleur has praised the defensive tackles for doing the dirty work, eating up blockers so others can make plays. But they need bodies. A six-foot-seven tackle with long arms is exactly the kind of raw tool you want to develop. And remember, Gutekunst is a front office that believes in developing players and finding hidden gems. Maybe Ndubuisi is the next one.

But let’s be realistic. Signing two practice squad players isn’t going to magically erase eight sacks. It’s not going to fix the dropped passes. And it’s not going to undo a twenty-point home loss. These are depth moves. Smart ones. But depth moves.

The real work happens with the guys already on the roster. And that’s why this bye week is so important.

Now let’s talk about the game everybody’s circling. The Los Angeles Rams. Wednesday, November twenty-fifth. Thanksgiving Eve. Eight o’clock Eastern, seven o’clock Central, on Netflix. A national stage. A chance to bounce back in front of the whole country.

And the biggest storyline? Micah Parsons.

Parsons has been back at practice for a few weeks now. He got work in eleven-on-eleven periods before the Vikings game. He even bull-rushed Tucker Kraft in practice. But LaFleur held him out against Minnesota, saying he looks great but just doesn’t have enough reps under his belt yet. And he was dealing with an illness that week too.

Here’s the key detail. His twenty-one-day practice window closes on November twenty-fifth. The same day as the Rams game. The Packers have to activate him by then. So the timing lines up almost perfectly. Reports have said Green Bay is targeting a possible return against the Rams. But it’s not confirmed. LaFleur has said getting Parsons back is the goal, but he hasn’t guaranteed it. And if Parsons does play, he’d likely be on a limited snap count at first while he shakes off the rust.

But think about what even a limited Micah Parsons could mean. This defense is already one of the best in the league at taking the football away. Xavier McKinney leads the NFL with six interceptions. Evan Williams is playing at an All-Pro level. Brandon Cisse was just named Defensive Rookie of the Month. And Jonathan Gannon keeps confusing quarterbacks with pressure from every angle. Now add a four-time All-Pro pass rusher coming off the edge. Opposing quarterbacks would have to account for him on every single dropback.

That’s the kind of boost that could turn this season around. Right when the Packers need it most.

And there’s more good news on the horizon. Josh Jacobs had surgery on his fractured finger, and reports suggested he could be back within two to three weeks. Kaleb Johnson has stepped up in his absence, averaging over four yards per carry against both New England and Minnesota. Zaire Franklin should be back from his shin fracture soon. And Sean Rhyan has been working his way back at center, which would help stabilize that offensive line.

So here’s what the Packers have to do with this bye week.

Number one. Fix the protection. Find the best five offensive linemen, settle on them, and get them on the same page. Communication against the blitz has to be better. And the coaches have to have the quick passing game ready from the very first drive.

Number two. Hold onto the football. Four turnovers against Minnesota. That can’t happen again. Not the fumbles. Not the botched snaps. Not the drops.

Number three. Get healthy. LaFleur’s three-day break was the first step. Skyy Moore, Jordan Love, the offensive line, everybody needs to come out of this bye fresh.

And number four. Get Micah Parsons ready. If he can go against the Rams, even on a limited snap count, it could change everything.

This bye week is a chance to hit the reset button. The season isn’t over. Not even close. There are seven games left. The Packers are six and four. And in a tight NFC North, with the Wild Card in play, there’s still plenty of football to play.

But the Packers have to look far more prepared against Los Angeles than they did against Minnesota. Jordan Love needs protection. The receivers need cleaner execution. And this entire team needs to show up ready to fight on Thanksgiving Eve.
