Packers Nation, there’s a stat floating around right now that tells you everything about this season. The Green Bay Packers are five and three. And they’ve run for just four hundred eighty-five yards through eight games. According to the research, that makes them the first team in NFL history to have a winning record through eight games while rushing for fewer than five hundred yards.

That’s history. But it’s not the kind of history anyone wants to make.

Welcome back to Third Floor. And today we’re talking about the move that could finally fix it. Because with the trade deadline less than a week away, Green Bay is being linked to Philadelphia Eagles guard Landon Dickerson. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler specifically connected him to the Packers. The Athletic named Green Bay as a strong landing spot. And Packers Nation, I’m telling you right now, if this deal is there to be made, Brian Gutekunst should make it in a heartbeat.

Now, let me be clear. No trade has happened yet. These are reports and links. But they’re coming from serious sources, and the fit makes a ton of sense. So today we’re going through everything. How bad the run game has really gotten. Why Dickerson is the perfect target. The defensive tackles on the radar. The running backs worth a look. And my priority list for the deadline. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the problem. Because to understand why Dickerson matters, you have to understand just how broken this run game is.

Think about what that stat really means. Most teams with a run game this bad are sitting near the bottom of the standings. Because offenses that can’t run the ball usually can’t stay on schedule. They can’t protect leads. They can’t take pressure off their quarterback. Green Bay is somehow the exception. And that says a lot about Jordan Love. He’s dragging this offense to a winning record almost on his own. But it also says this team is leaving a whole lot on the table.

And it’s not getting better. It’s getting worse. Bill Huber of Packers On SI pointed out something brutal after Thursday night’s game against Carolina. Half of Green Bay’s twenty-six rushing attempts gained one yard or less. Half! You’d think a run game this bad would improve a little bit just from randomness. A defense blowing a gap here and there. Instead, it’s going the other direction. And remember, Carolina came into that game with the worst run defense in the NFL.

Now, the problem has two sides. And we have to be honest about both.

First, the running backs haven’t been good enough. MarShawn Lloyd leads the team with one hundred forty-nine rushing yards on fifty-seven carries. That’s well under three yards a carry. Josh Jacobs, after missing the first six weeks, still doesn’t look like he’s back in full football shape. Chris Brooks is currently hurt. And Pierre Strong has barely been used. Honestly, I think he deserves more chances. When nothing is working, you at least owe it to yourself to see if a different back can find something the others can’t.

But second, and this is the bigger issue, the offensive line hasn’t been good enough either. It’s really two sides of the same coin.

Look at the PFF run-blocking grades for Green Bay’s offensive linemen. Of the ten who’ve played meaningful snaps, the highest is Sean Rhyan at sixty-two point five. He’s the only one above sixty. And Rhyan is now out with a high ankle sprain. Most of the rest of the group is in the fifties. A couple are in the forties. Aaron Banks is in the thirties.

That’s not a line that can create consistent running room for anybody. You could put the best running back in the league behind that group, and he’d still be fighting through contact in the backfield on a lot of snaps. That’s why I keep saying the fix has to start up front. A new running back alone won’t solve this.

And that brings us to Landon Dickerson.

Dickerson started this season on injured reserve and made his debut in Philadelphia’s recent Monday night game against Dallas. And there’s a strong case that the Eagles will be sellers at this deadline. They’ve won just two games. Jalen Hurts is out for the season. And they’ve lost six straight. This looks like a year where Philadelphia tries to recoup some draft capital and resets for 2027.

And Jeremy Fowler specifically linked Dickerson to the Packers. According to Fowler, something feels off between Dickerson and the Eagles. He’s one of the remaining holdovers from Jeff Stoutland’s offensive line, and he recently made what looked like a passive-aggressive comment about the front office and his long-term future with the franchise. Fowler noted that the Eagles are in a position to collect future picks and are getting younger up front.

He also mentioned that a mix of inconsistent play and injuries could affect Dickerson’s value. But he said Dickerson still has elite traits, and that the guarantees in his contract run out after this season. And here’s the encouraging part. Dickerson held up well in his return against the Cowboys, which should ease at least some of the injury concerns.

Fowler also mentioned the Chargers as a possible fit. But given how their season has gone, I’d be surprised if Los Angeles became a buyer. The Packers, on the other hand, are clearly in buy mode.

And The Athletic also identified Dickerson as one of the players most likely to be dealt, naming Green Bay as a strong landing spot along with the Raiders, Vikings, Patriots, and Broncos.

So why am I so fired up about this one Because I’ve watched this team miss on the interior line once already. Quenton Nelson was right there. The Packers were reportedly among the finalists. And then Seattle went out and traded for him for a third and a fifth. The Packers cannot afford to miss on the interior line again. Every week they wait, Jordan Love takes more hits, and this offense gets more one-dimensional.

And the price should be manageable. Based on the reporting, Dickerson’s cap hit for the rest of this season would be relatively modest. And Green Bay has plenty of day-three draft capital to work with, including multiple fourth and fifth-round picks. Remember, they’ve got the extra fourth from the Rashan Gary trade, plus their own picks, plus projected compensatory picks. They can make a strong offer without touching anything premium.

To me, this is about as close to a no-brainer as deadline deals get. And of all the teams linked to Dickerson, the Packers might be the most desperate. Because what they’re doing on the ground is historically bad.

Now, let’s talk about how much difference one lineman can actually make. Because it’s more than most people think.

A great guard doesn’t just win his own matchup. He helps the center next to him. He helps the tackle beside him. And he gives the running back a clean read on where the hole is going to be. When the interior line is shaky, everything breaks down a split second earlier. Defenders get into the backfield. Cutback lanes disappear. And the running back is making decisions under pressure before he even gets started.

And think about this line right now. Sean Rhyan is out. Jacob Monk is on IR. John Williams, a guard, is playing center. Travis Glover went down with a knee injury. Laken Tomlinson got moved to right guard with barely any practice reps there. Kevin Zeitler hasn’t played a snap. That’s chaos. Adding a player with Dickerson’s power and experience could stabilize the entire front.

And it would help Jordan Love in pass protection too. Love has been taking hits all season. A strong, physical guard in the middle means a cleaner pocket. It means Jordan can step up and throw instead of getting pushed off his spot. It means more time for Christian Watson and Matthew Golden to get open downfield.

Are there risks Of course. Dickerson has dealt with injuries, and Fowler himself noted that his play has been uneven at times. There’s also a scheme question. Matt LaFleur’s run game leans heavily on zone concepts, which ask linemen to move laterally and work together on combination blocks. Dickerson is at his best as a powerful, physical guard who moves people at the point of attack. But I think a player with his talent can adapt. And LaFleur has shown he can build in more gap and power concepts when the personnel calls for it. So it’s something to keep in mind, but for the price it would likely take, the upside far outweighs the risk.

Now let’s move to the defensive side, because the Packers have been linked to some names there too.

The one that keeps coming up is Vita Vea from Tampa Bay. There have been reports that Green Bay called the Buccaneers about him. That would be a fun, aggressive move. Vea might be the single best defensive tackle available right now. But that also makes him the most expensive. His contract isn’t ideal either. I’d love to get him for a fourth-round pick, but I don’t see Tampa letting him go that cheaply, especially with the Bucs still alive in the NFC South.

And yes, the Packers need a defensive tackle. Depth there has taken a hit. Javon Hargrave, who Green Bay signed in free agency expecting a steady veteran presence, is out for the year. Devonte Wyatt had his best game of the season on Thursday night, and Karl Brooks has been very consistent. But the rotation needs another body. And in the second half of the season, defensive line depth matters even more. Bodies wear down. Injuries pile up. And the teams that make deep playoff runs usually have enough interior linemen to keep everyone fresh into January.

The Athletic listed some more realistic options. One I really like is Moro Ojomo from the Eagles. He tends to fade into the background behind Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis, who get more attention and bigger paychecks. But Ojomo has been about as consistent as they come, and he wouldn’t cost much. And if the Packers are already talking to Philadelphia about Dickerson, why not package them together

Grover Stewart from the Colts is another veteran option. I would have preferred DeForest Buckner, but he’s already headed back to San Francisco. Roy Lopez is an interesting fit because he’s played for Jonathan Gannon before in Arizona. He’d know the system right away. And A’Shawn Robinson and Dalvin Tomlinson are other cheaper veterans who could add experience to the room. Daron Payne from Washington has also been connected to Green Bay in recent reports.

And notice the pattern here. Almost every one of these players is on a team expected to sell. That’s great news for Green Bay. Because sellers usually accept less as the deadline gets closer. Teams that are out of the race would rather get a mid-round pick now than watch a veteran walk away in free agency.

Now, running back. The other half of the run game equation.

Kenneth Gainwell was listed as a potential fit, and I wouldn’t mind him as a depth option. But I think the Packers need a more reliable second back. Every time MarShawn Lloyd comes into the game, I find myself bracing for a negative play. And that’s not a feeling you want with your number two running back.

Alvin Kamara and Tyjae Spears were also mentioned. I’ll admit, I was high on Kamara during the offseason, and I was wrong. He just doesn’t have the burst he used to. Between those two, I take Spears without much hesitation. He’s a different style of runner, and Green Bay needs variety. Right now, the backs on this roster are too similar.

But the name I keep coming back to is Tony Pollard. He’s having a strong season, and over the last few weeks, he’s been one of the best running backs in football. He’d give the Packers a pass-catching option out of the backfield and a real change of pace from what they already have. And because he’s on a team that could be selling, the price might be reasonable. Tennessee already moved Jeffery Simmons to Chicago, which tells you exactly where that franchise’s focus is. Pollard is the kind of veteran a rebuilding team would rather turn into a draft pick than keep around for a season that’s slipping away.

So what’s my priority list Here it is.

Offensive line first. Then running back. Then defensive tackle.

And I’d resist the temptation to chase the biggest name on the market. There’s plenty of buzz about Jonathan Taylor, and he would be a fantastic addition. The Colts are reportedly willing to move him. But a bidding war for a star running back could cost premium picks. And the Packers already gave up two first-round picks for Micah Parsons. On top of that, a star running back behind this line still wouldn’t fix the core problem.

But if the Packers can land Landon Dickerson and a back like Tony Pollard without giving up premium picks I think this team becomes a lot more dangerous in the second half of the season.

Because think about where this team is. Even with the worst run game in the league, Green Bay is five and three and in the playoff mix. That’s largely because of Jordan Love, who’s carrying an offense with almost no ground support. Two touchdown passes in seven of eight games. And because of the defense, which has been playing at an elite level even without Micah Parsons. Jonathan Gannon has turned that unit into the identity of this team.

Now imagine what this team looks like with even an average run game. Not great. Just average. Defenses couldn’t drop extra players into coverage. Play-action would become a real weapon. Jordan Love wouldn’t have to win every single game with his arm alone. Christian Watson would see more one-on-one shots. And when you can run the ball late in games, you can actually close them out, instead of watching a seventeen-point lead turn into a nail-biter like we saw against Carolina.

And that matters even more as the calendar turns. November, December, January. Cold weather at Lambeau. That’s when you need to run the football. That’s when the teams that can grind out yards on the ground win playoff games.

So here’s my message to Brian Gutekunst. You made the bold move for Micah Parsons. You made the smart hire with Jonathan Gannon. Now make the practical move that fixes the biggest hole on this roster. Go get Landon Dickerson. Add a running back like Tony Pollard if the price is right. Add a defensive tackle if you can. And give Jordan Love and this team a real shot at something special.
