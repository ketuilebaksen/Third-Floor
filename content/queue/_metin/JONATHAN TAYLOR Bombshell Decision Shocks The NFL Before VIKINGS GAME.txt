The decision is in. And it’s a stunner. The Indianapolis Colts are two and seven. They spent the last few weeks tearing their roster apart. Quenton Nelson to Seattle. DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. Keenan Allen to the 49ers. Josh Downs to the Chiefs. A full-blown fire sale.

And yet, when the trade deadline passed on Tuesday afternoon, Jonathan Taylor was still a Colt.

Packers Nation, welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’re breaking down the decision that shocked the league at the deadline, what it means for the Green Bay Packers, and why it lands at the worst possible time, with Josh Jacobs heading for surgery and the Minnesota Vikings coming up on Sunday. We’re also going through the brutal NFC North race, the short-yardage problem that has to get fixed, and why this team might still be the most dangerous in the division. Let’s get into it.

Before we start, smash that like button, subscribe to Third Floor, and turn on notifications. And drop a comment right now: should the Packers have offered more for Jonathan Taylor? Yes or no?

Let’s start with what happened. According to NFL insider Tom Pelissero, the Colts declined to trade Taylor before Tuesday’s deadline. And here’s the part that really jumps out. Multiple teams offered a second-round pick and more for Taylor. And the Colts turned them all down. They wanted a blockbuster offer to even consider it.

Think about that. A team at two and seven, a team that’s sure to miss the playoffs again, a team that just shipped out its best guard, its best defensive tackle, and two receivers. And it said no to multiple second-round offers for a running back.

Why? A few reasons came out. Owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon’s preference all along was reportedly to keep Taylor, who’s the face of the franchise. Taylor never asked for a trade. And CBS Sports pointed out that the Colts may have been overcorrecting after not getting full value for Quenton Nelson, who went to Seattle for just a third and a fifth. They weren’t going to let that happen twice. So unless someone came with a first-round-level offer, Taylor was staying.

And this decision surprised a lot of people. Because just a day before the deadline, almost everyone thought a deal was coming. Mike Florio went on NBC before Sunday Night Football and said a Taylor trade was expected before four o’clock Eastern on Tuesday. He listed the teams linked to him. The Giants. The Vikings. The Seahawks. The Broncos. The Packers. Maybe even someone unexpected like the 49ers. And Florio believed the Colts would just take the best offer.

The only reported offer at that point was a second and a fifth from the Giants. And that offer was reportedly pulled off the table. Minnesota was reported to be out. And Taylor himself sounded like a guy who was saying goodbye. After his last game, he told Indianapolis reporters that if it was his last game there, he was very grateful to them and to the city. And when he was asked if he’d requested a trade, he said it didn’t really matter.

Those didn’t sound like the words of a player expecting to stay. But in the end, he’s staying. At least through the rest of 2026. The Colts may revisit it in the offseason, when a new general manager could decide his future.

So what does this mean for the Packers?

It means the biggest running back on the market is off the table. And it means Green Bay has to figure out the run game with what it has. Right when its starting running back is about to go under the knife.

Let’s talk about that. Because the Jacobs news makes this whole situation so much more urgent.

In the first half of the Patriots game, Josh Jacobs fractured a finger. He reportedly hurt it on a stiff arm, when his fingers got caught in a defender’s facemask. He couldn’t grip the football. And if a running back can’t grip a football, he can’t carry it, and a back who can’t carry it isn’t helping you. That’s why the Packers kept him out of the rest of the game.

At first, it sounded like it might be a short-term thing. Maybe two weeks. Maybe three. But then on Monday, LaFleur announced that Jacobs needs surgery. He said Jacobs had no grip strength in that hand. And LaFleur didn’t rule out injured reserve. If Jacobs goes on IR, he’d have to miss at least four games.

So now think about the timing. The run game was already the weakest part of this roster. Then the starting back gets hurt. Then the best back on the trade market stays put. That turns what was a want into something much closer to a need. And the depth chart has to fill a hole that isn’t easy to fill.

And here’s the frustrating part. Sunday’s game in New England actually gave us a glimpse of what this team looks like when the run game works.

Let’s back up. After the first three weeks of the season, a lot of people questioned whether this team was any good. Then the Packers went four and one through October, and people started to believe. And the thinking was simple. If this team ever figures out the run game, it has an elite defense, a quarterback playing at an MVP level, and the formula to beat anybody.

And on Sunday, the Packers ran the ball better. That was the lesson. They went over one hundred rushing yards for the first time all season in week nine, after being one of the worst rushing offenses in NFL history through the first eight weeks. Thirty-two carries, one hundred twenty-nine yards, two touchdowns. And it looked like the offensive line might have finally found a combination that works, with Laken Tomlinson at left guard and Kevin Zeitler at right guard.

Now, let’s not be naive. One hundred yards doesn’t mean the run game is fixed. It doesn’t make it a top-five unit. But it showed what’s possible. And now, with Jacobs out, the Packers have to try to repeat it without him.

And this matters so much because of where the Packers sit in the standings. Let me say this sentence and let it sink in. The Green Bay Packers are six and three. And they’re in last place in their own division.

Think about how strange that sounds. This team has an MVP-level quarterback. An elite defense that hasn’t even had Micah Parsons yet. Two receivers who look unguardable. And it’s sitting at the bottom of the NFC North.

Look at that division going into this week. Minnesota at six and one, with the best defense in the league, and playing on Monday night with a chance to get to seven and one. Chicago at seven and two, even after Caleb Williams threw four interceptions on Monday Night Football the week before, and they still won. That tells you how hot the Bears feel right now. Detroit at six and two. And Green Bay at six and three, dead last.

Imagine being in the NFC South this year. But the Packers aren’t. They’re in the best division in football. And right now, it looks like all four teams could make the playoffs.

And that’s why every division game is basically a playoff game now. When four teams are bunched together and they all look like playoff teams, one loss in the division can swing seeding, or even decide who gets in at all. The gap at the top isn’t huge. One hot stretch changes everything. And that’s exactly why so many people wanted Green Bay to add a difference-maker before the deadline.

And look around the league. The other contenders were loading up. San Francisco traded for DeForest Buckner and added Keenan Allen. Seattle traded for Quenton Nelson. Jacksonville traded for Jessie Bates. Chicago, in our own division, traded two first-round picks for Jeffery Simmons. Kansas City got Josh Downs. The teams that people view as real contenders were all making big moves to fix their weaknesses.

And here’s the case that was made for Green Bay before the deadline. The Packers’ run game is their only real weakness. Everything else is there. An MVP-level quarterback. Two receivers playing at an All-Pro level. An elite defense about to get Micah Parsons back. And at some point, there’s going to be a night when Jordan Love is off. If that night comes at a bad time and you can’t run the football, you won’t win that game. You have to be able to run.

That’s why Jonathan Taylor was such a legitimate conversation for Green Bay. A back who forces defenses to put an extra defender in the box makes everything easier. Play-action. Third-down conversions. Closing out games. It’s not just about the rushing total. It’s about how the whole offense opens up.

But the Colts said no. And maybe, honestly, that was always going to be the answer. The Packers don’t have a first-round pick in 2027 after the Micah Parsons trade. If Indianapolis wanted a blockbuster, Green Bay probably didn’t have the ammunition to make it work anyway, at least not without hurting the future.

So now let’s talk about what the Packers do have. And it’s still a lot.

Let’s start with Jordan Love. Is he the MVP? I don’t think that’s an overreaction at all. What Love has been doing all season needs to be talked about way more than it is. The level he’s playing at, with almost no run game, is unbelievable.

Think about how much harder his job is than most quarterbacks’. Think about Jared Goff in Detroit. When defenses play the Lions, they have to respect Jahmyr Gibbs and that whole run game. Think about Caleb Williams in Chicago. Defenses have to respect Kyle Monangai and D’Andre Swift. But when defenses play the Packers, they know they can keep fewer guys in the box all game, because the Packers haven’t been able to run for much. And Jordan Love still puts up huge numbers anyway.

Against New England, he went twenty-three of twenty-nine for three hundred seventy-two yards and three touchdowns, plus a rushing touchdown. On national TV afterward, an analyst who admitted he’d criticized Love more than anyone actually apologized and said Love is playing himself into the MVP conversation.

It’s rare to see a quarterback play at this level without help from the run game. Quarterbacks with a strong running back get easier play-action and more predictable defensive looks. Jordan Love doesn’t get that. And he still delivers. That’s why the MVP argument has real weight. And if he has to carry this offense again without Jacobs, it’s only going to get stronger.

Next, the receivers. And honestly, I think Matthew Golden might be one of the best receivers in the league right now. What he did to Christian Gonzalez, one of the best cornerbacks in football, was remarkable. He made Gonzalez look completely lost.

And then there’s Christian Watson. How do you defend these two? They both have the speed to take the top off a defense. So you want to play off them. But you can’t play too far off, because they’re both phenomenal route runners. Especially Golden, who can come at you at full speed, stop on a dime, and turn a five-yard hitch or a four-yard out into an easy completion.

They’re unguardable right now. Remember, against New England, Golden and Watson became the first duo in Packers history to each have seven catches, one hundred fifty yards, and a touchdown in the same game. At this point, I’m just praying for their health, so they can keep this going for all eighteen weeks. Because right now, both of them are playing like top receivers in football.

And here’s how that ties back to the run game. When you have two receivers who can win deep and win underneath, defenses have to spread out. That should create space for whoever is carrying the ball. The pieces fit together. Even without Jacobs, even without Taylor, the receivers can help create running lanes, just by forcing defenses to respect them.

Then there’s Jonathan Gannon. And I’ll say it. He might be Matt LaFleur’s best hire ever. That’s not an overreaction. It’s just true. Look at what he’s doing without Edgerrin Cooper and without Micah Parsons. Parsons is one of the most impactful defensive players in the NFL, and he hasn’t played a single snap. And this defense is still playing like a top-five unit. Four takeaways against New England. Four sacks.

A defensive coordinator who can play at that level without his best player has built a scheme that works because of coaching, not just talent. And that means when Parsons comes back, the ceiling goes even higher. Parsons is back at practice, and his window runs through November twenty-fifth.

But there’s one thing LaFleur absolutely has to fix. And it’s going to matter even more now without Jacobs. Short yardage.

On Sunday, it honestly felt more comfortable watching the Packers on third and six or third and seven than on third and two. That’s how bad it’s gotten. The offensive line wasn’t getting a push. And some of the play calling in those spots was questionable. There were a couple of horizontal plays, like a screen and a jet sweep to Savion Williams, in situations where you just need two yards. And there were two drives in a row with third and two where the Packers either punted or turned it over on downs. There were even issues just getting the tush push snapped without a false start.

You have to convert those. You have to keep drives going.

And think about how a defense approaches third and two against a team that can’t run. It either sells out against the run, or it plays the pass and dares you to run it. Either way, the Packers haven’t had a reliable answer. When a team has a reliable short-yardage play, defenders have to hesitate. When it doesn’t, they attack. That’s why this problem is as much about personnel as it is about play calling. And without Jacobs, a physical back who could grind out those tough yards, it gets even harder.

So who steps up? MarShawn Lloyd. Kaleb Johnson. Pierre Strong Jr. Chris Brooks, who was designated to return from injured reserve on November fourth. And maybe Savion Williams, the big, physical player LaFleur wants to be the goon. Someone has to be the guy who gets the tough yards. And they have to figure it out fast.

Because Minnesota is next. On November fifteenth. The Vikings beat the Packers in week one, scoring twenty-nine unanswered points after Green Bay led twenty-two to ten. They have the best defense in the league. And in a division where every game is a playoff game, this is a huge one. If Green Bay wins, it climbs right back into the race. If it loses, the hole gets deeper.

So let me pull it all together.

The Packers are six and three. They’re in last place in the best division in football. They have a quarterback who deserves MVP talk, a pair of receivers who might be the best duo in the league, and a defense that’s about to get even better with Micah Parsons. They have one real weakness, the ground game. And that weakness just got worse because of Josh Jacobs’ fractured finger and upcoming surgery.

At the same time, the rest of the contenders loaded up at the deadline. And the one running back who could have changed everything, Jonathan Taylor, is staying in Indianapolis, after the Colts turned down multiple second-round offers and held out for a blockbuster.

Is that a disaster for Green Bay? No. The pieces are still here. The question marks are narrow. And this team has already shown it can win with Jordan Love carrying the load. But the margin for error just got a little smaller. And now LaFleur and this coaching staff have to find a way to run the football, convert short yardage, and close out games without their lead back.

Great teams respond. And this is Green Bay’s moment to respond.
