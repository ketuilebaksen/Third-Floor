Packers Nation, the Packers got the win. Twenty-seven to nineteen over the Carolina Panthers on Thursday night. Five and three on the season. But if you watched that fourth quarter, you know this one should have everybody in Green Bay asking some serious questions. Because a seventeen-point lead turned into a nail-biter. And late in the game, with a chance to put it away, Matt LaFleur made a decision that told us a lot about where this team really stands. He chose to punt instead of trying a fifty-five yard field goal.

Welcome back to Third Floor. Today we’ve got a lot to break down. The heroes from Thursday night, including a performance from Christian Watson that’s honestly hard to believe. A young linebacker who just announced himself to the league. The pass rush that took over. And then the problems. The kicker. The run game. The fourth quarter collapse. And finally, the trade market, where the Colts are now reportedly willing to move Jonathan Taylor, and another running back option is getting a lot of attention. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the winners. And there’s no other place to begin than Christian Watson.

Packers Nation, this is one of the toughest things I’ve seen a player do in a long time. Watson had already caught six passes for one hundred yards in the second quarter. Then, right after making a fifty-three yard reception, he suffered a chest injury. He was taken to the hospital for evaluation. Doctors found a broken rib. Fortunately, the tests showed no internal bleeding, and he was cleared to return.

And then he came back. He made it back to Lambeau Field, got back into the game, and finished with eight receptions for one hundred thirteen yards. With a broken rib.

That was his third game this season with at least one hundred receiving yards and a catch of fifty yards or longer. That’s exactly the kind of explosive production this offense needs. And with Matthew Golden still dealing with drops, Watson has become even more valuable to Jordan Love. He’s set a career high in receiving yards, and we’re only eight games in.

But his health has to come first. The good news is that Green Bay now has a ten-day break before the next game. That’s huge for a guy playing through a broken rib. Get healthy, Christian. This team needs you for the stretch run.

Winner number two. Ty’Ron Hopper.

With Edgerrin Cooper out for the season and Zaire Franklin missing another game with that hairline fracture in his shin, the linebackers had to step up. Hopper and Isaiah McDuffie had to carry the load. And Hopper delivered one of the best performances of his career. Eight tackles. One sack. One interception. Three passes defended. And two quarterback hits.

That stat line is ridiculous. And the interception was beautiful. Hopper deflected the pass at the line and then caught it himself. That’s awareness. That’s instincts. That’s a playmaker. And he contributed against the run and as a blitzer too.

Remember what LaFleur said about Hopper a couple of weeks ago? That he’s maximized every rep he’s been given. That his development has been pretty cool to watch. That the coaches could tell early in training camp he was on his way. Well, he’s arrived. For a defense dealing with all these injuries, seeing a young linebacker play with that kind of confidence is a massive positive.

Winner number three. The defensive front.

This group was all over Bryce Young. Lukas Van Ness combined with a rookie pass rusher on a crucial third-down sack that forced Carolina to settle for a field goal. Van Ness also teamed up with Devonte Wyatt for an important tackle for loss. Wyatt got a sack of his own. Brenton Cox and Barryn Sorrell also got to the quarterback. And the rookie kept making plays in the backfield all night.

Young rarely looked comfortable. And Jonathan Gannon’s defense, even without Cooper, without Franklin, without Hargrave, and without Micah Parsons, kept Carolina under pressure all game long.

And here’s a stat I absolutely loved. Jordan Love wasn’t sacked once. Not one time. After all the struggles this offensive line has had, after losing Sean Rhyan and Jacob Monk at center, after Travis Glover went down, after the shuffle that moved Laken Tomlinson to right guard with barely any practice reps there, Jordan Love stayed clean all night. That deserves real recognition.

Now. The disappointing side of the game.

Loser number one. The kicking situation.

After missing field goals from twenty-six and thirty-five yards in Detroit, Trey Smack needed a confidence-building night. Instead, he missed his very first extra point attempt against Carolina. Off the upright.

And that brings us to LaFleur’s decision. Late in the game, with a chance to effectively seal the win with a fifty-five yard field goal, LaFleur chose to punt instead. Now, fifty-five yards is a long kick for anybody. You can make the case that pinning Carolina deep was the smart call. But you can’t ignore what that decision says. When you’re not confident in your kicker, it changes everything. It changes your fourth-down decisions. It changes your field goal range. It changes how you manage the end of games.

LaFleur has publicly backed Smack. He’s said they’ve seen enough of a sample size, and that it’s a next-game mentality. But his actions on Thursday night told a different story. And Brian Gutekunst has to be asking himself some hard questions. The team traded two draft picks to move up and get this kicker. Right now, he’s costing them points and costing the coaching staff trust.

Loser number two. The run game. Again.

Josh Jacobs had thirty-nine rushing yards on twenty-one carries. That’s fewer than two yards per attempt. And as a group, the Packers’ running backs averaged fewer than two yards per carry. Against a Carolina defense that came in as the worst run defense in the entire NFL.

The only rushing play longer than six yards? An eighteen-yard run by Savion Williams. That’s it.

The blocking simply isn’t creating room. Jacobs can still make plays. He’s still a talented back. But even a great running back needs a little space to work. And right now, he’s getting hit almost as soon as he touches the football. That puts even more pressure on Jordan Love and the passing game. And it’s why so many fans are frustrated that Kevin Zeitler, one of the best run-blocking guards of his generation, still hasn’t played a snap.

Loser number three. Fourth-quarter execution.

The Packers led twenty-seven to ten in the second half. That should have been a comfortable finish. Instead, Carolina cut the lead to twenty-seven to nineteen and got one more chance to tie the game.

Look at Green Bay’s final three offensive possessions after that third-quarter touchdown. An interception. A three-and-out. And another three-and-out. One of those possessions actually lost fifteen yards and burned less than a minute off the clock. And for the game, the Packers turned the ball over three times.

That’s exactly how you let a team back into a game you should already have locked up. And while LaFleur’s play calling deserves some scrutiny, this wasn’t only about the calls. Poor execution and mistakes played a big role. The defense ended up protecting the lead, which is great. But Green Bay can’t keep relying on late defensive stops to survive. We saw this movie last season, with leads slipping away in the second half. Jordan Love himself said finishing was the biggest lesson of the offseason. On Thursday, the defense finished. The offense didn’t.

So here’s the summary. Hopper looked like a real defensive playmaker. The pass rush was outstanding. Watson was heroic. Love stayed clean. But the run game and late-game execution remain serious concerns. And that leads us straight into the trade deadline.

The deadline is November tenth. And the Packers are being connected to two very different running back possibilities.

The first is Tony Pollard of the Tennessee Titans.

In an October twenty-ninth article for Clutch Points, writer Enzo Flojo proposed a trade that would send Pollard to Green Bay. Now, that’s important to understand. It’s a hypothetical proposal from a writer. It’s not a confirmed negotiation between the two teams. But from a football perspective, the idea makes a lot of sense.

Through seven games, Pollard had rushed for four hundred seventy-eight yards at four point three yards per carry, with four rushing touchdowns. Against Cleveland, he put up one hundred twenty-nine rushing yards and three touchdowns. That’s the kind of explosiveness Green Bay has been missing.

Tennessee was sitting at one and six when the article was published, which naturally raises the question of whether the Titans would sell veterans for future picks. The proposed price was a 2027 fourth-round pick and a conditional 2028 sixth-rounder that could become a fifth if Pollard hits certain rushing or scrimmage yardage marks. For Green Bay, that’s a pretty manageable price.

Pollard is twenty-nine, and he’d give LaFleur another option next to Jacobs. He brings acceleration, receiving ability, and the ability to turn short plays into big gains. Jacobs could stay the primary physical runner, and Pollard could be the change of pace and another threat on passing downs. That would help Jordan Love by forcing defenses to account for more than just the pass.

But here’s the catch. Pollard wouldn’t automatically fix the offensive line. If defenders keep breaking into the backfield before running plays can develop, changing running backs won’t solve everything. That’s why I like the idea only if the price stays reasonable. A fourth and a conditional late-round pick? Worth considering. Giving up premium draft capital for a short-term addition? Much harder to justify.

Now the second possibility. And this one is even more intriguing. Jonathan Taylor.

We’ve talked about Taylor before. On October twenty-fifth, a Packers Wire article discussed reporting from ESPN’s Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport identifying Green Bay among the teams that had considered, or were expected to consider, a Taylor trade, along with Seattle, Denver, and Minnesota.

But now the situation has developed. According to an October thirty-first NFL Network report, Indianapolis is willing to trade Taylor, and discussions with multiple teams are active and ongoing. Green Bay is among the teams reported to have shown some level of interest. And the New York Giants have entered the picture too.

That’s a significant development. But let’s be clear. It does not mean a trade to the Packers is agreed upon. It means Taylor is available, and Green Bay is in the mix.

And Taylor’s production is outstanding. Through the first six weeks, he had four hundred ninety-two rushing yards, seven touchdowns, and twenty-three catches. And he’s kept producing since then. The latest reporting credits him with five hundred eighty-seven rushing yards and a league-leading eight rushing touchdowns. At twenty-seven years old, he’s one of the most dangerous running backs in the league.

But unlike a Pollard deal, Taylor would be a much bigger commitment. He signed a two-year, forty-four million dollar extension before this season, including a fifteen million dollar signing bonus. He’s under contract through 2028, with a little over sixteen million due in 2027 and nearly eighteen million in 2028. And previous reports said teams were being told the price was a first-round pick and more. Remember, the Packers already traded away two first-round picks to get Micah Parsons.

So the question becomes, which move actually makes sense?

Here’s how I see it. Taylor is the bigger name, the bigger talent, and the bigger splash. Picture him in this backfield with Love, Watson, Kraft, and Golden. That’s a scary offense. But the cost in picks and money would be huge, and he’d still be running behind the same line that’s holding Jacobs under two yards a carry.

Pollard is the smaller move. Cheaper, short-term, less risky. He wouldn’t transform the offense, but he’d add a different kind of speed and receiving ability, and he wouldn’t cost the future.

And neither of them fixes the root problem by themselves. The offensive line has to be better. Whether that means finally playing Kevin Zeitler, adding an interior lineman, or both, that’s where the real fix starts.

Because think about where this team is. Five and three. The defense is legit. Hopper is emerging. The pass rush is getting home. Micah Parsons is expected to start practicing soon. Jordan Love is playing at a high level and wasn’t sacked once on Thursday. Christian Watson is playing through a broken rib and still putting up a hundred yards. This team is good. Really good. But it has one glaring weakness, and every opponent knows it.

Fix the run game, and this team becomes a real problem for the rest of the NFC. Don’t fix it, and you’ll keep seeing fourth quarters like Thursday night, where a big lead turns into a sweat because the offense can’t run the clock out.

So my message to Brian Gutekunst? Be aggressive, but be smart. If the Colts’ price for Taylor comes down to something reasonable, take a hard look. If not, a Pollard-type move plus a real fix up front might be the better path. Either way, don’t stand still. The deadline is eight days away, and the rest of the NFC is loading up.
