Six days. That’s all the time Brian Gutekunst has left. Six days until the NFL trade deadline slams shut. And when it does, the Green Bay Packers will either have fixed the one thing holding this team back, or they’ll spend the rest of the season wondering what could have been.

Because I truly believe there’s a single deal sitting on the table right now that could change the entire direction of this season. And his name is Landon Dickerson.

Let me start by painting you a picture of what Jordan Love has been dealing with this season. Because I don’t think people fully understand it.

Every Sunday, Jordan Love walks into the huddle knowing one thing. The run game isn’t going to help him. Not on first down. Not on second down. Not when the team needs to grind out the clock in the fourth quarter. He knows that if this offense is going to move the football, it’s going to be on his arm. And the defense across the line knows it too.

Think about what that does to a quarterback. Defenses don’t have to respect the run. They can drop seven, eight guys into coverage. They can sit on Christian Watson deep. They can bracket Matthew Golden. They can send pressure up the middle because they know the handoff isn’t a threat. And Jordan has to beat all of it. By himself. Every single week.

And he’s doing it. Two touchdown passes in seven of eight games. Five wins. A team in the playoff race. But here’s the stat that tells you how unusual this is. The Packers have run for just four hundred eighty-five yards through eight games. And according to the research, they’re the first team in NFL history to have a winning record through eight games while rushing for fewer than five hundred yards.

Let that sink in. In the entire history of the National Football League, no team has ever done what this team is doing. Winning with a run game this bad. That’s not luck. That’s Jordan Love. That’s Jonathan Gannon’s defense. That’s a team finding a way.

But you can’t keep finding a way forever. Not in November. Not in December. And definitely not in January.

Because here’s what happens when you can’t run the football late in the season. You can’t protect leads. We saw it against Carolina. A twenty-seven to ten lead in the second half turned into a twenty-seven to nineteen sweat, because the offense went interception, three-and-out, three-and-out. One of those drives actually lost fifteen yards and burned less than a minute off the clock. When you can’t run, you can’t close. And when you can’t close, good teams come back and beat you.

And it’s getting worse, not better. Against Carolina, the team with the worst run defense in the NFL, half of Green Bay’s twenty-six rushing attempts gained one yard or less. Half. That’s not a bad day. That’s a broken system.

So where does it break? Let’s be honest. It’s not one thing. The running backs haven’t been good enough. MarShawn Lloyd leads the team with one hundred forty-nine yards on fifty-seven carries, well under three yards a pop. Josh Jacobs still doesn’t look like he’s in full football shape after missing six weeks. Chris Brooks is hurt. Pierre Strong has barely touched the ball.

But the bigger issue is the men up front. Of the ten Packers offensive linemen who’ve played meaningful snaps, only one has a PFF run-blocking grade above sixty. Sean Rhyan, at sixty-two point five. And Rhyan is now out with a high ankle sprain. Most of the rest of the group is in the fifties. A couple are in the forties. Aaron Banks is in the thirties.

And look at what this line has gone through. Rhyan down. Backup center Jacob Monk on IR. John Williams, a guard, snapping the ball at center. Travis Glover out with a knee injury. Laken Tomlinson moved from left guard to right guard with barely any practice reps there. Kevin Zeitler still hasn’t played a snap. You cannot build a run game on that kind of chaos.

You could put the best running back in the world behind that group, and he’d still be getting hit in the backfield. That’s the truth. And that’s why the fix has to start up front.

That brings us to Landon Dickerson.

Let me tell you why this name matters so much right now. Dickerson started this season on injured reserve with the Philadelphia Eagles. He made his debut in Philadelphia’s recent Monday night game against Dallas. And he held up well, which eases at least some of the concerns about his health.

But the bigger story is what’s happening in Philadelphia. The Eagles have won just two games. Jalen Hurts is out for the season. And they’ve lost six straight. This is a team that looks ready to sell. To collect draft picks. To reset for 2027.

And ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler specifically linked Dickerson to the Packers. According to Fowler, something feels off between Dickerson and the Eagles. He’s one of the last holdovers from Jeff Stoutland’s offensive line. And he recently made what looked like a passive-aggressive comment about the front office and his future with the franchise. Fowler pointed out that the Eagles are positioned to collect future picks and are getting younger up front. And he noted that while injuries and uneven play could affect Dickerson’s value, he still has elite traits. And the guarantees in his contract run out after this season.

The Athletic went even further, naming Dickerson as one of the players most likely to be traded, and listing Green Bay as a strong landing spot, along with the Raiders, Vikings, Patriots, and Broncos.

So the player is available. The team is selling. The fit is obvious. And the price should be reasonable. Based on the reporting, Dickerson’s cap hit for the rest of the season is relatively modest. And Green Bay has plenty of day-three draft capital to work with. Multiple fourth and fifth-round picks. An extra fourth from the Rashan Gary trade. Projected compensatory picks. The Packers can make a strong offer without touching anything premium.

Now, I want you to remember something. Because this is the part that keeps me up at night.

A few weeks ago, Quenton Nelson was available. One of the best guards in football. The Packers were reportedly among the finalists. And then Seattle traded for him. For a third-round pick and a fifth-round pick. That’s it. A third and a fifth, for an elite guard, while Green Bay’s run game was sitting dead last in the league.

The Packers missed that one. They cannot miss again.

Every week they wait, Jordan Love takes more hits. Every week they wait, this offense gets more one-dimensional. Every week they wait, the margin for error gets thinner. And with a seven-point total point differential through eight games, there is no margin for error left.

So what would Dickerson actually change? More than you think.

A great guard doesn’t just win his own matchup. He makes everyone around him better. He helps the center with double teams. He helps the tackle next to him on stunts. He gives the running back a clean read on where the hole is going to be. When the interior is strong, the running back can be patient. He can press the hole, set up his blocks, and cut. When the interior is weak, everything happens a split second too early. The defender is already in the backfield. The cutback lane is gone. The back is running for his life.

Dickerson is a big, powerful, physical guard. A guy who moves people at the point of attack. Put him on this line, and suddenly you’ve got a real anchor in the middle. Josh Jacobs gets a crease. Pierre Strong or MarShawn Lloyd get a lane. The play-action game starts to work. And Jordan Love gets a cleaner pocket to step into.

Now, is there risk? Of course. Dickerson has dealt with injuries. His play has been uneven at times. And there’s a scheme question. Matt LaFleur’s run game leans heavily on zone concepts, where linemen have to move laterally and work together. Dickerson is at his best in more of a power, downhill style. But talent adapts. And LaFleur has shown he can build more gap and power runs into the plan when his personnel calls for it. For the price this would likely cost, the upside is massive and the risk is small.

And here’s the thing. Dickerson doesn’t have to be the only move. He just has to be the first one.

If Gutekunst wants to go further, there are options. At running back, Tony Pollard from Tennessee has been one of the best backs in football over the last few weeks. He catches the ball out of the backfield, he’s a change of pace from what Green Bay already has, and the Titans, who already traded Jeffery Simmons to Chicago, look ready to sell. Tyjae Spears is another name worth a look, a different style of runner for a backfield that’s too similar right now.

On the defensive line, depth is getting thin with Javon Hargrave out for the year. Vita Vea from Tampa would be the dream, but he’ll be expensive. Cheaper options include Moro Ojomo from the Eagles, who could even be packaged with Dickerson in the same deal, Grover Stewart from the Colts, and Roy Lopez, who already played for Jonathan Gannon in Arizona.

And then there’s Jonathan Taylor. The biggest name on the market. The Colts are reportedly willing to move him. But the price could mean premium picks, and the Packers already gave up two first-rounders for Micah Parsons. And a star running back behind this current line? He’d still be getting hit in the backfield. You have to fix the line first. That’s why Dickerson comes before Taylor. Every time.

Now let me show you why this matters so much. Let me show you what this team could become.

Right now, without a run game, the Packers are five and three. Jordan Love is carrying the offense. The defense has allowed under fifteen points per game over the last four weeks. Ty’Ron Hopper is turning into a star. Evan Williams is playing like the best safety in football. Lukas Van Ness is having a career year. Christian Watson is playing through a broken rib and still putting up a hundred yards. And Micah Parsons is expected to return to practice soon.

That’s a good team. A really good team. With one glaring hole.

Now fill that hole. Add Landon Dickerson to the line. Get Kevin Zeitler and Travis Glover healthy and back in the mix. Let Josh Jacobs get back into football shape behind real blocking. Maybe add a Tony Pollard to change the pace.

Suddenly, defenses can’t just sit back in coverage anymore. They have to respect the run. Safeties creep up. Linebackers stay home. And Jordan Love, the guy who’s been beating defenses with one hand tied behind his back, gets both hands free. Play-action shots to Watson. Crossers to Golden. Tucker Kraft working the middle. Savion Williams being the goon.

And on the other side, Micah Parsons comes back. Coming off the edge next to Van Ness. With Hopper, McKinney, and Evan Williams behind them. Gannon dialing up pressure on every down.

That’s not a five and three team anymore. That’s a team nobody wants to see in January.

Picture it. Late December. Snow falling at Lambeau. The Packers are up by ten in the fourth quarter. And instead of throwing the ball and hoping, they hand it off. Again and again. Four yards. Five yards. First down. The clock keeps running. The other team never gets the ball back. Game over.

That’s what a run game does. It doesn’t just add yards. It closes games. It protects leads. It wins in the cold. And it gives your quarterback the help he’s earned.

So here’s my message to Brian Gutekunst. You made the boldest move in the league when you traded for Micah Parsons. You made the best hire in the league when you brought in Jonathan Gannon. Now make the most important move of all. The practical one. The one that fixes the hole everybody can see.

Go get Landon Dickerson.

Because Jordan Love has carried this team long enough. It’s time to give him some help. And it’s time to turn the first team in NFL history to win with a run game this bad into the team that wins it all.
