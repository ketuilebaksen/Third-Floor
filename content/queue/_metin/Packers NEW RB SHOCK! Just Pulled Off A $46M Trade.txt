Packers Nation, the trade deadline has come and gone. And while the rest of the NFL was making moves, the Green Bay Packers made exactly zero trades. Not one. And yet, there’s a name floating around Green Bay right now that has fans buzzing. A three-time Pro Bowl running back. A contract with forty-six million dollars left on it. And a backfield that could look completely different down the road.

His name is James Cook.

Welcome back to Third Floor. And before anybody runs to change their jersey order, let me be crystal clear right from the start, because that’s how we do it on this channel. The Packers have not traded for James Cook. There’s no deal. There’s no confirmed negotiation. And there’s no promise that Green Bay will chase him this offseason. What we have is a trade idea from a writer back in October. But it’s an idea that tells us a lot about what this team needs, and what it might look like in the future.

So today we’ve got two big stories. First, what Green Bay actually did at the deadline, the surprising roster move they made instead of a trade, and the running back room heading into the Vikings game. And then we’ll dig into the James Cook idea. The production. The contract. The fumbling problem. And whether it could ever really happen. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with the deadline. Tuesday, November tenth. The last day teams could make trades during the 2026 season. According to Zach Kruse of Packers Wire, six trades were reported around the league that day. And Green Bay stayed completely out of the action. Despite all the questions about the offensive line. Despite all the questions about running back depth. Despite Josh Jacobs heading for surgery.

And if you were hoping for a big splash, the team’s only announced move that day was a much smaller one. The Packers signed rookie tight end Kenny Fletcher Jr. to the practice squad.

Now, this one is official. The team confirmed it. So it’s not a rumor. But it’s also definitely not a blockbuster answer to the rushing problems.

So who is Kenny Fletcher Jr.? He’s six-foot-three and two hundred fifty pounds. He went undrafted out of Rutgers. And here’s the interesting part of his story. He actually switched from defensive end to tight end before the 2024 season. Across forty-one college games, he caught forty-six passes. That unusual background makes him a developmental prospect, not an established receiving threat.

He spent training camp with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, then joined the Indianapolis Colts’ practice squad during the first month of the season. In Tampa’s preseason, he played thirty-two offensive snaps. Twenty of those were as a run blocker. He ran just ten routes and didn’t catch his only target.

So what does that tell you? It tells you blocking experience might be one big reason Green Bay wanted him. The team hasn’t announced a specific role, but think about what this offense has been missing. Push in the run game. Help in short yardage. A tight end who’s spent time on the defensive line knows a thing or two about moving bodies at the point of attack. And with the short-yardage struggles we’ve seen this season, a guy like that could eventually have value.

Fletcher joins Drake Dabney on the practice squad. Dabney had been elevated to the game-day roster the week before. And on the active roster, the Packers already have Tucker Kraft, Mark Redman, Jonnu Smith, and Josh Whyle at tight end. Luke Musgrave remains on the physically unable to perform list with a neck injury. And remember, LaFleur said a few weeks ago that until proven otherwise, he doesn’t expect Musgrave back this season, though he’d never close the door on it.

Now, let’s talk about the running backs. Because this is where things get really interesting.

Josh Jacobs suffered a fractured ring finger in the win over New England and needs surgery. LaFleur said Jacobs had no grip strength in that hand. That raised a lot of uncertainty about his availability and whether he might need a stint on injured reserve. If he goes on IR, he’d have to miss at least four games. But as of now, there’s no confirmed IR placement. And there’s no decision to move on from Jacobs.

Behind Jacobs, the Packers have MarShawn Lloyd, Kaleb Johnson, Chris Brooks, and Pierre Strong Jr. Brooks has been working his way back from injured reserve after the team designated him to return on November fourth. And that creates a potential numbers game. Healthier bodies eventually mean pressure on the fifty-three-man roster.

Analysts like Bill Huber have raised the possibility of some difficult roster decisions coming. And Pierre Strong’s name has come up as a potential casualty of that crunch. A release, or another roster move, could be considered if Green Bay needs room. But let me be clear. That’s a possibility. Not a transaction. Pierre Strong has not been cut.

And honestly, I’d be careful about cutting running back depth right now, with Jacobs facing surgery and the Vikings coming to town. You need bodies. You need options. And earlier this season, plenty of fans were asking for Strong to get more chances, because when nothing is working, you owe it to yourself to see if a different back can find something.

So that’s what Green Bay actually did. No trades. A developmental tight end on the practice squad. And a running back room that’s about to be tested without its lead back.

Which brings us to the big story. The name that has everybody talking. James Cook.

Cook is the Buffalo Bills’ running back. A three-time Pro Bowler. And he entered the Packers conversation because of a Heavy article written by Max Dible back on October fourteenth.

Now, here’s the key timing detail. That article was written before the November trade deadline. And it imagined a possible in-season trade. The deadline has now passed without that happening. So any future trade would have to wait until the offseason, when teams can make deals again. And so far, neither team has been shown to be working on anything.

So why did James Cook even come up in the first place?

Because of how bad Green Bay’s run game was at the time. According to that October report, the Packers were averaging only sixty point four rushing yards per game at that point. The lowest in the entire league. With just two rushing touchdowns all season. And both of those came against the Chicago Bears.

Those numbers are from that October snapshot, not an updated November ranking. But they explain why a writer would imagine a dramatic move. When your run game is that bad, you start dreaming big.

And James Cook had the production to make that dream tempting. Through the five games cited in the article, Cook had rushed for five hundred thirty-two yards and five touchdowns. That’s five point six yards per carry. Five point six! He also had seven catches for thirty-one yards.

Now imagine that kind of production in Green Bay. A back who rips off five and a half yards every time he touches the football. Behind Jordan Love, who’s playing at an MVP level. Next to Christian Watson and Matthew Golden, who just became the first duo in franchise history to each go for seven catches, one hundred fifty yards, and a touchdown in the same game.

Defenses would have nowhere to hide. Play two safeties deep to stop Watson and Golden, and Cook gashes you on the ground. Bring a safety down to stop Cook, and Jordan Love hits someone over the top. That’s the scariest version of this offense. That’s the dream.

And Heavy described Cook as having four years and forty-six million dollars remaining on his contract, including the 2026 season. That’s a big commitment, but for a three-time Pro Bowler at a premium position for this team’s biggest weakness, you can understand why people would talk about it.

But there’s a big concern attached. And we have to talk about it. Ball security.

In Buffalo’s thirty-seven to thirty-four overtime win over the Los Angeles Rams, Cook fumbled twice and lost one. According to that October report, he had four fumbles through five games, with two lost. At that point, that was the most in the league.

And Bills head coach Joe Brady publicly warned that if those mistakes continued, it could cost Cook carries. At the same time, Brady also acknowledged how well Cook was running the football.

And that’s an important distinction. A coach demanding better ball security is not the same thing as a team putting a player on the trade market. Buffalo was frustrated with the fumbles, but that doesn’t mean they wanted to get rid of him.

And for Green Bay specifically, ball security matters a ton. Think about what just happened with Josh Jacobs. He’s having surgery precisely because he can’t grip the football. LaFleur said a running back has to secure the ball through contact. So if the Packers were ever to go after Cook, his fumbling history would be a big part of the evaluation.

Now, let’s talk about the price that was floated. And again, this was hypothetical. Heavy suggested a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick as an example of what Green Bay might offer. That wasn’t a real proposal to Buffalo. It was just an idea. And no such trade happened before the deadline.

Is that a fair price? Think about it in context. We just watched the Colts turn down multiple offers of a second-round pick and more for Jonathan Taylor. They wanted a blockbuster. So a team like Buffalo, if it really valued Cook, might want a lot more than a second and a fifth. And remember, Green Bay doesn’t have its 2027 first-round pick after the Micah Parsons trade. So every pick it still has matters a lot. As we talked about with the quiet deadline, Brian Gutekunst values draft picks more than almost anything.

Then there’s the Josh Jacobs question. The October article raised questions about Jacobs’ situation, and now his finger injury adds a separate availability concern. But neither of those things means Green Bay plans to replace him. Would the Packers rather trust Jacobs, Lloyd, Johnson, Brooks, and Strong? Or would they reshape the whole room in the offseason? Those are open questions. Not reported decisions.

And the article also mentioned Saquon Barkley as another theoretical big-name possibility. But that doesn’t mean Barkley was available, or that Green Bay ever called Philadelphia. In fact, ESPN reported that the Eagles were not trading Barkley. So that’s just a name thrown out there.

So for a James Cook trade to actually happen in the future, a lot of things would have to line up. Buffalo would have to be willing to negotiate. Green Bay would have to actually want him. And the two teams would have to agree on both the money and the compensation. And right now, the reporting doesn’t establish any of those things.

A headline about a forty-six million dollar Pro Bowler can be exciting. I get it. But it shouldn’t be mistaken for a trade announcement.

So here’s my honest take.

I love the idea of a big-time running back in Green Bay. This team has been dying for a run game all season. Even with the one hundred twenty-nine yards against New England, the Packers came into that game dead last in rushing. And without Jacobs, the challenge is only going to get harder.

But I don’t think James Cook is the answer right now. Not because he isn’t talented. He clearly is. But because the price, the fumbling issues, and the fact that the deadline has passed all make this a long shot. And because this front office has shown, again and again, that it won’t overpay just to make a splash.

If anything, this conversation might be more about the offseason. With a stockpile of compensatory picks coming for players like Romeo Doubs, Malik Willis, and Quay Walker, plus the extra fourth-round pick from the Rashan Gary trade, the Packers will have a lot more ammunition next spring. If a player like Cook or another top back becomes available then, Green Bay could be in a much better spot to make a real offer.

But for now, it’s about the guys in the building.

And that brings us to the most immediate concern. The Minnesota Vikings. This Sunday.

It’s a division game, and in the NFC North right now, every division game feels like a playoff game. The Packers are six and three, and they’re in last place in the division, because Minnesota, Chicago, and Detroit have all been winning too. A win against the Vikings can swing the momentum of the whole season. And the Vikings beat Green Bay in week one, scoring twenty-nine unanswered points after the Packers led twenty-two to ten. This is a revenge game.

And it’s a game where a reliable run game really matters. Minnesota has one of the best defenses in the league. If the Packers can’t run the ball at all, the Vikings can sit back and take away the deep shots to Watson and Golden. So whoever is carrying the ball, Lloyd, Johnson, Brooks, Strong, or maybe even Savion Williams, has to give this offense something.

The good news? Jordan Love has carried this team with almost no run game all season. Jonathan Gannon’s defense just forced four takeaways against New England. And Micah Parsons is getting closer to his return, with his practice window running through November twenty-fifth.

So let’s recap where things stand.

Green Bay made no trades at the November tenth deadline. The team officially added rookie tight end Kenny Fletcher Jr. to the practice squad, a converted defensive end who could help as a blocker. Josh Jacobs needs surgery on his fractured finger, but no IR decision has been confirmed. Pierre Strong Jr. could face a roster squeeze as Chris Brooks gets healthy, but he hasn’t been released. And James Cook remains a hypothetical future target from an October trade pitch. Not a Packers acquisition. Not a promised offseason move.

But it’s a fun idea. And it shows just how much the Packers’ future in the backfield is up in the air.
