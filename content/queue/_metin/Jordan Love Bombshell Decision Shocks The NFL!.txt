Packers Nation, let’s start with a number that should tell you everything about this season. Five and three. On paper, that looks comfortable. But this Packers team has done it with the worst running game in the entire National Football League. And the man holding the whole offense together, week after week, with almost no help on the ground, is Jordan Love.

Matt LaFleur said it himself. When he was asked what’s working in the run game, he said, if he’s being honest, not a lot right now. He said that’s on the coaches, they have to figure it out, because it’s not good enough. And he said the team has to find something it can hang its hat on in the run game, and unfortunately, it hasn’t done that yet.

So the front office has made a decision. They picked up the phone. ESPN and NFL Network have both reported that the Packers are among the teams that have talked to Indianapolis about Jonathan Taylor. And the reason, in plain words, is that Jordan Love has carried this offense as far as one man can. Now he needs help.

Let’s start with the record. Because five and three is telling a story that starts to fall apart when you look closer.

Through eight games, Green Bay has scored one hundred seventy-one points and allowed one hundred sixty-four. That’s a cushion of seven points across two full months of football. Seven. And two of the five wins came down to a combined six points. A twenty to seventeen overtime escape against the Jets, and a seventeen to fourteen grind in Tampa Bay. Flip those two finishes, and this is a three and five team searching for answers. That’s how thin the margin has been all year.

And the run game is the reason the margin stays so thin. Green Bay walked into week eight dead last in the NFL at fifty-nine point nine rushing yards per game. In the week three loss to Atlanta, the Packers ran for just seventeen yards as a team. Seventeen. Then on Thursday night against Carolina, a defense that came in ranked last in the league against the run, Green Bay still managed only sixty-six rushing yards. Josh Jacobs carried twenty-one times for thirty-nine yards. Under two yards a carry.

LaFleur admitted the defense is helping the team survive right now while they try to figure out the run game. And that’s the truth. When you can’t run on the worst run defense in football, the problem isn’t the matchup. The problem is inside the building.

A contender can hide a weakness like that for a month. It can’t hide it in January.

And the division makes it even sharper. Green Bay is one and two inside the NFC North, with losses in Minnesota and Detroit wrapped around that blowout of Chicago. Minnesota came into Sunday at six and oh. Chicago is five and two, and just traded two first-round picks for All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons. Nobody in this division is standing still.

The record says contender. The point differential says coin flip.

Now, to understand how it got this tight, you have to walk through these eight weeks in order. Because almost every single week took something away from this team.

Week one set the tone. Green Bay led twenty-two to ten in Minnesota, then watched the Vikings score twenty-nine unanswered points. The swing came when a Vikings offsides penalty turned a field goal try into fourth and one, and Jordan Love was stopped on a quarterback sneak. Final score, thirty-nine to twenty-two.

Week two, a twenty to seventeen overtime win over the Jets on a field goal from rookie kicker Trey Smack. But that win cost them receiver Jayden Reed, who suffered a neck injury that ended his season.

Week three was the low point. Atlanta came into Lambeau and blew out the Packers, handing Green Bay its first home opener loss since 2012.

Week four, a seventeen to fourteen win in Tampa. But linebacker Edgerrin Cooper tore his Achilles. LaFleur said it was a tough one to lose, pointing to what Cooper showed in basically a half of football.

Week five, the thirty-one to seven beatdown of Chicago. And then defensive tackle Javon Hargrave tore his Achilles late in a game that was already decided. LaFleur called it deflating. Two key defenders gone in back-to-back weeks. And somehow, the defense got better anyway.

Week six, a twenty-three to thirteen win over Dallas on Sunday Night Football.

Week seven, the twenty to seventeen loss in Detroit, the same week Josh Jacobs returned from the commissioner’s exempt list. Jacobs said he’s just trying to come back, play ball, learn from his mistake, and move forward.

And week eight, the twenty-seven to nineteen win over Carolina on Thursday night, built on a defense that refused to break late.

Look at the shape of those eight weeks. The wins kept coming from one side of the ball. And that wasn’t luck. It goes back to decisions Brian Gutekunst made back in March. And most of them started with letting people leave.

Gutekunst spent March selling instead of hoarding. Edge rusher Rashan Gary went to Dallas for a 2027 fourth-round pick. Receiver Dontayvion Wicks went to Philadelphia for a 2026 fifth and a 2027 sixth. Defensive tackle Colby Wooden went to Indianapolis in a swap for linebacker Zaire Franklin. None of those deals made big headlines. But all of them turned veterans into picks, or into a player who fit the new defense better. That’s a general manager who treats roster spots like currency.

Free agency took more. Romeo Doubs signed with New England. Quay Walker signed with Las Vegas. Left tackle Rasheed Walker signed with Carolina. And veteran lineman Elgton Jenkins was released and landed in Cleveland.

Now, I’d call the receiver and linebacker losses survivable, because the roster had young depth at both spots. The offensive line is a different story. Losing Rasheed Walker and Jenkins in the same offseason left the run game with less experience up front. And eight games of rushing numbers back that up.

Two smaller exits matter a lot more now than they did in March. Running back Emanuel Wilson signed with Seattle, which thinned out the depth behind Jacobs before anybody knew how badly they’d need it. And defensive coordinator Jeff Hafley left to become head coach of the Miami Dolphins.

On the surface, that looks like losing a backup runner and your defensive play caller in the same winter. But Hafley leaving opened the door for the most important hire Green Bay made all year. And that hire explains why this defense is winning games the offense can’t.

Jonathan Gannon.

The former Arizona Cardinals head coach was brought in as defensive coordinator in January, and he came with a reputation for pressure. Thursday night showed exactly why. Against Carolina, Gannon kept sending blitzes even after the Panthers cut the lead to eight late in the fourth quarter. Green Bay finished with six sacks from six different players. And Carolina went just five for eighteen on third down.

Gannon inherited a defense missing its best player, and he turned it into the identity of this team. The numbers prove it. Across the last four games, Green Bay has allowed just fourteen point seven five points per game. And it did that without Micah Parsons, without Edgerrin Cooper, and without Javon Hargrave.

Ty’Ron Hopper stepped into a bigger role and picked off Bryce Young on Thursday night. LaFleur said he can’t say enough great things about him, pointing to the sack, the pass breakup, and the interception. Xavier McKinney and a rookie cornerback have held the back end together. A unit that loses this much talent and still gets better is being coached, not carried. And that’s exactly the kind of foundation a front office builds a deadline plan around.

The offense got some arrivals too, just smaller ones. On August thirtieth, the same day Josh Jacobs landed on the commissioner’s exempt list, Green Bay traded a 2028 sixth-round pick to Pittsburgh for running back Caleb Johnson. A tight end signed a day earlier. Those were patches. And the rushing numbers prove patches were never going to be enough. MarShawn Lloyd and Chris Brooks handled the early starts at running back, and nothing really stuck.

The defense got a coordinator. The run game got a stopgap. And that gap is exactly where the draft comes in.

Green Bay entered the 2026 draft without a first-round pick for the first time in forty years. That pick, number twenty overall, went to Dallas in the Micah Parsons trade. Gutekunst still made six selections, and four of them went to defense. Speed and length on defense came first. Everything else came second. That’s why the defense looks fast right now. And it’s also why the offense looks thin in the backfield heading into November.

Cornerback Brandon Cisse came off the board at pick fifty-two and has started since week one. Green Bay sent picks eighty-four and one sixty to Tampa Bay to move up for rookie defensive tackle Chris McClellan at seventy-seven, a move that looks smarter every week Hargrave is out. Edge rusher Dani Dennis-Sutton went at pick one hundred twenty after posting a relative athletic score of nine point nine six out of ten. Center Jager Burton went at one fifty-three, with a pick that came over in the Wicks trade. And Green Bay still holds that extra 2027 fourth-round pick from the Gary deal.

The case for this class is simple. The defense got younger and faster. The case against it is just as simple. Green Bay didn’t draft a running back or a receiver for an offense that now ranks last running the ball.

And the bill keeps coming. The 2027 first-round pick also went to Dallas in the Parsons deal. So the Packers don’t have their first next year either.

Which brings us to the Jonathan Taylor price.

ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that the Colts want something close to the 2022 Christian McCaffrey package. That was a second, a third, a fourth, and a fifth-round pick. And here’s the thing. Green Bay can actually build that package without a first-round pick. They’ve got their own picks, the extra fourth from the Gary trade, and they’re projected to get compensatory picks for players who left. The question is whether Indianapolis will take it. The Colts view Taylor as a big part of their rebuild, and he’s not on an expiring contract. Several contending teams are interested.

And there’s another piece to this. Josh Jacobs has no guaranteed money left on his deal. According to Over the Cap, releasing him would wipe about thirteen and a half million dollars off the 2027 cap. And the NFL is reportedly reviewing new allegations involving Jacobs. So while the league looks into that, Green Bay is shopping for the best running back on the market. That’s not a panic move. It’s a plan with a paper trail.

And now let’s talk about the part nobody is giving enough credit for. Jordan Love.

Through eight games, Jordan has completed one hundred ninety-two of three hundred twelve passes for two thousand two hundred forty-six yards, sixteen touchdowns, and six interceptions, with a ninety-two point four passer rating. He’s thrown at least two touchdown passes in seven of his eight games.

Now think about the conditions he’s doing it in. Jayden Reed is out for the year. Matthew Golden has been dealing with drops and injuries. Christian Watson just played through a broken rib. Tucker Kraft has been working his way back from a major injury. The offensive line has been shuffled over and over, with the starting center and right guard both down. And the run game is giving him almost nothing. Dead last in the league.

And still, two touchdowns in seven of eight games. Five wins. A team in the playoff race.

That’s why the Taylor calls make sense. Jordan Love isn’t the problem. He’s the reason this team is five and three instead of three and five. And the front office’s decision to go hunting for a star running back is really a decision about Jordan. It’s about giving their franchise quarterback the help he’s earned.

Because think about what Jordan could do with a real run game. Right now, defenses know Green Bay can’t run, so they can sit back, play coverage, and dare Jordan to beat them. And he keeps doing it anyway. Now imagine defenses having to load the box to stop Jonathan Taylor. Imagine safeties creeping up. Imagine play-action actually working because the run is a real threat. Christian Watson running free deep. Golden and Kraft getting softer coverage underneath. That’s when this offense goes from good to scary.

And then add the pass rusher with a date circled on the calendar. Micah Parsons is expected to return to practice soon. A defense that’s already holding teams under fifteen points a game, with Parsons coming back? Plus a real run game helping Jordan Love? That changes the math in the NFC North. That changes the math in the whole conference.

So here’s where I land. The defense is legit. Gannon was the hire of the year. Jordan Love is quietly having a fantastic season under brutal conditions. And the one thing holding this team back is obvious to everyone. The run game. Whether it’s Jonathan Taylor, or a smaller move like Tony Pollard, or finally giving Kevin Zeitler a shot on the line, something has to change before November tenth.

Because five and three with the worst run game in football is impressive. But five and three is not a championship. And this team, with this quarterback and this defense, has a real chance to be something special if it gets one more piece.
