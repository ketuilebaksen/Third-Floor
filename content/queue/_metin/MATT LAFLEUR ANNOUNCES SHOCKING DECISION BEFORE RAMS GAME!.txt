Let’s start with where this team is coming from. Because the Vikings loss still stings.

Thirty to ten at Lambeau Field. The Packers fell to six and four. One and three in the NFC North. And swept by the Vikings for the season. LaFleur walked to the podium and said it plainly. This is a humbling league, and today we got humbled. Eight sacks on Jordan Love. Four turnovers to Minnesota’s one. And LaFleur admitted it was a bad decision not to go to the quick passing game earlier, because his quarterback was getting abused just like in the first meeting.

And Jordan Love took responsibility too. He said the offense wasn’t good enough up front, and he wasn’t good enough at getting the ball out. That’s a franchise quarterback owning it.

But the bye week came at the perfect time. A chance to rest. A chance to heal. A chance to reset. LaFleur even gave the players a three-day break. And now, as the team comes back to work, the moves are starting to come in.

So let’s talk about the decision. Pierre Strong.

The Packers waived Pierre Strong and kept MarShawn Lloyd on the fifty-three-man roster. And this decision was tied to two things. A crowded backfield. And an important return on defense.

Let’s break down the backfield first. Josh Jacobs missed the first six games of the season on the commissioner’s exempt list. Then Chris Brooks was on injured reserve with an ankle injury. Then Brooks was finally activated, and Jacobs went down again with his finger injury. Now, with both of them healthy or getting there, the Packers had to trim the group.

Jacobs, Kaleb Johnson, and Brooks were safe. That left Strong and Lloyd as the two candidates to be waived. And the Packers chose to keep Lloyd.

Now here’s why that’s such a big signal. Reports say the Packers feel confident that Josh Jacobs will be back to play against the Rams. Because if they weren’t confident, why would they cut a running back right now? You don’t thin out your backfield unless you expect your lead back to return.

And if Strong clears waivers, the Packers can sign him back to the practice squad. Then they’d keep four running backs on the active roster, Jacobs, Johnson, Brooks, and Lloyd, with Strong on the practice squad as extra depth. That’s smart roster management.

And the second part of this decision might be even bigger. Waiving Strong opens a roster spot during the bye week. And that spot matters for week twelve. Because the Packers are going to need it to activate Micah Parsons.

Remember, Parsons is on the physically unable to perform list. To play, he has to be activated to the fifty-three-man roster. And for that, you need an open spot. Well, now the Packers have one. And according to the reporting, the team’s expectation is that Parsons will be ready to go against the Rams.

So let’s talk about Micah Parsons. Because LaFleur gave us his clearest answer yet.

On Monday, LaFleur was asked if the plan is for Parsons to return for the Thanksgiving Eve game against the Rams. And he said, well, that’s my plan. And then he joked a little. He said, but I think my plan’s been different than everybody else’s the whole time.

That’s LaFleur having a little fun with the whole Parsons saga. Because there’s been so much speculation, so many target dates floated around, so many questions every single week. But then he got serious. And he said there are still a couple more hurdles for Parsons to clear before they can definitively say he’s a go.

So here’s where things stand. The plan is for Parsons to play. But it’s not locked in yet. The team holds a few more practices, and they’ll see where he is by the end of the week.

And think about how long this journey has been. Parsons tore his ACL in his left knee in week fifteen last season, against the Denver Broncos. A non-contact play. He had surgery on December twenty-ninth. And if he plays against the Rams, it’ll be the first time in more than eleven months that he’s been on a football field in a game.

And look at what he was doing before the injury. Twelve and a half sacks in fourteen games last season, after the Packers traded two first-round picks to get him from Dallas. The first player in NFL history to start his career with five straight seasons of at least twelve sacks. A four-time All-Pro.

And remember what he said when he first came back to practice. He said after three hundred days of work, he had no nerves. He talked about the organization’s patience. About not skipping steps. About how there’s never a perfect time to come back, but you have to push through.

And there’s one more important detail. His twenty-one-day practice window closes on November twenty-fifth. The day of the Rams game. So the Packers have to activate him by then. The timing lines up perfectly.

And if he does play, expect a limited snap count at first. He’s been honest that his conditioning isn’t where it was. He said last season he averaged around fifty snaps per game, and he doesn’t expect to handle that right away. But even twenty or twenty-five snaps of Micah Parsons changes everything for this defense.

Now let’s talk about Josh Jacobs. Because his news is really encouraging.

Jacobs fractured his right ring finger in the win over New England on November eighth. And this past Friday, he had successful surgery in Los Angeles. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport said the procedure was performed by Dr. Steven Shin, who he described as one of the nation’s experts. And Rapoport said Jacobs faces a two to three week recovery.

Now here’s the key decision the Packers made. They did not put Jacobs on injured reserve. If they had, he would’ve had to miss at least four games. Even with the bye week included, that would’ve pushed his return way down the road. But by keeping him on the active roster, the Packers preserved the chance of getting him back for the Rams game. The only cost was one roster spot during the Vikings game. And the bye week absorbs the rest of his recovery time.

And according to Rapoport, Jacobs has a realistic chance of returning against the Rams on November twenty-fifth. That would mean he only misses one game. Just the Vikings game. Because the bye falls right in between.

Now, keeping him off IR doesn’t mean he’ll definitely play. He still has to be healthy enough to hold onto the football. Remember, the whole reason for the surgery was that he had no grip strength in that hand. But this is a much clearer and more hopeful picture than when LaFleur first announced the surgery.

And the running game might be in better shape than people think. Because Kaleb Johnson has been stepping up. Over the last two games, while Jacobs has been out for most of them, Johnson has carried the ball twenty-four times for one hundred ten yards and a touchdown. That’s more than four and a half yards a carry. In an offense that’s been averaging well under three yards a carry for most of the season.

And offensive coordinator Adam Stenavich said this week that he’s excited about the run game. When he was asked about his confidence in the ground attack, he said, yeah, I do, I’m excited about that. And he said a lot of that optimism comes from what Johnson has done the past two weeks. He’s seen real flashes from the second-year back.

So picture this. Josh Jacobs comes back as the physical hammer. Kaleb Johnson brings the vision and the burst. Chris Brooks and MarShawn Lloyd add depth. For the first time all season, this running back room might actually be a strength.

Now, the offensive line. And this might be the best news of all.

The protection has been the most obvious crack in this offense all season long. Eight sacks against Minnesota. Twenty-one pressures. Twelve quarterback hits. A revolving door of linemen. And center John Williams, a guard playing out of position, struggling with botched snaps.

But the bye week came at the right time. Because two veterans are trending toward a return against the Rams. Sean Rhyan at center. And Kevin Zeitler at right guard.

Rhyan had a shot at playing against the Vikings, but he didn’t practice that week because of his ankle. Now, with ten days before the next game, he has a much more realistic path back to the lineup. By the time the Packers face the Rams, he’ll be almost five weeks removed from his high ankle sprain. And reports say the Packers will likely have him back.

And that matters a lot. Because Rhyan isn’t an elite center, but he’s a much better option than Williams. And he’s had one of the better run-blocking grades on the line this season.

And then there’s Zeitler. LaFleur said the team was actually expecting Zeitler to start at right guard against the Vikings. But he had what LaFleur called a freak thing happen to his triceps during Thursday’s practice. On top of the ankle injury he was already dealing with. So he couldn’t go. And when he was asked if Zeitler will play against the Rams, LaFleur said, we’ll see where he’s at.

But here’s what we know. Zeitler showed in a small sample against New England what a steady veteran can do for that interior. And if he and Rhyan both come back, alongside Jordan Morgan, Laken Tomlinson, and Anthony Belton, the Packers might finally have their best possible offensive line formation. For the first time all season.

Think about that. All year long, this line has been patched together. Six different players have played guard for Green Bay this season. And now, right when the stretch run starts, it could finally be whole. If this group can stabilize and repeat what it’s already shown in flashes, the Packers have a much better shot at getting a consistent version of this offense over the final seven games.

And remember what the Patriots game looked like when the protection held up. Forty-five points. Jordan Love with a career night. Watson and Golden making franchise history. That’s what this offense can be with a clean pocket.

There’s also good news on defense. Zaire Franklin, who’s been out with that hairline fracture in his shin, is nearing a return too. That’s a big deal for a linebacker room that’s been running on Isaiah McDuffie and Ty’Ron Hopper with Edgerrin Cooper done for the year. And Skyy Moore, the dynamic returner LaFleur called a big-time weapon, has been working his way back from his ankle injury. Coleman Owen is on the practice squad as insurance if Moore isn’t ready.

Now let’s talk about the opponent. The Los Angeles Rams. And they’re coming in hot.

The Rams have won four straight games. And on Sunday, they crushed the Arizona Cardinals thirty-four to six. That’s a team playing with a lot of confidence.

But that win came at a big price. The Rams lost not one, but two centers in that game. Coleman Shelton is out for weeks. And Dylan McMahon landed on injured reserve.
