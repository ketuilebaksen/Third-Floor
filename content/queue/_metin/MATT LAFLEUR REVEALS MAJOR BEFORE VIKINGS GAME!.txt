Let’s start with the decision. Micah Parsons.

The Packers opened his twenty-one-day practice window last week, and the hope was building that maybe, just maybe, this would be the week. And this week, he made real progress. For the first time since returning to practice, Parsons got work in eleven-on-eleven periods. And there was a moment on Thursday that got everybody talking. Parsons bull-rushed Tucker Kraft in practice. If you know anything about Tucker Kraft, you know he’s not an easy guy to move. And Parsons moved him.

But in the end, the Packers ruled him out for today’s game. And LaFleur explained exactly why. He said Parsons looks great, but he just doesn’t have a ton of reps under his belt yet. And he said they want to continue building him up.

And there was another complication. Earlier this week, Parsons got sick. LaFleur said he was ill and couldn’t take part in the jog-through on Wednesday. So the illness took away even more valuable reps from a guy who desperately needed them.

So this will be Parsons’ tenth game missed this season. And I know that’s frustrating. Everybody wanted to see him line up against Minnesota. But think about what Parsons himself said when he came back to practice. He talked about the organization’s patience. About not skipping steps. About making sure he’s as protected as possible from reinjury. He said there’s never a perfect time to come back, but you have to be ready. And the team clearly decided he isn’t quite there yet.

And here’s the important part. Because the Packers designated Parsons to return, he has to be activated to the active roster by the end of his twenty-one-day practice window. That window runs through November twenty-fifth, which is the Rams game on Thanksgiving Eve. So the clock is ticking. He’s coming. It’s just not today.

That’s the right call, Packers Nation. You don’t risk a player you traded two first-round picks for, coming off a torn ACL, by throwing him into a division game without enough reps. You build him up. You get him ready for December and January. Because that’s when this team is going to need him most.

Now, let’s go through the rest of the injury report. Because it’s a lot.

Ruled out alongside Parsons: running back Josh Jacobs, center Sean Rhyan, and tight end Josh Whyle.

Jacobs, of course, fractured his ring finger in the win over New England, and surgery is planned. He had no grip strength in that hand, and you simply can’t play running back if you can’t hold onto the football. The good news is that reports suggest he could be back within two to three weeks. So this may not be as long an absence as some feared.

Sean Rhyan is out again with his ankle injury. Earlier in the week, LaFleur was hopeful that Rhyan could return. LaFleur had also said Rhyan wasn’t going on injured reserve and that it was a shorter-term injury. But he missed practice all week, and he’ll miss another game. So the offensive line will have to hold up without its center once again.

Josh Whyle is out with a concussion.

And then the doubtful list. Linebacker Zaire Franklin, who’s been dealing with that hairline fracture in his shin, didn’t practice at all this week and is listed as doubtful. The upside here is that Franklin should be good to go by Week 12. So once again, it’ll be Isaiah McDuffie and Ty’Ron Hopper at linebacker. And remember, Hopper had one of the best games of his career against Carolina, with a sack, an interception, and three passes defended. He dealt with an illness this week, but he was a full participant by Friday.

And then the questionable list. Seven players. And some of these are really important.

Safety Xavier McKinney. This is the one that caught everybody off guard. McKinney popped up on the injury report on Friday with a new foot injury, and he was limited in practice. He’s questionable. And this is a big deal, because McKinney is one of the most durable players on this team. He hasn’t missed a game due to injury since 2022, back when he was with the New York Giants. And he’s been a playmaker all season. He picked off Drake Maye on the very first play of the Patriots game. He had two interceptions against the Bears. If McKinney can’t go, that’s a huge hole in the back end.

Javon Bullard is also questionable with ankle and knee injuries. He didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday, but he said Thursday that he was optimistic he’d be able to play against the Vikings. If Bullard can’t go, Kamal Hadden is the backup in the slot.

So two starting defensive backs, McKinney and Bullard, both questionable. That’s a concern. But think about what this secondary has already done. Evan Williams, graded by PFF as the best safety in the NFL. Brandon Cisse, just named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Month at twenty-one years old. This group has depth and talent, and Jonathan Gannon has proven he can coach around injuries.

Lukas Van Ness is questionable with a back injury. He missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday before returning on a limited basis on Friday. And that’s important, because with Parsons out, the Packers can’t afford to lose another pass rusher. Van Ness has been having a career year, with three and a half sacks, just half a sack behind his career high.

On the offensive line, Kevin Zeitler is questionable, and his situation is rough. He came out of his first extended action of the season with an ankle injury, and then he added a triceps injury before Friday’s practice and didn’t participate. Travis Glover is also questionable with a knee injury after being limited all week. And Aaron Banks was limited with knee and hip issues. So the interior of this line is banged up again. This is going to be a real test against a Vikings defense that’s been one of the best in the league.

And now, the good news. Because there is some.

LaFleur said running back Chris Brooks and wide receiver Skyy Moore both have a good chance to return this week. Both have been on injured reserve. Both are listed as questionable. But when LaFleur was asked if they had a good chance to play, he said, yeah, they definitely do.

Brooks has been working back from an ankle injury, and he’s a big piece of this running back room without Jacobs. Remember, he broke off a twenty-seven yard run earlier this season.

And Skyy Moore? LaFleur called him a big-time weapon. He said if Moore is able to go, it’ll definitely boost the return game, and then they’ll see how they use him on offense. Remember, before he got hurt against the Bears, Moore was averaging sixteen yards per punt return and twenty-seven and a half yards per kick return. LaFleur called him a dynamic returner. In a close division game, a big return can swing everything.

And there’s more good news at running back. MarShawn Lloyd, who missed the Patriots game with a knee injury, was not on the injury report this week. He looks like he’s recovered and on track to play.

And then there’s Kaleb Johnson. After Jacobs left the New England game, Johnson stepped in and ran fourteen times for sixty-eight yards and a touchdown. That’s nearly five yards a carry. He and Pierre Strong split the work after Jacobs went down. And now Johnson could get a real opportunity to carry the load against Minnesota.

Remember what LaFleur said a few weeks ago about Kaleb Johnson? He said if you want more opportunities, you have to contribute on special teams and be rock solid in pass protection. Well, now the opportunity is here. This is his chance.

And Trey Smack, the kicker, was dealing with a right calf injury this week. But he was a full participant on Friday and he’s good to go. And after going seven for seven against New England, the confidence should be sky high.

Now, let’s talk about LaFleur’s mindset this week. Because it tells you a lot about how he’s handling all of this.

With the trade deadline passing and no outside help coming in, LaFleur was asked how he felt about it. And he said he tries to stay unemotional about all of that. He said, to be honest, he just focuses on what the team has. And he said there’s a big task in front of them with the Vikings.

That’s exactly the right attitude. No complaining about what you don’t have. Focus on the guys in the building.

LaFleur also said he views divisional games as a game and a half. Think about that. Because when you beat a division rival, you gain a game in the standings and you push them back at the same time. That’s why this game matters so much. The Packers are six and three. The Vikings are six and two. And right now, Green Bay is sitting in last place in the NFC North because everybody in this division is winning. A win today and the Packers jump right back into the thick of it.

And LaFleur summed up what it’ll take to beat Minnesota. He said you have to do a great job in all phases. In the run game and in the pass game against this unit. Every phase. Offense. Defense. Special teams. Nothing can be left on the table.

He also talked about the resilience of this team. He said they’re going to keep battling, and that’s all you can ask for. And you’ve seen it all season. Injury after injury after injury. And this team keeps finding ways to win. Five of the last six. Forty-five points against the defending AFC champions.

Now let’s talk about the Vikings. Because they’re hurting too. Maybe even worse.

Minnesota will be without five players today. Quarterback Kyler Murray, who’s dealing with a hamstring strain. Receiver Jauan Jennings with a calf injury. Tight end T.J. Hockenson with wrist and chest injuries. Defensive lineman Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins with an ankle injury. And linebacker Blake Cashman with a concussion. And Cashman is the Vikings’ leading tackler. That’s a big loss for their defense.

And they’ve got three more players listed as questionable. Outside linebacker Kyle Van Noy with a calf injury. Receiver Jordan Addison, who was added to the report on Friday with a thumb injury. And safety Josh Metellus, also added Friday with a shoulder issue.

So both teams are banged up. This is going to be a battle of depth. A battle of next-man-up. And honestly, that might favor the team that’s already proven it can win without its stars. And that’s the Green Bay Packers. They’ve been doing it all year.

And now, the storyline that’s going to have everybody glued to their TVs. Kirk Cousins.

With Kyler Murray out, the Vikings went out on Tuesday, the day of the trade deadline, and acquired Kirk Cousins from the Las Vegas Raiders for a 2027 third-round pick. And on Friday, Kevin O’Connell officially named Cousins the starting quarterback for today’s game.

Kirk Cousins. Back in a Vikings uniform. Starting at Lambeau Field. Against the Packers.

Packers fans know Kirk Cousins very well. He spent six seasons in Minnesota before leaving for Atlanta, and then ended up in Las Vegas. And now he’s back, just days after the trade, with barely any practice time with his new-old team. He practiced on Thursday for the first time. That’s a quick turnaround for any quarterback.

And this is where Jonathan Gannon’s defense has a huge opportunity. Think about it. A quarterback who just arrived. Without his top tight end in Hockenson. Without Jauan Jennings. Possibly without Jordan Addison. In a hostile environment at Lambeau. Against a defense that’s been forcing turnovers all season. Five takeaways against Chicago. Four takeaways against New England.

LaFleur explained recently why this defense is so good at taking the ball away. He said the guys at the second and third levels have great ball skills. And the team puts pressure on the quarterback in a lot of different ways, so sometimes quarterbacks make bad decisions. And the Packers capitalize.

Pressure Kirk Cousins early. Confuse him with disguised looks. Make him hold the ball. And let the ballhawks go to work. That’s the blueprint.

But don’t sleep on Cousins either. He knows this rivalry. He knows Lambeau. He’s a veteran who’s thrown for a lot of yards against Green Bay over the years. And the Vikings still have a dangerous defense, even without Cashman. So the Packers can’t take anything for granted.

And on the Packers’ side, there’s a revenge factor. Remember week one? Green Bay led twenty-two to ten in Minnesota, and then watched the Vikings score twenty-nine unanswered points. The swing came when a Vikings offsides penalty turned a field goal try into fourth and one, and Jordan Love was stopped on a quarterback sneak. Final score, thirty-nine to twenty-two. That one stung. And this team hasn’t forgotten it.

So here’s what I’ll be watching today.

Number one. The run game without Jacobs. Can Kaleb Johnson, MarShawn Lloyd, Chris Brooks, and Pierre Strong give this offense enough balance to keep the Vikings honest? After one hundred twenty-nine rushing yards against New England, we know it’s possible.

Number two. The offensive line. With Rhyan out and Zeitler, Glover, and Banks all banged up, can this line protect Jordan Love against one of the best defenses in the league?

Number three. The secondary. If McKinney or Bullard can’t go, can Evan Williams, Brandon Cisse, Kamal Hadden, and the rest hold up?

Number four. The pass rush without Parsons, and maybe without Van Ness. Can Gannon’s scheme still get to Kirk Cousins?

And number five. Jordan Love. Can he keep playing at an MVP level? Twenty-three of twenty-nine, three hundred seventy-two yards, and four total touchdowns last week. With Watson and Golden coming off a historic game. If Love is on, the Packers can beat anybody.

Packers Nation, this is the kind of game that defines a season. A division rival. A revenge game. Both teams hurting. And a Kirk Cousins storyline that writes itself. The Packers have won five of their last six. They’re playing at home. And they’ve proven again and again that they can win through adversity.

Micah Parsons isn’t playing today. But his return is coming. And if the Packers can win this one without him, imagine what this team looks like when he’s back.
