Now let’s talk about the problem. And yes, there’s a problem. A reporter came at LaFleur with a stat that honestly made me wince. In the Packers’ last two home games against strong rushing teams, they gave up 275 yards on the ground. Baltimore did it last year. Atlanta did it this year. Two hundred seventy-five rushing yards. At Lambeau Field. That is not okay, Packers Nation. That is not Green Bay football. And now here comes Chicago, a team that loves to run the ball and does it about as well as anybody in the league.

LaFleur didn’t sugarcoat it. He said the Bears are really good up front, that the trenches are one of the biggest strengths of their entire team. He said they get after you. And then he said something that tells you exactly how seriously this staff is taking that backfield. He said Chicago has two really good running backs, and you could argue they might be the best one-two combo in the entire league. That’s a big statement. That’s not coach-speak. That’s respect.

And here’s the key part. LaFleur pointed out that the Bears’ passing game feeds off their run game. Think about what that means. If Chicago is gashing you on the ground, you have to bring that safety down, you have to load the box, and suddenly the play-action game opens up and you’re getting burned over the top. So stopping the run isn’t just about stopping the run. It’s about shutting down their entire offense.

So how do you do it LaFleur laid it out, and I love how he said it. It takes all eleven. Everybody has to be on the same page. You have to own your assignment. And then, in his words, you’ve got to build a wall. You’ve got to leverage the football, and you need multiple hats at the ball, because these backs can make you miss. That’s the recipe. Gap discipline. Gang tackling. Swarming to the football. No hero ball, no freelancing, no guys trying to make the splash play and leaving their gap wide open. Eleven guys flying to the ball like their hair is on fire. That’s what it’s going to take.

And then there’s the time of possession issue, which honestly might be the hidden storyline of this whole game. A reporter pointed out that Chicago has dominated time of possession this year, to the point where their offense has run something like a hundred more snaps than their defense has been on the field. Let that sink in. A hundred more snaps. That means they’re grinding you down. They’re holding the ball. They’re keeping their own defense fresh on the sideline while your defense is out there gasping for air in the fourth quarter.

LaFleur agreed completely. He said the Bears are capable of creating explosive plays, but they also do a great job of staying on the field. They’re in third and manageable a lot. And then, he said, you have to defend four downs against them, because they’re not afraid to go for it. That’s a nightmare for a defense. You make a stop on third down, you think you’re off the field, and nope, they’re going for it on fourth and two.

So LaFleur said this one is going to take all three phases. Win the field position battle on special teams. Get stops on defense. And offensively, keep your own defense fresh and off the grass. That’s complementary football, and in a game like this, it’s everything.
