Let’s start with the decision. Because it’s one that matters a lot for this offensive line.

LaFleur was asked a direct question. Is Sean Rhyan going on injured reserve? And his answer was simple. I don’t believe so.

Then he was asked the follow-up. So you think it’s a shorter-term injury? And LaFleur said yes.

Packers Nation, that’s a big deal. Remember, Rhyan was diagnosed with a high ankle sprain. And earlier reports said he could miss four to five games. But the team chose not to put him on IR, which would have forced him to miss at least four games. Keeping him off IR means the team believes he could be back sooner. And that matters, because Rhyan has been one of the steadiest pieces of this line. He started every game before the injury. Earlier this season, he had the highest run-blocking grade on the entire offensive line, the only one above sixty.

And think about what’s been happening without him. John Williams, a guard, has been snapping the ball at center. Laken Tomlinson has been moved around. Kevin Zeitler finally got in at right guard. It’s been a patchwork group. Getting Rhyan back sooner could help this line settle into something more stable. Especially with Josh Jacobs now facing surgery on his fractured finger, the run game needs every bit of help it can get.

So that’s decision number one. Keep Sean Rhyan off IR, and bet on him coming back sooner rather than later.

Now let’s talk about the rookie who just made history. Brandon Cisse.

LaFleur was asked about Cisse being named NFL Defensive Rookie of the Month. And remember who we’re talking about. Cisse was taken at pick fifty-two in this year’s draft. And he’s started at cornerback since week one. At just twenty-one years old.

LaFleur said it’s pretty remarkable. And then he explained why. He said, first off, the guy is wired the right way. He loves football. He’s always studying. And LaFleur gave credit to the coaching staff too, for getting him up to speed, because the NFL is a very different game than college. But he said it’s a big-time credit to Cisse himself, for coming in with the right mindset and the right work ethic, and going out there and playing really good football.

And we’ve seen it on the field. Against Detroit, Cisse prevented two potential touchdowns against Jameson Williams, including a diving breakup in the end zone, and he had an interception wiped out by a penalty. LaFleur said after that game that he’s mentally tough. And that corners have to be resilient and bounce back from bad plays. Cisse has done exactly that.

Twenty-one years old. Rookie of the Month. Starting from day one. That’s the kind of player Brian Gutekunst built this defense around. And it’s exactly why the front office values its draft picks so much.

Next, let’s talk about the defensive line. Because there was an interesting question here.

A reporter pointed out that if you just look at the stats, the defensive tackles’ numbers are pretty modest. But the defense as a whole has been really good. So what are guys like Karl Brooks and Devonte Wyatt doing so well?

And LaFleur gave an answer that really tells you how this defense works. He said they’re doing their job. He said there’s a lot of selfless play that goes into playing good team defense. And depending on the call, sometimes those guys just have to eat up blockers. And they’re doing it.

That’s the unglamorous part of football. The defensive tackle who takes on a double team so the linebacker can run free. The guy who occupies two blockers so the edge rusher gets a one-on-one. It doesn’t show up in the sack column. But it’s a huge reason this defense is playing at a top-five level, even without Javon Hargrave, even without Edgerrin Cooper, and even without Micah Parsons.

And on Brenton Cox, LaFleur was full of praise. He said Cox just gets better and better and better. He said he’s always been a really physical player, but with more opportunity, he’s taken his game to the next level.

Then came one of the best questions of the press conference. Why is this defense so much better at taking the ball away this year?

And LaFleur broke it down. He said it’s a combination of talent. The guys at the second and third levels, the linebackers and the defensive backs, have really good ball skills. And then they get put in positions where the team is putting pressure on the quarterback in a lot of different ways. And sometimes that pressure leads quarterbacks to make bad decisions. And the Packers’ players are capitalizing on those mistakes.

That’s Jonathan Gannon’s defense in a nutshell. Pressure from everywhere. Disguised looks. Simulated pressures. Blitzes. Quarterbacks get confused, they rush their decisions, and the ball ends up in the hands of guys like Xavier McKinney and Evan Williams. Five takeaways against the Bears. Four more against the Patriots. This defense is a turnover machine.

LaFleur also talked about how much respect he had for some of the players on the other side, like a pass rusher who he admitted he hadn’t heard much about until he watched the tape, and then found himself asking, who is this guy? That’s LaFleur. He never overlooks anybody. He prepares for every single player on the other side of the ball. And that’s exactly the mindset this team needs heading into Minnesota.

And then he was asked about a veteran who has been there for every single game. A guy who’s played in every game, including the playoffs, during his time with the team. And LaFleur said something great. He said they always talk about how your best ability is your availability. And the fact that this guy has been able to do that is pretty uncommon. And then he added, he’s a G.

I love that. In a season where so many players have been hurt, availability is a superpower.

Now let’s talk about the moment that really got me. Jordan Love.

At the time of this press conference, a reporter pointed out that Jordan’s next touchdown pass would be the one hundredth of his career. And LaFleur admitted he didn’t even know that. But then he reflected on the journey. He said he’s been with Jordan for six years now. And just to see where Jordan started, to where he is now, has been a pretty cool experience. Watching his growth, not just as a player, but as a person.

And then LaFleur said what he thinks the coolest part is. He said that throughout this whole journey, Jordan has stayed the same. He’s the same guy each and every day. And LaFleur said that’s a big-time credit to Jordan, and to his family for how they raised him.

And then a reporter brought up something that really puts Jordan’s career in perspective. In terms of touchdown pace, Jordan is ahead of Brett Favre. And just a little bit behind Aaron Rodgers.

Think about that, Packers Nation. Ahead of Brett Favre. Right behind Aaron Rodgers. The two legends who came before him. The two quarterbacks who made Lambeau Field famous for three decades.

And LaFleur talked about the pressure that comes with that. He said there are lofty expectations that come with being the quarterback of the Green Bay Packers. He called it unprecedented, what’s happened here over the course of thirty years. And he said there was a lot of pressure on Jordan. But to see him handle it the way he has, with humility and class, is a big-time credit to him.

And then, of course, Jordan went out against the Patriots and did it. He threw a five-yard touchdown pass to Matthew Golden. Career touchdown pass number one hundred. And he finished that game twenty-three of twenty-nine for three hundred seventy-two yards, three passing touchdowns, and a rushing touchdown. A career night. Forty-five to twenty-seven over the defending AFC champions.

From the kid who sat behind Aaron Rodgers for three years, to a quarterback who’s now being talked about in the MVP conversation. That’s the journey LaFleur was talking about. And he’s still the same guy every day.

There was also a funny moment. A reporter joked that they had a working theory that LaFleur made Jordan dress up as a blind referee for Halloween. And LaFleur laughed and said, you didn’t prepare me for this question. He admitted Jordan sent him the clip and he thought it was pretty hilarious. But he said he should probably refrain from giving his two cents on that. Smart move, coach. Smart move.

Now, let’s get to the most emotional part of all of this. Micah Parsons.

After more than three hundred days of rehab from his torn ACL and meniscus damage, Parsons finally got back on the practice field. And when he talked to reporters, he opened up in a way we haven’t really heard before.

He was asked if he had any nerves. And he said, when you miss over three hundred days and work for over three hundred days, you pretty much have no nerves. He said he was excited about the next week, when he could actually practice for real and see what he’s got, and see where he’s at.

And he explained that for the past four or five weeks, he’d been building strength and doing position work on the side, so he could integrate back in with the team.

But what really stood out was how grateful he was. He said he’s appreciative of the organization’s patience. That they understood where he was, and they didn’t rush anything. And he credited a member of the training staff named Nate, who kept telling him they weren’t going to skip any steps. They were going to check every box and do every little thing to make sure he’s as protected as possible from reinjury.

And then he talked about how lonely the journey was. He said it’s a lonely journey. He gave a shout-out to a teammate he’d been rehabbing with since training camp, someone who also had to deal with another injury setback, because only that teammate really knows what they went through together. Just the two of them in the rehab group, locking in on routines, preparing themselves day after day.

And he said the most fulfilling part of being back is just being around the guys again. Being with his teammates. Going through the process together. And enjoying every moment out on the field with them.

When he was asked about his patience, he laughed and said his patience is amazing. He said he thinks he’s got more patience than anyone. And he explained that his situation was different from others, and he’s learned to enjoy the process every time he got better. Even the days when he felt like he wasn’t getting better, even though he actually was.

And then he told the story of how the timeline kept moving. He said it started when he got to Green Bay in July. He tested, and the staff said, this is a possibility, we’ll see what happens. And then Nate came in and said, we don’t like it yet, this isn’t right, we’re going to wait. Then they had a new date. And again, they said, we still don’t like it. And they tested again.

He admitted that was hard. Every time you get your hopes up, thinking you might be close, and then you hear, no, we’re not ready yet. He said everyone around him kept asking, when? And as a player, you’re asking yourself the same thing. When am I going to be ready? When am I finally going to feel normal again?

And then he said something really powerful. He said, in reality, it’s never going to be the perfect time. You’re going to have to battle through what feels like it hurts. You’re going to have to push through uncomfortable challenges to put yourself in a position to feel okay.

And he gave enormous credit to Nate. He said when he needed a father figure, Nate was a father. When he needed a friend, Nate was a friend. And when he needed a trainer, Nate was a trainer. He said Nate adapted and grew, and brought in experts to help him. Including people from Wisconsin who came down and told him exactly what he needed to focus on. And after that, he said, he really took off.

And when he was asked if there’s still an internal battle, despite all the people helping him, Parsons said he’s learned that everything is about perspective.

Packers Nation, this is a guy the team gave up two first-round picks to get. A four-time All-Pro. The first player in NFL history to start his career with five straight seasons of at least twelve sacks. And he’s been through a long, lonely, frustrating road to get back. And instead of complaining, he’s talking about patience, perspective, and gratitude.

That’s decision number two, if you think about it. The Packers’ decision to be patient with Micah Parsons. To not rush him. To not skip steps. Even when fans were begging for him to come back. Even when the defense was banged up. The organization made the decision to protect him for the long run. And now that decision is about to pay off.

His practice window runs through November twenty-fifth, the same day the Packers face the Rams on Thanksgiving Eve. That could be a target for his return. But nothing is confirmed. And based on everything he said, he’s not going to rush it either.

So now let’s bring it back to this week. The Minnesota Vikings.

This is a huge one. The Vikings beat the Packers in week one, scoring twenty-nine unanswered points after Green Bay led twenty-two to ten. And right now, the NFC North is the best division in football. The Packers are six and three, but they’re in last place in the division, because everybody else has been winning too. Every division game is basically a playoff game now.

And Minnesota has one of the best defenses in the league. So everything LaFleur talked about matters this week. The offensive line needs stability, and keeping Sean Rhyan off IR is a step toward that. The defense needs to keep taking the ball away, using the pressure and ball skills LaFleur described. Brandon Cisse and the secondary need to keep playing at a Rookie of the Month level. The defensive tackles need to keep doing the dirty work. And Jordan Love needs to keep being the same steady, confident quarterback LaFleur has watched grow for six years.

And without Josh Jacobs, who’s facing surgery after fracturing his finger against New England, the run game will have to find a way with MarShawn Lloyd, Kaleb Johnson, Chris Brooks, and Pierre Strong. That’s the biggest question mark heading into Sunday.

But here’s what gives me confidence. This team just scored forty-five points against the AFC champions. It’s won five of its last six. It’s got an MVP-caliber quarterback, two receivers who just made franchise history, a defense that forces turnovers, and a superstar pass rusher who’s almost ready to come back. And it’s got a head coach who prepares for every detail and a front office that makes patient, long-term decisions.

That’s a team that’s built for November. And as Micah Parsons said, November prepares you for December. And December decides who you become in January.
