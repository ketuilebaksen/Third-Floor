Packers Nation, the short week is not doing this team any favors. Thursday Night Football. The Carolina Panthers coming to Lambeau. And the injury report heading into this one is honestly hard to look at. Two starting offensive linemen out. The defensive captain out with a fracture. One of the best young receivers in football listed as questionable. And because of all that, the Packers are about to make some big lineup decisions, including a guy who’d never snapped a football in an NFL game until last Sunday now being in line to play center.

Welcome back to Third Floor. And today we’ve got a lot to cover. The full injury picture. The offensive line shake-up. A familiar face the Packers have reportedly called about on the trade market. The big names Green Bay chased and missed on. A new defensive star who’s going to need a big payday. And the keys to beating Carolina. Let’s get into it.

Alright, let’s start with the injuries, because this is a lot. Right now, the Green Bay Packers have twelve players on injured reserve. Twelve. And that doesn’t even count the starters who aren’t on IR but still won’t play on Thursday.

Ruled out: linebacker Zaire Franklin, center Sean Rhyan, and right guard Travis Glover.

Listed as questionable: Matthew Golden, Devonte Wyatt, Brenton Cox, and Anthony Campbell.

And the biggest, most concerning name on that list is Matthew Golden. Yes, he leads the league in drops right now, and that’s something he has to clean up. But he’s also been one of the best receivers in the entire NFL this season. He’s barely done anything at practice this week because of shoulder and foot injuries. If he can’t go, that’s a big hole in this passing game. Christian Watson would have to carry even more of the load, and guys like Savion Williams, who LaFleur says has given this team a spark, would have to step up in a big way.

Now let’s talk about Zaire Franklin, because his situation turned out to be worse than we thought. His shin injury, which came from being accidentally kicked in practice, is more than just a bad bruise. It’s a hairline fracture. But here’s the silver lining. The Packers did not move him to injured reserve. And that suggests the hope is that he’ll be back in less than four weeks. He could still miss more games, but this isn’t a season-ender. And that’s important, because Franklin is a defensive captain. With Edgerrin Cooper already done for the year, this linebacker room is thin.

Isaiah McDuffie wore the green dot against Detroit and played really well, according to LaFleur. Ty’Ron Hopper has been maximizing his reps. But the Packers aren’t just sitting back and hoping. They’re reportedly looking outside the building for help.

And the name? Zack Baun from the Philadelphia Eagles.

Reports say the Packers have called the Eagles about Baun, with Philadelphia’s season looking like it’s heading nowhere. And Packers Nation, this one makes a ton of sense. First, Baun is a former Wisconsin Badger. You know we love that. A Wisconsin kid coming home to Green Bay? That’s a great story. But more importantly, he looks like a perfect fit for Jonathan Gannon’s defense.

Interesting note here. Gannon and Baun never actually overlapped in Philadelphia, which is kind of surprising, because Baun is exactly the type of player Gannon loves. A guy who can line up anywhere. He can rush the passer. He can drop into coverage. And he’s great against the run. And he’s still playing at a high level, even with the Eagles struggling.

So this would be a massive addition. The question is what it would cost. Remember, the Packers don’t have their 2027 first-round pick because of the Micah Parsons trade, so every pick matters. Drop a comment and tell me: what would you be willing to give up to get Zack Baun to Green Bay?

Now, the offensive line. And this is where the big decisions come in.

Sean Rhyan, the starting center, was diagnosed with a high ankle sprain. He’s not going on IR, and he’s not expected to need surgery, but he could miss four to five games. Now, I’ll be honest. I don’t think Rhyan has been playing particularly great this season. But he’s a leader of that offensive line group, and he’s started every single game this year. Losing that consistency hurts.

And it gets worse. Against Detroit, backup center Jacob Monk came in for Rhyan and then immediately got hurt himself. Monk has now been placed on injured reserve with a quad injury, so he’ll miss at least four games.

To fill that roster spot, the Packers promoted Mekhi Becton. Six-foot-seven, three hundred sixty pounds. He signed with Green Bay about a month ago and has finally been moved up from the practice squad. LaFleur said this week that Becton has done a nice job learning the offense and that he absolutely believes he can help.

But Becton is a guard, not a center. So who snaps the ball?

That brings us to the decision. John Williams.

Williams is a guard. But against the Lions, when both Rhyan and Monk went down, he got thrown into emergency duty at center. And he did a really admirable job. LaFleur said it was pretty impressive to see him go in there the first time and play the way he did. He said he was really impressed.

And listen to what Williams said about it. He said he’d practiced at center during training camp and OTAs, but never in a game. And when he was asked if he was okay with the move, he said it’s fine by him. He said he just likes to contribute and be part of the team. Whatever they need him to do, that’s what he’s going to do.

That’s the attitude you love to see. No complaints. No excuses. Just, put me in, coach.

Jordan Love talked about Williams too. He said that on a short week, it’s mostly walkthrough reps, not a full live week of practice. But he thought Williams handled the game in Detroit great. He said the snaps were consistent, that they got a lot of reps together during that game, and now it’s about Williams dialing in the plan, understanding the calls, and improving on the things he can clean up from Sunday.

There’s also rookie Jager Burton, who actually played center at Kentucky. He’d normally be in the mix, but he’s dealt with his own injuries this year and has only appeared in two games. He’s not on the injury report this week though, so keep an eye on him.

And with Travis Glover out, the Packers need a new right guard too. It’s going to be either Kevin Zeitler or Mekhi Becton. When LaFleur was asked if Zeitler is the guy, he just said, you shall see. But he also said he feels confident in Zeitler if they need him. A thirty-six-year-old veteran with a ton of experience making his first start of the season next to an emergency center? That’s the definition of a patchwork line. But sometimes these are the moments where guys rise to the challenge.

Now, here’s something that frustrated me a little. Some big help for this offensive line could have come in the form of Quenton Nelson. Reports say the Packers made a real run at the Colts star and were among the finalists. But Nelson was ultimately traded to the Seattle Seahawks. And the Packers also reportedly made calls about DeForest Buckner before he was traded to the San Francisco 49ers.

So I’ve got two thoughts on that. One, two other NFC contenders just got better by adding those players. That’s not great for Green Bay. But two, the Packers are in the mix. They’re making calls. They’re trying to make this team better. That’s encouraging. But being in the mix isn’t good enough. At some point, we need to actually see a deal get done. Whether it’s Zack Baun, or Jonathan Taylor, whose name has also been floated, or someone else, this team needs reinforcements.

And speaking of reinforcements, the biggest one might not come through a trade at all. Micah Parsons.

This week, LaFleur was asked if he expects Parsons to start practicing next week. And he said, I sure hope so. When a reporter pointed out that it was a different answer than usual, LaFleur admitted it was. He said it’s up to the medical staff, but he’s itching for it. And when he was asked, so you’re saying there’s a chance? LaFleur said, I’m saying there’s a chance.

That’s a bigger addition than any trade the Packers could realistically pull off. A healthy Micah Parsons for the stretch run could completely change this defense.

Okay, now let’s get to some good news. Because there is some. The Packers have a real star in Evan Williams.

Pro Football Focus currently grades Evan Williams as the number one safety in the entire NFL this season. Now, you don’t have to treat PFF grades like gospel. But in this case, it backs up exactly what our eyes are telling us. Williams leads the Packers in tackles. He has three games with ten or more tackles this year, including that twelve-tackle performance against the Bears. He’s got two interceptions and six pass breakups. He’s twenty-five years old, and he’s having a breakout season.

And the best part for the Packers? He’s still on his rookie contract through the end of the 2027 season. So right now, he’s a huge bargain. But at some point, he’s going to need to get paid. And paid big. So Brian Gutekunst and the front office have to start planning now, moving some money around to get ready for that extension. Because players like this don’t grow on trees. Evan Williams is a big reason this team has a winning record despite all the adversity it’s been through.

Now let’s talk about the Carolina Panthers.

This is a team that scores a lot of points and gives up a lot of points. They’re near the top of the league in scoring, but they’ve also been giving it up on defense. And they’re coming in off an embarrassing forty-three to thirteen loss to Tampa Bay. LaFleur said this week that he expects Carolina to rebound and come in very motivated. And don’t forget, Carolina came into Lambeau last year and beat the Packers.

But here’s the matchup that’s going to decide this game. Carolina has the worst rushing defense in the entire NFL. And the Packers have the worst rushing offense in the NFL. It’s the classic irresistible force meets the movable object. Whichever team is better at making its weakness not so weak in this game is probably going to win it.

That’s why Josh Jacobs matters so much this week. In his first game back against Detroit, he had eleven carries and admitted he felt some rust. LaFleur loved the physicality and the edge he brought. Now he gets a Carolina defense that’s been getting run over. If Jacobs can get rolling, even behind a patched-together offensive line, it opens everything up.

And there’s more. Carolina’s offensive line is almost as beat up as Green Bay’s. They’re expected to have multiple starters out for this game. So expect Jonathan Gannon to dial up pressures and blitzes and cause some real havoc for Bryce Young. Lukas Van Ness, Brenton Cox if he can go, the whole front has a chance to feast.

And on the other side, the Panthers are thin at cornerback. Which means Jordan Love and this receiving group have a chance to attack a beaten-up secondary.

Jordan talked about it this week. He said Carolina is down a couple of corners, but scheme-wise, it’s very similar to last year. He went back and reviewed last year’s game and said they run a lot of the same coverages and principles. He said they want to keep the top on the defense and limit explosive plays, and that they’ve been pretty good against the pass this year, but the run game has gotten them.

And then he said something really important. Last year against Carolina, the Packers had seven drives without punting once. Seven drives, no punts. And they still lost, because they couldn’t finish in the red zone and they turned the ball over too many times. So the focus this week is clear. Finish in the red zone. Protect the football. Have a better plan. Learn from last year.

Now, on defense, the key is locking up Tetairoa McMillan. He’s a six-foot-five receiver and Bryce Young’s favorite target. And here’s a stat that tells you everything. This season, every time McMillan has gone over eighty receiving yards, the Panthers have won. Every time he’s been under eighty, they’ve lost. So if the Packers can keep McMillan under control, they’re in great shape.

And this should ring a bell for Packers fans. The last time Green Bay faced a big receiver like this was Drake London, and he went off for nine catches and one hundred ninety-four yards. That was also on a Thursday night. That cannot happen again.

One of the Packers’ defensive backs talked about the mindset this week. He said he always assumes he’s getting tested. He said even elite corners will tell you that when you get lazy in this league and you don’t think the ball is coming your way, that’s exactly when it comes at you, and that’s when you end up looking bad. So he tries to treat every single play like it has its own mind and be ready to go.

That’s the right approach against a receiver like McMillan.

And Jordan Love summed up the bigger picture. He said the team has to be better than it was the last time it played a Thursday night game at home. And he said he knows they will be. He admitted the short week is always tough. Everything gets jam-packed, and it’s hard to get your body back, especially with so many guys injured. But he made it clear, they’re never going to make excuses.

No excuses. I love that.

So let’s put it all together. Here’s what we know heading into Packers versus Panthers.

Sean Rhyan, Travis Glover, and Zaire Franklin are out. Franklin has a hairline fracture, but stayed off IR, so hopefully he’s back in less than four weeks.

Matthew Golden, Devonte Wyatt, Brenton Cox, and Anthony Campbell are questionable. Golden is the big one.

John Williams is likely to be the center after stepping in with an admirable performance in Detroit. Kevin Zeitler or Mekhi Becton will likely play right guard.

The Packers have reportedly called about Zack Baun, after missing out on Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner.

Micah Parsons might start practicing next week.

Evan Williams is playing like the best safety in football.

And the game comes down to this: worst run defense against worst run offense. Patch-work lines on both sides. Lock up McMillan. Finish in the red zone. Protect the ball.

Packers Nation, this is a must-win. After the loss in Detroit, this team cannot drop two in a row at Lambeau, on national TV, against a team that beat them there last year. The injuries are real. The short week is real. But this team has shown it can fight through adversity. And I believe they’ll do it again on Thursday night.
