Packers Nation, the trade deadline is coming in hot, and Green Bay’s name keeps popping up in some very interesting places. Daron Payne from Washington. Vita Vea from Tampa Bay. Jonathan Taylor from Indianapolis. And one source told a top NFL insider that chaos is about to break loose across the league over the next week and a half.

But before we get to the trade market, we’ve got to talk about what happened on Thursday night against Carolina. Because the Packers won, they moved to five and three, Christian Watson did something absolutely insane, and Matt LaFleur made a lineup decision that left a lot of fans scratching their heads. Me included.

Welcome back to Third Floor. And let me be upfront like always. The Packers have not traded for Daron Payne yet. What we have are multiple reports that Green Bay has been calling about him, and that talks have heated up. So today we’re going through all of it. The Kevin Zeitler mystery. The run game problem. Every trade target connected to this team. And why Christian Watson might be the toughest man in football right now. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the decision that had everybody talking. Kevin Zeitler.

Zeitler has been in Green Bay for more than a month now. This is a guy who spent most of a decade and a half as one of the best run-blocking guards in the entire league. And on Thursday night, with starting right guard Travis Glover inactive, and with a run game that’s been among the worst in football, Zeitler still didn’t play a single snap.

Here’s how it played out. Glover was inactive with his knee injury. Aaron Banks came off the inactive list. So most people, myself included, assumed this was finally Zeitler’s moment. Banks goes back to left guard, Zeitler slides in at right guard, and we finally get to see what the veteran can do.

That’s not what happened at all.

Instead, Laken Tomlinson, who had been playing left guard since week four, moved over to right guard. Banks took left guard. John Williams, who stepped in at center in Detroit after both Sean Rhyan and Jacob Monk went down, stayed at center. And the tackles stayed the same as they’ve been since week two.

After the game, LaFleur was asked about it. And he said the way Tomlinson has been playing, the team just couldn’t take him out of the lineup. With Banks coming back, they went that route. And he made a point to say it wasn’t meant to take anything away from Zeitler, and that they feel good about him too.

I respect that answer. Tomlinson has played well. And Banks is a career left guard going back to his natural spot. That makes sense. But here’s the interesting part. Tomlinson has also spent most of his career at left guard. And LaFleur admitted that Tomlinson had very few practice reps at right guard before Thursday. So the staff chose to move a veteran to an unfamiliar side rather than plug in a veteran who has played right guard his entire career.

That tells you one of two things. Either they trust Tomlinson that much right now, or they don’t trust Zeitler’s readiness.

And here’s my issue. The run game has been nearly nonexistent all season. Against Carolina, a team with one of the worst run defenses in the league, Josh Jacobs had thirty-nine rushing yards on twenty-one carries. Under two yards a carry. Against the worst run defense in football. And Zeitler’s greatest strength his entire career has been run blocking. If there was ever a time to find out if he could help open things up, wouldn’t it be right now?

The more I think about it, the more I see three possible explanations.

Explanation number one is the simplest. The coaching staff believes Tomlinson is just the better player right now. That’s possible. Coaches see every practice rep, every meeting, every walkthrough. We don’t.

Explanation number two is harder to say, but it might be true. Zeitler might not be the player he once was. That’s not a knock on him. He’d basically stepped away from the game before the Packers signed him. He’s been in the league about fifteen years. He’s thirty-six. He didn’t have an offseason or a training camp. Maybe he just isn’t as sharp or as ready as the team hoped.

And explanation number three is the one I’m holding out hope for. Maybe the Packers are saving him. Maybe they believe this current line can get them into the playoffs, and they want to keep a veteran like Zeitler fresh for the final stretch and the postseason, when every snap matters most. LaFleur did say earlier this month that when Zeitler is ready to roll, he’ll be out there. For a player close to retirement, managing his workload over a full season might actually make sense.

I honestly don’t know which one it is. But I do know the run game needs something to change.

And remember how good Zeitler was at his peak. A first-round pick back in 2012. Years as one of the most reliable right guards in football, with stops in places like Cincinnati, Cleveland, Baltimore, and Detroit. His calling card was always the run game. He moves people at the point of attack. He finishes blocks. For a team that can’t get the ground game going, that’s exactly the skill set you’d want to at least test. Even a series or two would tell the coaching staff something.

Now let’s talk about something that has no question marks at all. Christian Watson.

Packers Nation, what this man did on Thursday night is the kind of thing you tell your grandkids about. Watson broke a rib. He was coughing up blood. He rode to the hospital in an ambulance. And then he came back and finished the game with more than one hundred receiving yards.

Read that again. Broken rib. Coughing up blood. Ambulance. Came back. Over a hundred yards.

Some rival fans online tried to joke that he just got the wind knocked out of him. No. He cracked a rib and kept playing. That’s toughness at a level you rarely see in any sport.

And he’s already set a career high in receiving yards, and we’re only eight games into the season. Remember when he signed his contract, and a lot of people criticized it? Said he hadn’t earned it? Said he couldn’t stay healthy? That deal looks better every single week. He’s not just the top receiver and the deep threat. He’s also been more consistent at the catch point than anybody else in that room. And his connection with Jordan Love has become one of the best things about this offense.

Here’s why that matters so much. When the run game can’t get going, Jordan Love needs someone he can trust on the outside. Someone who can win deep and also make tough catches in traffic. Watson has become that guy. And if the Packers can just get the run game to a respectable level, the Love and Watson connection becomes even more dangerous, because defenses won’t be able to sit back and take away the deep ball anymore.

Alright, now the trade deadline. And it’s about to get wild.

NFL insider Jordan Schultz said this week that after the Chicago Bears made a blockbuster trade for Jeffery Simmons, the deadline is expected to be even crazier than anyone thought. One source told him chaos is about to break loose over the next week and a half.

And think about that for a second. The Bears just got a lot better on the defensive line. The Seahawks got Quenton Nelson. The 49ers got DeForest Buckner. The NFC contenders are loading up. The Packers can’t afford to stand still.

Now, historically, Brian Gutekunst hasn’t been the most aggressive deadline general manager. But to be fair, this front office has shown it can make huge moves. Trading for Micah Parsons was one of the boldest decisions any team has made in years. The question is whether that same aggressiveness shows up in November.

So who are the Packers calling? According to the reports, mostly defensive tackles. Which is a little surprising given the state of the offense. But let’s go through them.

The first name, and the one in our title, is Daron Payne from the Washington Commanders.

A reporter who covers Washington said trade talks around Payne have heated up, with the Bengals, Packers, and Giants all involved. And that came after a Packers reporter said Green Bay had already called about Payne roughly a week earlier. So we have two separate reports pointing in the same direction. That makes this one feel real.

So what would Payne bring? He’s twenty-nine and in the final year of his contract. His best season came back in 2022, when he had eleven and a half sacks. He’s played well this year, but let’s be honest, he’s probably not that version of himself anymore. He’s a solid, reliable player. Not a game wrecker. But a solid interior lineman could help this defense a lot.

Why? Because the Packers lost Javon Hargrave for the season. That was a veteran they thought they could rely on all year. And with Warren Brinson also dealing with a long-term injury, the interior depth is thin. Anthony Campbell has stepped up nicely, but you need bodies in there. Payne could help against the run, add some interior pressure, and give Jonathan Gannon another piece to work with.

And the good news is that the market suggests Payne won’t be expensive. DeForest Buckner just went to San Francisco for a fourth-round pick. So Payne’s value is probably somewhere around a late third or an early fourth. For a playoff team, that’s a reasonable price.

The bigger obstacle might be the competition. If the Bengals and Giants are also chasing him, the Packers might have to outbid two motivated teams.

The second name excites me a lot more. Vita Vea.

Reports say the Packers and Bengals have both called Tampa Bay about Vea. He’s one of the highest-paid defensive tackles in the league, and for good reason. When he’s on, he’s a nightmare in the middle. A massive presence who collapses the pocket and eats up double teams.

There’s a catch, though. Vea signed a one-year extension, which means he’s set to be a free agent again after the season. So this would likely be a rental. Whether it makes sense depends entirely on the price.

But imagine what Vea could do for this defense. Gannon’s unit is already getting pressure off the edge. Lukas Van Ness has been playing the best football of his career. Add a massive interior presence like Vea, and offenses can’t double team everybody. Quarterbacks can’t step up into the pocket. Running backs can’t find easy lanes up the middle.

And now add Micah Parsons. Because Parsons is expected to return to practice next week. LaFleur said he sure hopes so, and that there’s a chance. Picture Parsons on the edge and Vita Vea in the middle. That combination would be terrifying for any offensive line in the league. That’s the dream scenario. It just has to come at a price that doesn’t hurt the future.

And the Packers do have some trade ammunition. They’re projected to receive several mid-round compensatory picks for losing players like Romeo Doubs, Malik Willis, and Quay Walker. They also picked up an extra fourth-round pick when they traded Rashan Gary to Dallas. That’s enough to make a meaningful offer for a veteran on an expiring deal without touching the top of the draft.

But here’s where my priorities have shifted. After eight weeks, and especially after Thursday night, fixing the run game is priority number one. If Gutekunst can add a defensive tackle and fix the run game, great. But the run game comes first.

So what would that look like? It could mean adding an interior offensive lineman. A player like Jackson Powers-Johnson from the Raiders has been mentioned as a trade candidate, and he probably wouldn’t cost much. It could mean adding a running back who can create yards on his own, like Tony Pollard, who’s on an expiring deal in Tennessee. Or it could simply mean finally giving Kevin Zeitler a real shot. None of those options are flashy. But any of them could help more than another big name on defense.

And then there’s the biggest name of all. Jonathan Taylor.

The Packers have been linked to him multiple times, and that’s still the case. Several teams have reportedly called Indianapolis about Taylor, including the Packers, Giants, Bengals, Vikings, and Broncos. And most of them were quickly told the price was a first-round pick and more. Cincinnati seems to be the most interested.

And notice the pattern. The Bengals keep showing up in these reports. They’ve been linked to Payne. To Vea. To Taylor. Cincinnati is clearly determined to make a big move. That makes them direct competition for the Packers on several fronts. If both teams are chasing the same handful of players, prices go up. That’s one more reason Gutekunst needs to decide quickly what he wants and act before somebody else does.

But honestly, I don’t think trading for Taylor makes sense for Green Bay, especially at that price. Adding a running back wouldn’t be a bad idea. But Taylor can’t fix this run game by himself. The problem is as much about the offensive line as it is about the backs. And the Packers don’t have a first-round pick this year. They gave it up, along with another first, to get Micah Parsons. So a Taylor trade would mean digging into future assets the team really can’t afford to give away.

It’s fun to imagine. I’ll admit it. But this isn’t fantasy football. Draft picks matter, especially for a team that’s already traded away two firsts.

And here’s the other thing. Even if Taylor came to Green Bay, he’d be running behind the same line that’s struggled to create space for Jacobs. Josh Jacobs has been one of the most productive running backs in the league over the last few years. He isn’t suddenly a bad player. When a back of his caliber is averaging under two yards per carry, the problem isn’t just the back. Adding Taylor without fixing the blocking might just give you a more expensive version of the same problem.

And honestly, the trade I keep thinking about is the one that already happened. Quenton Nelson went to Seattle for a third-round pick and a fifth-round pick. A third and a fifth, for one of the best guards in football. Reports said the Packers were among the finalists. After watching the run game on Thursday, I just don’t understand why Green Bay didn’t get that deal done. I get the counterargument. Nelson’s contract expires after the season, and the Packers would have had to figure out how to pay him after that. But when your run game is this broken? That feels like a move they should have made. And I think we’re going to keep feeling that way all season.

So here’s how I see the next eleven days.

The Packers are five and three. Micah Parsons is expected to return to practice next week. The defense is playing at a high level. Jordan Love is carrying the offense. Christian Watson is playing the best football of his career, broken rib and all. And the weakness is obvious to everyone. The run game.

The Packers have a real choice to make. They can wait and hope that changes on the offensive line, maybe including Zeitler, eventually fix it. Or they can go out and find help before the deadline.

My vote? Be aggressive, but be smart. Don’t overpay for Taylor. Do go after a defensive tackle like Daron Payne or Vita Vea if the price is right. And most importantly, find a way to make this offense two-dimensional again. Because if this team can run the ball even a little bit, with Love throwing to Watson, with Parsons coming back on defense, with a solid interior lineman added up front? This team can absolutely be a contender in the NFC.
