Packers Nation, the clock is ticking. The NFL trade deadline is Tuesday, November tenth. Just days away. And the Green Bay Packers are running out of time to fix the biggest problem on their roster. The offensive line.

And right now, there’s a decision sitting on the table that could change the entire second half of this season. A three-time Pro Bowl guard. Available from a team that’s selling. At a price that might be as low as a fifth-round pick. His name is Landon Dickerson. And the question for Matt LaFleur, Brian Gutekunst, and this whole organization is simple. Are they going to pull the trigger?

The contract structure that makes this deal almost too good to ignore. The one complication nobody’s talking about. And what it all means with Micah Parsons on his way back. Let’s go.

Let’s start with the problem. Because to understand why this decision matters so much, you have to understand what this offensive line has been through.

It has been a messy year up front. And that might be putting it nicely. The starting right tackle went down with a patellar tendon tear in week two and never came back. Left guard Aaron Banks, who the team handed a significant contract two offseasons ago, has been unavailable for stretches. Center Jacob Monk eventually landed on injured reserve. Starting center Sean Rhyan has missed time with a high ankle sprain. Right guard Travis Glover went down with a knee injury. And John Williams, a guard, has been snapping the football at center.

The Packers came into the 2026 season with real playoff aspirations. And their offensive line has been a constant source of pain for Jordan Love and the entire offense.

And look at how Green Bay has responded. They signed three veteran guards just to keep things together week to week. Laken Tomlinson came off the Cowboys’ practice squad. Kevin Zeitler signed a one-year deal. And Mekhi Becton was brought in too. That’s not the profile of a team that had its offensive line figured out. That’s a team scrambling.

And the results show it. Only one Packers offensive lineman has a run-blocking grade above sixty this season. A couple are in the thirties. The Packers have the worst rushing attack in the NFL, with just four hundred eighty-five rushing yards through eight games. And Jordan Love has been carrying this offense almost by himself.

So with the deadline approaching, Green Bay has an opportunity it probably didn’t expect to have. And it comes from Philadelphia.

According to ESPN insider Jeremy Fowler, something feels off between the Eagles and Landon Dickerson. The Eagles are dealing with their own turbulent season, and they’re in a spot where they could get some draft capital back by moving a veteran who isn’t in their long-term plans.

Fowler noted that Dickerson recently appeared to take a passive-aggressive shot at the Eagles’ front office about his long-term outlook with the franchise. That’s a sign the relationship may have soured. Philadelphia is reportedly going younger along the offensive line, and Dickerson, one of the holdovers from the Jeff Stoutland era, could be one of the players moved before the deadline.

ESPN currently gives a Dickerson trade about a twenty-five percent chance of happening. And the projected best fits, according to ESPN? The Los Angeles Chargers and the Green Bay Packers. Bleacher Report’s Christopher Knox also named Green Bay as a potential suitor in his week nine trade big board.

Now here’s why this matters so much.

Landon Dickerson is twenty-eight years old. He was a second-round pick by Philadelphia in 2021. And in the five-plus seasons since, he’s earned three straight Pro Bowl selections, from 2022 through 2024. He was a cornerstone of the Eagles’ offensive line that helped Philadelphia win the Super Bowl two years ago. At his best, he’s exactly the kind of player teams pay big money for in free agency and almost never get in a trade.

And ESPN’s Matt Bowen has said that when Dickerson is healthy, he can move defenders off the ball in both zone and gap schemes, set an anchor in pass protection, and handle power down the middle with his six-foot-six, three hundred thirty-two pound frame.

Now, let’s be honest. The 2026 season hasn’t been his best. He opened the year as a starter, got hurt in week one, spent time on injured reserve, and only came back for the Eagles’ game against Dallas. And in a small sample of snaps since his return, his pass-blocking grades have been below his usual standard. Some of that is rust. Some of it might reflect the tension inside that organization.

So the point is this. You’re not trading for peak Landon Dickerson. You’re trading for Landon Dickerson at a discount, with plenty of reason to believe the talent is still there. And the financial structure makes it almost too easy to understand why the Packers would be interested.

Let me walk you through the contract, because this is the part that makes this deal so attractive.

Because of how the Eagles structured his deal, most of Dickerson’s money already came through as an option bonus that Philadelphia has already paid. His 2026 base salary, which would follow him to whatever team trades for him, is just the veteran minimum.

So if the Packers acquired him right now, they’d absorb roughly seven hundred forty-two thousand dollars against their salary cap for the rest of this season.

Seven hundred forty-two thousand dollars. For a three-time Pro Bowler. That’s an extraordinary number.

And then it gets even better. From there, Green Bay would hold two team options going forward. A twenty-two million dollar figure in 2027, and a twenty-one million dollar figure in 2028, with option bonuses the team would have to exercise before week one of each season. And neither option is guaranteed unless the Packers choose to use it.

So if Dickerson plays great the rest of this year, Green Bay can keep him as a long-term answer. And if he got hurt, the Packers would only owe about two point one million for 2027, with nothing fully guaranteed beyond that. That’s minimal risk with significant upside.

And the trade cost? Based on multiple analyses, it’s likely in the fifth-round range. Some projections have it as a fifth and a seventh. Others have a fifth and a roster player. Knox’s proposal at Bleacher Report was built around a conditional second-round pick, so there’s a range. But either way, this is not a blockbuster package. This is a pretty modest investment for a three-time Pro Bowler under team control through 2028.

So why wouldn’t you do it? Well, this is where the decision gets a little more complicated than a simple yes or no.

First, the roster fit. Green Bay brought in Tomlinson, Zeitler, and Becton to fix the guard problem. All three are on one-year deals. Aaron Banks is under contract for two more years, but there’s a strong expectation around the league that the Packers will move on from him in the offseason because of the cap implications. So after 2026, the Packers have basically no long-term answers at guard. And Dickerson, with that contract structure, could be that answer. That’s actually a big argument in favor.

But here’s the complication worth acknowledging. The position Green Bay needs most urgently right now might actually be center, not guard. With Monk on IR and Rhyan also missing time, the middle of this line has been a revolving door. And Dickerson has played almost all of his NFL snaps at left guard, with very little experience at center. So he’s not a plug-and-play fix for the most pressing need. He’s more of a long-term solution at a position the team is also going to have to address.

Still, think about it this way. Right now, Tomlinson is playing right guard after spending most of his career on the left side, with barely any practice reps over there. Banks has been in and out. Adding a player like Dickerson at guard would let everybody else settle into their best spots. It would stabilize the whole interior. And a strong guard makes the center next to him better too, by helping on double teams and picking up stunts.

And then there’s the general manager. Brian Gutekunst is known for being careful with draft capital. And the Packers don’t have a 2027 first-round pick after the Micah Parsons trade, which limits their flexibility in a bigger deal. But a fifth-round pick for a player with Dickerson’s résumé? That fits perfectly with the idea of adding talent without breaking the bank.

And there’s a bigger reason to act. The window with Jordan Love is open right now. Love has been one of the best quarterbacks in the NFC over the past two seasons. This year, he’s thrown at least two touchdown passes in seven of eight games, with almost no help from the run game. But he needs protection to keep operating at that level. Every time Love gets pressured or hit unnecessarily, it doesn’t just cost a play. It reminds everybody how fragile this whole run can be.

And now look at the other side of the ball. Micah Parsons is back at practice. One report has floated a possible return as soon as this week against the New England Patriots, while other reporting has pointed toward the Rams game on Thanksgiving Eve. Parsons himself hasn’t set a date. But either way, he’s coming. And the defense is about to get dramatically better.

So put those two things together. If the defense gets Micah Parsons back, and the offense can stabilize in front of Jordan Love, the Packers become a genuinely dangerous team heading into the second half and the postseason.

That’s what this decision is really about.

The question is whether Gutekunst will pull the trigger. His track record suggests caution. And we’ve already seen what caution costs. The Packers showed interest in Quenton Nelson, and they were reportedly among the finalists. But they didn’t move in time, and Nelson ended up in Seattle for a third and a fifth. If that experience taught Gutekunst anything, it should be this. When a good offensive lineman is available, waiting can mean watching him go somewhere else.

And the Chargers are out there too, according to ESPN. And other teams are combing the guard market. Every day Green Bay waits, the chance of losing him goes up.

So here’s where I stand.

Landon Dickerson might not be the same player he was in 2024. He might not solve every problem the Packers have up front. He’s not a natural center. And his health has to be checked carefully.

But he’s twenty-eight. He has three Pro Bowls on his résumé. He was a starter on a Super Bowl champion line. His contract costs about seven hundred forty-two thousand dollars this season, with team options that give Green Bay all the control. And the trade cost is likely around a fifth-round pick.

For a team that has spent this entire season patching its offensive line with one-year veterans and practice squad signings, the argument for making this move is just too strong to ignore.
