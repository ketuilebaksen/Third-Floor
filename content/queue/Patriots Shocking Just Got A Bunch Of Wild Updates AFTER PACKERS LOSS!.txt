Let’s remember who Jonathan Taylor is. He’s 27 years old. A three-time Pro Bowler. More than 8,000 career rushing yards and 76 rushing touchdowns. Last season, he ran for 1,585 yards and led the NFL with 18 rushing touchdowns. This year, through six games, he had 122 carries for 492 yards and seven touchdowns.

And the Colts are open to moving him. They fired their general manager, Chris Ballard, and they’re struggling. But Bleacher Report reported that the Colts turned down an offer of a 2027 second-round pick plus another pick. So the price is high. A second-round pick and more, at minimum.

So here’s the real question. Is it worth it?

And I’ll be honest with you. I have real doubts.

Yes, there are questions in the backfield right now. Mike Vrabel joked that the MRI machine has basically been working overtime this week. That’s not ideal. There are questions about Rhamondre Stevenson’s health. There are questions about TreVeyon Henderson, who’s recovering from a frightening neurological episode that put him in the hospital for several days. And his health comes first, way before football.

The Patriots still have Cory Ker. They have Lan Larison. And there’s also the reported interest in Derrick Henry, who would cost a lot less, probably around a third-round pick, according to AtoZ Sports.

But is it worth dropping all that draft capital for Taylor?

My honest answer is no. I don’t think it’s the immediate help this team needs most.

Here’s why. What the Patriots really need on offense is to protect Drake Maye. And right now, the guys playing guard in front of him are, in all reality, depth pieces. Practice squad guys. Players who’ve been pressed into starting roles because of injuries.

That interior offensive line needs help. That’s the glaring issue. Not the running back.

Think about it. Mike Onwenu is out for the season. Alijah Vera-Tucker is on injured reserve. Ben Brown has gone down too. Walter Rouse and Matt Prior have been forced to start. The Patriots used 14 different offensive line combinations in their first six games.

You can put the best running back in the world back there. If there’s no hole to run through, it doesn’t matter. Taylor can’t make a guard block better just by showing up.

And the money matters too. Taylor’s cap hits jump to 21 million dollars in 2027 and 23 million in 2028. Paying a running back that kind of money, while Drake Maye is still on his rookie contract, is a huge commitment.

So sure, Taylor would add another weapon. But I don’t think he fixes the real weakness. And I don’t think it’s worth the trade capital.

Now, let’s talk about the moves the Patriots just made. Because they tell us a lot about what’s coming.

The Patriots released rookie tight end Tanner Arkin, out of Illinois. He was brought in as a blocking tight end, a big body for those heavier personnel groups when the Patriots want extra size on the line.

And they released Jose Ramirez, the edge rusher who had just been brought back to the practice squad a couple of weeks ago.

So what’s going on?

Here’s my read. And I want to be clear, this is my read, not a report. I think they’re making room for Greg Van Roten. Van Roten returned to practice from injured reserve last week, and the Patriots need to activate him so he can travel to Germany for the Lions game. Because they desperately need help on the interior of the offensive line.

And I think Arkin will most likely clear waivers and come back on the practice squad. Then Van Roten gets activated to the 53-man roster.

That looks like the obvious next step to me. We’ll see exactly what they do. But the need is clear.

Now, we have to talk about the elephant in the room. Christian Gonzalez.

Gonzalez had a horrendous game against the Packers. He allowed 10 receptions for 187 yards. That’s the third-most yards allowed by any player in a single game this season.

And this is one of the highest-paid cornerbacks in the league. And it’s a shock, because through his first six games, Gonzalez had allowed just 196 total yards. In six games. And then 187 in one afternoon.

And honestly, even during the Bears game, there were moments where I was watching him and thinking, he doesn’t look quite right. It seemed like he was giving a big cushion. He wasn’t making the plays we know he can make.

But I want to be on the record about this. I’m not going to throw Christian Gonzalez under the bus.

I’ve seen a lot of people online saying they’re done with him. Get him off the team. We should never have paid him.

No. Stop.

Gonzalez has proven he’s one of the best cornerbacks in the league. Yes, he just had one of the worst games of his career. But great players have bad days. Tom Brady had games where he threw four interceptions and looked awful. He’s still the greatest of all time.

And remember, Gonzalez missed time earlier this season with a shoulder injury. Sometimes, coming back from injury, it takes time to get fully back to yourself.

This needs to be a turning point for him. He needs to wake up. Turn it around. And get ready for the next game in Germany.
